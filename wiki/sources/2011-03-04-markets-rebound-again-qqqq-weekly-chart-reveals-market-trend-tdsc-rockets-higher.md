# WW 2011-03-04 — Weekly trends as the anti-shakeout discipline

**Source:** [Markets rebound–again; QQQQ weekly chart reveals market trend; TDSC rockets higher](../../raw/posts/2011-03-04-markets-rebound-again-qqqq-weekly-chart-reveals-market-trend-tdsc-rockets-higher.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2011/03/markets-rebound-again-qqqq-weekly-chart-reveals-market-trend-tdsc-rockets-higher/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

**The anti-shakeout discipline, stated as a recurring personal failure**: "**I have to relearn the lesson over
and over again, to focus on weekly trends, so as not to be shaken out of the market by daily fluctuations.**"
The weekly chart he is looking at shows the pattern he wants — "**the key bullish pattern of 4wk>10wk>30wk
average** is evident and the index has continually found support at its 10 week average" — with the exit named as
a price: "**a key sell signal for me will be a *close* below the 10 week average, currently at 56.83.**" Stage 2
is confirmed by the index sitting well above a rising 30-week. For beginners he gives the whole setup free:
"you can use the free chart program at **freestockcharts.com**. Just **add the moving averages, 4, 10, 30 as
indicators and select the week as your time period**" — three averages and a timeframe, which is the entire
weekly framework reduced to a configuration anyone can reproduce. Judy appears again with TDSC, up from ~\$31 in
December to \$53.29 and an all-time high: "my stock-buddy, Judy, **researches 'concept' stocks, and finds more
future winners than any other person I have ever met**."

## Key claims

- **"Focus on weekly trends, so as not to be shaken out by daily fluctuations"** — and he says he has to relearn it repeatedly.
- **The whole weekly framework is three averages and a timeframe**: 4, 10, 30 weekly — reproducible free by anyone.
- The sell signal is a *close* below the 10-week, quoted as a number in advance.
- 4wk > 10wk > 30wk named as "the key bullish pattern," with repeated support at the 10-week as the confirming behaviour.
- Judy characterised as a researcher of "concept" stocks — a fundamental/story screen running alongside his technical one.
