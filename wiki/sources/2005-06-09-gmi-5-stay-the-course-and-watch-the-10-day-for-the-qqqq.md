# WW 2005-06-09 — The 10-day average as a yellow light; put insurance on the volatile names

**Source:** [GMI: +5;   Stay the course and watch the 10-day for the QQQQ](../../raw/posts/2005-06-09-gmi-5-stay-the-course-and-watch-the-10-day-for-the-qqqq.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2005/06/gmi-5-stay-the-course-and-watch-the-10-day-for-the-qqqq/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

The 10-day moving average given its job, which is not the job of a sell rule. The QQQQ "has completed its
**3rd day below its 10 day moving average — an ominous sign**," and the general principle behind that reading:
"**Sharply rising stocks, including the QQQQ index, tend to remain above their rising 10 day average. A close
below the average may not be a sell signal, but it serves as a yellow light to me.**" The operative consequence
is a gate on *new* commitments rather than an exit: "**I would not take on new positions until the QQQQ closes
above its 10 day average**, now at 38.12." Alongside it, protective puts in practice — he is "glad that I am
holding 'put insurance'… on **my most volatile positions** (CME and GOOG)," which is the selection rule for
where insurance goes. He closes on why the analysis contains no macro: "I am sometimes struck by how focused my
comments are exclusively on price and volume trends. I think discussions about the Fed, world events, and other
economic factors merely obfuscate the true nature of the market" — the truck metaphor again, react first.

## Key claims

- **The 10-day average is a yellow light, not a sell signal** — and its natural use is to gate new buying while price is below it.
- A rising 10-day average is the signature of a *sharply* rising instrument; losing it is the first thing to go.
- Three consecutive days below is what he calls ominous — the count matters, not the single close.
- **Put insurance is allocated by volatility**, bought on the most volatile holdings rather than across the book.
- The exclusion of macro is deliberate and stated: price and volume, because everything else "obfuscates the true nature of the market."
