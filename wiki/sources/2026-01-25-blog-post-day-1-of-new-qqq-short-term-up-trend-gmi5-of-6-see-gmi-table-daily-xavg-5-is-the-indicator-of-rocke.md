# WW 2026-01-25 — The 5-day EMA as a rocket's tell after a GLB

**Source:** [Blog Post: Day 1 of new $QQQ short term up-trend; GMI=5 (of 6), see GMI Table; Daily XAVG 5 is the indicator of rockets, see $SMCI in 2024  and $SLV in 2025 as examples.](../../raw/posts/2026-01-25-blog-post-day-1-of-new-qqq-short-term-up-trend-gmi5-of-6-see-gmi-table-daily-xavg-5-is-the-indicator-of-rocke.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2026/01/blog-post-day-1-of-new-qqq-short-term-up-trend-gmi5-of-6-see-gmi-table-daily-xavg-5-is-the-indicator-of-rockets-see-smci-in-2024-and-slv-in-2025-as-examples/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

**The post-GLB ride, with an indicator attached.** "**I have found that the 5 day exponential moving average,
XAVG 5, is an excellent indicator of a climbing rocket. I scan for stocks with a recent GLB (green line break-out
at an ATH) that are riding their XAVG 5 (purple dotted line) higher.**" So the fastest average on his charts gets
a specific job: not an entry trigger but a **hold** signal — a stock that stays above a five-day exponential
average after a breakout is one whose advance has not paused, and losing it is the first thing that happens when
it does. That is the same role the [10-day yellow light](../methodology/moving-average-rules.md) plays for an
index, one step faster and applied to a stock in a confirmed move. He leaves the mechanism as an open question
for readers, which is characteristic: "**Question to ponder: Why do some stocks track a moving ave[rage]?**"
Worked on SMCI and SLV — including a commodity ETF, so the observation is about behaviour rather than about
equities.

## Key claims

- **The 5-day EMA (XAVG 5) as the ride-along indicator for a stock in a post-GLB advance** — a hold signal rather than an entry trigger.
- Scanned for directly: recent GLB **plus** riding the 5-day EMA higher.
- The fastest average is the first thing a stalling advance loses, which is what makes it an early tell.
- He poses the mechanism as an open question — "why do some stocks track a moving average?" — rather than asserting one.
- Demonstrated on SMCI and SLV, so it is a claim about price behaviour rather than about equities specifically.
