# WW 2010-12-21 — Buying the bounce off the 30-day instead of the handle breakout

**Source:** [21st day of QQQQ short term up-trend; ARMH cup-with-handle?](../../raw/posts/2010-12-21-21st-day-of-qqq-short-term-up-trend-armh-cup-with-handle.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2010/12/21st-day-of-qqq-short-term-up-trend-armh-cup-with-handle/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

**Two entries on the same chart, and he prefers the quieter one.** ARMH forming a cup-with-handle: "**the
traditional IBD approach would be to buy it upon a high volume break out a little above the high of the handle,
at about 19.06. However, another way I like to buy is after the stock bounces off its rising 30 day average**
(red line). **After the purchase, I would place my stop order to sell below Monday's low, at about \$18.19.**"
The arithmetic is the argument: entering near \$18.30 on the bounce with a stop at \$18.19 risks a fraction of
what entering at \$19.06 with the same invalidation would. "**Buying off of the bounce of a proper moving average
is a**" lower-risk entry than the breakout — the same preference he later formalises as
[BOS](../methodology/entry-signals.md) and as the reason he favours bounces over breakouts generally. What makes
it work is that the moving average supplies a *nearby* place to be wrong; a breakout entry has to reach back to
the base for one.

## Key claims

- **The bounce off a rising 30-day average is preferred to the handle breakout** — same stock, same pattern, different entry.
- **The argument is stop distance**: the average provides a nearby invalidation (\$18.19) that a breakout entry (\$19.06) cannot.
- The stop is placed below the *bounce day's low*, not below the average itself.
- IBD's canonical entry is stated fairly and then declined — he is choosing between two valid rules, not correcting one.
- This is the 2010 ancestor of the BOS setup named in 2016.
