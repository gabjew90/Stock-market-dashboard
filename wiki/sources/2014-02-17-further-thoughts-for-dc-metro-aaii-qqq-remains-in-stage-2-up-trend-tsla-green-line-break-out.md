# WW 2014-02-17 — ETFs to lower stress; I-bonds; weekly calls on GLD

**Source:** [Further Thoughts for DC Metro AAII; $QQQ remains in Stage 2 up-trend; $TSLA– green line break-out](../../raw/posts/2014-02-17-further-thoughts-for-dc-metro-aaii-qqq-remains-in-stage-2-up-trend-tsla-green-line-break-out.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2014/02/further-thoughts-for-dc-metro-aaii-qqq-remains-in-stage-2-up-trend-tsla-green-line-break-out/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

Notes after a DC Metro AAII workshop for "an audience of primarily Boomers," and the advice is audience-fitted
in a way worth recording: "**I stressed the desirability of focusing more on ETF's than individual stocks in
order to lessen volatility and stress levels.**" The afterthought he wished he had included is a cash-management
recommendation that appears nowhere else: **US Government I-bonds** for "ultra safe money" — savings bonds that
"adjust every 6 months to the level of inflation. Thus they are very safe and their yield will rise if interest
rates do. **They will not fall in value like regular bonds can when rates rise.** I believe they are better than
CD's or savings accounts" (yielding 1.38% at the time). On GLD, the covered-call overlay quantified: "I have
been buying GLD and **writing weekly calls** on the position. **The return is about 3% *per month* on my
investment**," with the position justified by GLD closing back above its 30-week average for the first time
since January 2013 and a double bottom near 114.50. Sentiment read the usual way round: "the market climbs a
'wall of worry' so bring on the whiners. **I will become concerned when the pundits start saying that the market
has nowhere to go but up.**" TSLA had a GLB the previous week.

## Key claims

- **ETFs over individual stocks as a stress-reduction recommendation** — the volatility argument, aimed at retirees rather than at traders.
- **I-bonds as the ultra-safe cash sleeve**, chosen specifically because they cannot lose value when rates rise, unlike the bonds his 2014 rate thesis warns about.
- **Weekly covered calls returning ~3% per month** on a GLD position — the 2012 "teaching myself" note has become a running practice with a number attached.
- A position is opened on the *stage change* (a close back above a flattening 30-week average) rather than on the bottom.
- The contrarian tell stated as its own inverse: complaining crowds are fine, unanimity is the warning.
