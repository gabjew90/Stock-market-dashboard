# WW 2005-04-26 — About the General Market Index (GMI)

**Source:** [About the General Market Index (GMI)](../../raw/posts/2005-04-26-general-market-index-gmi.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2005/04/general-market-index-gmi/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

The founding post: the GMI is defined here, component by component, nine days after the blog
began. It had been the wiki's **most-cited post with no summary page** across two reviews. It
is also in his own "My Favorite Posts" category — the queue this batch is drawn from.

Written with the index reading **0**, which sets the tone: "With a zero reading, I will not
waste our time talking about stocks to buy."

## Key claims

- **Motivation.** "Keep in mind that I am a chicken and have successfully dodged the 98 bear,
  the 2000-2002 bear and most of the present decline." The GMI exists to summarise "the
  indicators I use to monitor the general market trend" in one number.
- **The universe:** "almost 4,000 actively traded stocks that were $5 or over several months
  ago," counted in TC2005. (By 2026 the table says 6,000+ — see [gmi.md](../methodology/gmi.md).)
- **Component 1** is explicitly provisional: he had been computing the Successful 10-Day index
  only since the prior March, took 106 as a reference, and set the bar at 100 — "I reserve the
  right to change this threshold as I gain more experience with it." He did: by 2014 it became
  ≥50%, and the published table adds a `min. 20` floor.
- **Rationale for component 1:** "In a rising market, traders have the confidence to let strong
  stocks climb... In a bad market, anxious traders take profits quickly and stocks tend to
  gyrate."
- **Component 2's logic:** when daily new lows exceed new highs "we really should not be buying
  growth stocks with the expectation of making a profit. The odds are simply against us."
- **Components 3–5 are deliberately undisclosed**, and he says so with a joke: the QQQQ index is
  positive "when my technical indicators suggest that it is in a rising trend. (You don't
  really expect me to disclose how I do that, do you? You would never visit me again.)" The
  weekly QQQQ is the same measure on the weekly trend, and "a change in trend will occur in the
  daily indicator before the weekly indicator."
- **Component 6** comes from the IBD newspaper's mutual fund section and must be above its
  50-day average. The post carries a **later inline update** — IBD stopped printing the 50-day
  average, "IBD subscribers can get the full chart by typing in **0muti**" — which is how the
  founding post itself records the 2009 sourcing change.
- **Epistemics.** On component 6: "Don't take my word for it. Go back and see how this index
  relates to your trading success."
- The GMI "just counts the number of these conditions that are positive."
