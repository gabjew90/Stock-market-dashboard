# WW 2005-07-13 — The 8% rule and never averaging down, against Cramer

**Source:** [GMI:+6; GDW; Shame on you, Cramer](../../raw/posts/2005-07-13-gmi6-gdw-shame-on-you-cramer.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2005/07/gmi6-gdw-shame-on-you-cramer/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

A rule stated by rejecting its opposite. "Tonight I heard Cramer make the distinction between the short term
trader and the investor. **Traders should cut their losses at 8% but investors who know the 'true value' of
stocks can buy on the way down. What a prescription for disaster! No one should average down and no one should
let their losses on a purchase exceed 8%.**" The counter-example is specific: "**one could have bought Enron all
the way down by being a true believer in the accounting statistics and the repeated optimistic company
announcements. By the time we outsiders learn the facts, the insiders** [have already sold]." Two things worth
separating. The **8% loss limit is O'Neil's**, adopted without qualification and applied to *everyone* — he
explicitly refuses the trader/investor distinction that would exempt some holders from it. And the
never-average-down rule is grounded in an information asymmetry rather than in price behaviour: the outsider
learns last, so a falling price is not a discount to someone without the insiders' knowledge.

## Key claims

- **The trader/investor distinction is rejected outright** — the 8% limit and the no-averaging-down rule apply to both.
- **"No one should average down and no one should let their losses on a purchase exceed 8%"** — the clearest single statement of both rules, in 2005.
- **The argument against averaging down is informational**: by the time outsiders learn the facts, insiders have acted. Enron as the case.
- "True value" as a licence to buy declines is named as "a prescription for disaster."
- Consistent with [value is a myth](../methodology/trading-philosophy.md): if price is a poll, a lower price is not evidence of a bargain.
