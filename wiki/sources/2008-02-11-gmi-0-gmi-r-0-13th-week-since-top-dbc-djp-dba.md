# WW 2008-02-11 — Component 1 gets its `min. 20` floor — the third and final revision

**Source:** [GMI: 0; GMI-R: 0; 13th week since top; DBC, DJP, DBA](../../raw/posts/2008-02-11-gmi-0-gmi-r-0-13th-week-since-top-dbc-djp-dba.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2008/02/gmi-0-gmi-r-0-13th-week-since-top-dbc-djp-dba/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

The post where the floor visible on every later GMI table is introduced. He restates the component — "this index looks at the number of stocks in my universe that hit a new 52 week high 10 days ago and counts how many of these closed today higher than on the day they hit the new high 10 days earlier. The idea is that in a strong market, stocks will continue to advance after hitting a new high" — then the change: "this indicator is positive if there are 100 successful 10-day new highs, or if not, if the percentage of these stocks that are higher is at least 50%. **I have added the additional requirement that it be 50% and that there were at least 20 new highs 10 days ago (the denominator ≥ 20). I think that the 50% requirement is not enough if it is based on fewer than 20 stocks**, as was the case last Friday." Context: 29 new highs against 83 new lows, with new lows far below January's peak of 1,453 — "most stocks are holding above their January lows."

## Key claims

- **The `min. 20` denominator floor is added 2008-02-11** — the third and final revision to component 1 (count > 100, 2005-04; count *or* ≥ 50%, 2005-07-11; *and* denominator ≥ 20, 2008-02-11).
- His reasoning: "the 50% requirement is not enough if it is based on fewer than 20 stocks."
- The component's purpose restated: in a strong market, stocks keep advancing after a new high.
- New-low counts compared against a prior extreme (1,453 in January 2008) as a read on whether lows are holding.
