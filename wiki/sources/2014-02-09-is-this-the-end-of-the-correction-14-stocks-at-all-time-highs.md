# WW 2014-02-09 — Rotating from SQQQ to TQQQ — 'the key is to not rush in but to ease into the new move'

**Source:** [Is this the end of the correction? 14 stocks at all-time highs!](../../raw/posts/2014-02-09-is-this-the-end-of-the-correction-14-stocks-at-all-time-highs.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2014/02/is-this-the-end-of-the-correction-14-stocks-at-all-time-highs/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

The clearest description of how he turns a portfolio around at a trend change. D-10 on Friday; "another flat or up day on Monday will end the down-trend… I am most certain of a change in trend when it persists for at least 5 days, however. **I sold most of my SQQQ on Friday and will sell the rest on Monday if the QQQ holds. I will then buy a very small position in TQQQ and will only add to it at a higher price later in the week, probably on Friday.** If a new short term up-trend begins and it is for real, then I will have many days on which to accumulate TQQQ. **The key is to not rush in but to ease into the new move.**" And the symmetric branch: "if the QQQ weakens a lot on Monday, I will buy back some more SQQQ and ride the continuing down-trend. **One must be agnostic in this business and ride whatever trend becomes apparent.**" Plus the new-high list read during a decline: "stocks appearing on it are exhibiting unusual strength."

## Key claims

- The turn procedure: exit the inverse ETF as the trend ends, take a very small position in the leveraged long, add only at **higher** prices later in the week.
- "The key is to not rush in but to ease into the new move."
- Both branches pre-planned — if the trend does not turn, buy back the inverse.
- "One must be agnostic in this business and ride whatever trend becomes apparent."
