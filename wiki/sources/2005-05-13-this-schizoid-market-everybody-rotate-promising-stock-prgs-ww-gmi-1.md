# WW 2005-05-13 — Rotation detection by component breadth; MoneyStream explained; a pilot buy priced

**Source:** [This Schizoid Market, Everybody Rotate, Promising Stock PRGS — WW-GMI: +1](../../raw/posts/2005-05-13-this-schizoid-market-everybody-rotate-promising-stock-prgs-ww-gmi-1.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2005/05/this-schizoid-market-everybody-rotate-promising-stock-prgs-ww-gmi-1/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

The longest untouched post in the corpus (1,909 words). Three durable methods: detecting sector
rotation by comparing *component breadth* across indexes; the fullest explanation of
**MoneyStream** anywhere in the corpus; and a pilot-buy setup priced to the cent, including the
buy-stop alternative and its cost.

## Key claims

- **Rotation is read from component breadth, not index prices:** "Seventy percent of the stocks
  in the NASDAQ 100 rose today, compared with 25% of the stocks in the S&P500... **Are we
  rotating from commodities to techs?**" A same-day divergence in the *share of components
  rising* is the tell, and he checks it isn't one stock: DELL +7.4% "cannot be attributed
  solely" — 14 Nasdaq stocks rose 3%+, so "there was broad based buying in techs."
- **The Weekly QQQQ Index's meaning, quantified early:** "This weekly index, when it turns
  positive, indicates to me that **a major uptrend has begun that typically lasts months, not
  days**." (Corroborates its later "primary long-term indicator" status.)
- **A caveat on his own new-high statistics during rotations:** the bearish new-high counts
  "would not reflect the new strength in the tech stocks because these stocks would still be far
  from trading at new highs" — breadth built on 52-week highs lags a rotation by construction.
- **MoneyStream explained:** the Worden proprietary cumulative volume indicator, "similar (but
  not identical) to on-balance volume"; in a rising stock "I want to see the MoneyStream at
  **new peaks**." Plus the 50-day-average-of-volume line as the heavy-volume benchmark:
  strength = "almost all such higher volume days are up days."
- **The PRGS setup, priced:** gap up on huge volume in March → run → low-volume pullback to the
  30-day average → high-volume bounce Friday. The trade: "a pilot buy of 25 or 50 shares...
  sell stop right below today's low, around 26.40 — willing to risk about **$1.10 per share**."
- **The buy-stop alternative, with its cost stated:** place the entry above the day's high at
  27.65 so "I only purchase PRGS if it has the power to move higher than it did today. The
  downside is that I buy it a little higher and risk losing a little more — about $1.25/share."
- Also: a short-term interest-rate index below its 50-day for the first time in over a year,
  read as the market pricing a Fed pause — macro entering only through a chart.
