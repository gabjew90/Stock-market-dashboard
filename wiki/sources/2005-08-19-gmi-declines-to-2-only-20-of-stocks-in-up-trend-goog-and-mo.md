# WW 2005-08-19 — The naked chart, and the 10/30 rule it produced

**Source:** [GMI declines to +2; only 20% of stocks in up-trend; GOOG and MO](../../raw/posts/2005-08-19-gmi-declines-to-2-only-20-of-stocks-in-up-trend-goog-and-mo.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2005/08/gmi-declines-to-2-only-20-of-stocks-in-up-trend-goog-and-mo/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

**The "naked chart" technique, and the rule it produced.** He plots the **10-day and 30-day averages with the
prices removed**: "it was clear that the up trend in a stock (GOOG as an example) was clearly shown by the **10
day average's being consistently above the 30 day average**." Stripping price out is the point — what remains is
the relationship between the two averages, with nothing to argue with. Applied to GOOG after its secondary-offering
announcement: "for the first time since the stock rallied in April, its 10 day average remains consistently below
its 30 day average." **The rule**, stated as a lesson: "**Do not buy or hold any stock where its 10 day average
is consistently below its 30 day average. Such stocks are my short candidates, especially after a long period
where the 10 day was above the 30 day**, as in GOOG." Note the qualifier *consistently* — a single crossing is
not the signal. Market context: GMI down to +2, 66 new highs, D-3, and the percentage of his universe in a
short-term up-trend at a new low of 20%; "for now, **buying stocks at new highs is a high risk strategy**." He
held only puts and cash in the IRA.

## Key claims

- **The naked chart**: the 10- and 30-day averages drawn without price, so the trend question reduces to which average is on top.
- **The rule it yields**: do not buy or hold a stock whose 10-day average is *consistently* below its 30-day — and such stocks become short candidates.
- **The best short candidates are former leaders**: the rule bites hardest "after a long period where the 10 day was above the 30 day."
- "Consistently" is doing real work — the signal is a sustained relationship, not a crossing.
- A news event (GOOG's stock issuance) is used to *check* a technical read that was already in place, not to generate one.
