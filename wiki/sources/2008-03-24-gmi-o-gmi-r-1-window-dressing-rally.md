# WW 2008-03-24 — How bear markets end — the leadership sequence

**Source:** [GMI: O: GMI-R: 1; Window dressing rally?](../../raw/posts/2008-03-24-gmi-o-gmi-r-1-window-dressing-rally.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2008/03/gmi-o-gmi-r-1-window-dressing-rally/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

**A model of how a bottom is populated, from 40 years of watching them.** "I remember how bear markets have
ended over the past 40 years and that **the first stocks that people feel more comfortable buying are the big
name large cap stocks. Only after a new bull market is under way and people have some profits, do they feel
confident to buy the more speculative stocks.**" So early-recovery leadership is large-cap and defensive by
*psychology* rather than by fundamentals — buyers with recent losses buy what feels safe first, and the
speculative names only run once those buyers are ahead. That is a testable claim about sequencing, and it
explains why his own growth-stock screens stay empty for a while after a turn. The same post gives the
window-dressing mechanism precisely: "we are near the end of the first quarter when mutual funds are believed to
buy strong stocks to appear in their portfolios when their end of quarter reports are released. **Since these
reports do not show the date of purchase, the fund managers can look especially smart, even though they may have
held these winners only for a few days.**" D-54, 24 new highs against 174 new lows.

## Key claims

- **Recovery leadership is sequenced by psychology**: large caps first, speculative names only after buyers have profits.
- That is why growth screens stay empty immediately after a bottom — the stocks he wants are the *last* to be bought.
- **The window-dressing mechanism turns on disclosure**: quarterly reports show holdings but not purchase dates.
- Drawn from watching "how bear markets have ended over the past 40 years" — presented as pattern recognition, not measurement.
