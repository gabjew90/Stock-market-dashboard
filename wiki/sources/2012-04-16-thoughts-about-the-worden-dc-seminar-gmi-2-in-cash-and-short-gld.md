# WW 2012-04-16 — The Worden DC seminar; how he uses the GMI signals — pension by the weekly Stage 2, IRA by the GMI Sell (2 days below 3)

**Source:** [Thoughts about the Worden DC Seminar; GMI: 2; In cash and short GLD](../../raw/posts/2012-04-16-thoughts-about-the-worden-dc-seminar-gmi-2-in-cash-and-short-gld.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2012/04/thoughts-about-the-worden-dc-seminar-gmi-2-in-cash-and-short-gld/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

Loose ends after his Worden seminar. Covered calls (Alan Ellman's books; he is teaching himself weekly calls on SPY and QQQ). Then the clearest early statement of the two-speed design: "The QQQ weekly chart is in a Stage 2 up-trend. As long as the weekly pattern remains in a Stage 2, I keep my university pension invested in mutual funds. There are limits to how often I can trade the mutual funds. I only sell my pension funds when the 30 week average curves down. I am short or in cash with my more short term IRA and speculative money, however, because the GMI gave me a sell signal last week (**2 consecutive days below 3**)." Other short-term guides listed: the 30-day averages of SPY/DIA/QQQ, their daily 10.4.4 stochastics, T2108, IBD's market pulse. "Every person must design a systematic set of entry and exit rules that fit with his/her tolerance for risk. Without such a set of rules, it is preferable to forego individual stocks and to buy some index ETF's like SPY or QQQ and to just ride the major market trend." QQQ up-trend at 75 days — "rare… to last much longer"; QQQ 10.4 stochastic ~22.

## Key claims

- GMI Sell = 2 consecutive days below 3, stated April 2012 (two years before the 2014 post usually cited).
- Pension = weekly Stage 2 / 30-week curve-down; IRA = GMI signals — the two-speed design in one paragraph.
- The short-term dashboard: 30-day averages, daily 10.4.4 stochastics, T2108, IBD market pulse.
- No rules → index ETFs and ride the trend.
