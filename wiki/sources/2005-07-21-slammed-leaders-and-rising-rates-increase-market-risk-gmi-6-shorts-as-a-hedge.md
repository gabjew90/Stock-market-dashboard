# WW 2005-07-21 — Selling ahead of the GMI; leaders slammed on good earnings

**Source:** [Slammed leaders and rising rates increase market risk; GMI: +6; shorts as a hedge](../../raw/posts/2005-07-21-slammed-leaders-and-rising-rates-increase-market-risk-gmi-6-shorts-as-a-hedge.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2005/07/slammed-leaders-and-rising-rates-increase-market-risk-gmi-6-shorts-as-a-hedge/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

With the GMI still at its maximum of +6 — a full Buy reading — he writes that he is "a little concerned"
and reaches for the exit anyway: "**Yes, I know the GMI is still at +6, but if I wait for it to give a definite
sell I will lose most of my profits.**" The trigger is not the index but the behaviour of the leaders: "**When
stocks like GOOG and MSFT get slammed after reporting earnings, it may be time to take some money off of the
table**… If good earnings cannot support GOOG, then what type of news is left to support it? Too many of the
leaders are finding themselves in multiple point declines. **When the leaders get into trouble, the rest of the
market is not far behind.**" Second worry: his short-term interest-rate indicator "has gapped up and keeps
hitting new highs," and on the monthly chart "it took much less of an increase in rates from 1998-2000 to burst
the market bubble." He is explicit that this is not what his own method licenses — "I know I am not supposed to
predict the market, but I have the feeling we are in for a rough period." Component 1 was the soft spot inside
the +6: only 92 of 174 (53%) of the stocks that hit a 52-week high ten days earlier closed higher.

## Key claims

- **A maximum GMI can be overridden by leadership damage.** The stated reason is the lag: waiting for a definite sell costs most of the accumulated profit.
- **Leaders sold off on good earnings is the tell** — the earliest instance of a diagnostic he restates in 2006, 2014 and 2016.
- A short-term interest-rate indicator (the 13-week bill yield) is on his weekly/monthly charts from 2005, read for the rate *trend*, not the level.
- He flags his own forecasting as a departure from the method rather than dressing it up as a signal.
- The long disclaimer block appears in full here, and dates his trading experience at **40 years** as of 2005.
