# WW 2005-04-25 — The indicator's published range, and the mirror in week two

**Source:** [The Interest Rate Index](../../raw/posts/2005-04-25-the-interest-rate-index.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2005/04/the-interest-rate-index/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

The blog's ninth day, and it already contains both halves of component 1 **with a published range**:
"**WishingWealth 10 day successful new high index: 4/25 20; 4/22 19; 4/21 28; 4/20 12. Range since March, 2005:
8-106.** (This index counts the number of stocks, of 4,000, that hit a 52 week high 10 days ago and closed today
greater than they did 10 days ago.)" A stated observed range is what makes a raw count readable — 20 means
something only against 8–106 — and it is the earliest calibration anywhere in the corpus. **And the mirror
statistic, on 25 April 2005**: "**I have a similar index that counts new lows that continue to decline — it is 56
today. New lows are a better bet than new highs these days.**" That is three weeks earlier than the May 2005
instance the wiki dates the mirror to, and it is already being used the way it always would be — to say which
side the tape favours. The conclusion drawn is the market gate in embryo: "with only 20/4000 stocks hitting new
highs and continuing to rise, **it would seem to me that this is not the time to buy stocks trading at new
highs. I will wait for a stronger market.**"

## Key claims

- **The mirror statistic exists on 2005-04-25** — within days of the blog's start, and three weeks before the instance the wiki dates it to.
- **A published observed range (8–106) since March 2005** — the earliest calibration of any indicator here, and what makes a raw count interpretable.
- The component is used immediately as a *strategy* verdict, not a market one: "this is not the time to buy stocks trading at new highs."
- Both tails were being computed from the beginning; only the high side was ever published as part of the GMI.
