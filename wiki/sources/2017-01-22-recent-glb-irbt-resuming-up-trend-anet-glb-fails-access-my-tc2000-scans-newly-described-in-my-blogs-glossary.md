# WW 2017-01-22 — The TC2000 Club scans documented; the Darvas scan's weekend yield; RWBCount 12 of 12; what no indicator warns you about

**Source:** [Recent GLB $IRBT resuming up-trend; $ANET GLB fails; Access my TC2000 scans–newly described in my blog’s glossary](../../raw/posts/2017-01-22-recent-glb-irbt-resuming-up-trend-anet-glb-fails-access-my-tc2000-scans-newly-described-in-my-blogs-glossary.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2017/01/recent-glb-irbt-resuming-up-trend-anet-glb-fails-access-my-tc2000-scans-newly-described-in-my-blogs-glossary/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

He documents his WatchLists and EasyScans in the blog's own glossary and publishes them to his TC2000 Club — including an **Alltimehighs** watchlist that the other scans run against. The Darvas scan returned 53 stocks that weekend; IRBT is the worked case (GLB in late October, a run to ~$60, a five-week consolidation, renewed strength) with "a maximum RWBCount of 12 (of 12). All of the weekly moving averages line up properly." And the honest limit, on ANET: after he tweeted about it moving up on above-average volume, it gapped down on huge volume on legal news — "I saw no technical indicator that warned me in advance about this significant decline except for the fact that the stock traded back below its 30 day average a few days later."

## Key claims

- Scans and watchlists are published to his TC2000 Club and described in the blog glossary; an Alltimehighs watchlist is the base universe.
- **RWBCount** — the count (0–12) of weekly GMMA averages in correct order; 12 of 12 is full alignment.
- News gaps are not forecastable by his indicators; the 30-day average is the after-the-fact tell.
