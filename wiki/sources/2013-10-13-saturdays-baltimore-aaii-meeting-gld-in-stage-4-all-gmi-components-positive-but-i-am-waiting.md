# WW 2013-10-13 — The AAII talk: sleeping point, failed GLBs, GLD's Stage 4

**Source:** [Saturday's Baltimore AAII meeting; GLD in Stage 4; All GMI components positive, but I am waiting](../../raw/posts/2013-10-13-saturdays-baltimore-aaii-meeting-gld-in-stage-4-all-gmi-components-positive-but-i-am-waiting.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2013/10/saturdays-baltimore-aaii-meeting-gld-in-stage-4-all-gmi-components-positive-but-i-am-waiting/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

A summary of his Baltimore AAII presentation — one of the off-blog teaching venues the reviews
flagged as unmapped, with a **link to the December 2012 Worden webinar** as the canonical
strategy summary. Adds the "sleeping point" doctrine, the failed-GLB-as-weakness signal in its
sell-side form, and a full GLD stage walkthrough on the short side.

## Key claims

- **Sell down to the sleeping point:** "it is not a crime to get out of the market and to **sell
  down to the sleeping point** (when one is comfortable with one's portfolio risk)." Addressed
  to a Boomer audience "trying to grow our assets while preserving them."
- **Tax friction acknowledged for taxable accounts** — wash-sale rules cited — with his own
  arrangement restated: trading in IRAs, "no immediate tax consequences when I buy and sell."
- **The failed GLB inverted into a sell-side signal:** "When a green line break-out fails, it is
  often a sign of technical weakness. Stocks should break through a green line top and keep
  rising. **When a new green line top is formed... I watch for an exit.**" A stock that builds a
  *new* green line after failing is a candidate to leave, not to buy.
- **GLD as the worked short-side stage case:** Stage 2 2009–2012 → new green line top late
  2011 → long Stage 3 under a flattening 30-week → clear Stage 4 by April 2013. "**I never hold
  a security that closes below its 30 week average.**" When would GLD interest him again? "When
  it enters a Stage 2 up-trend" — the answer is a stage, not a price.
- **Staged everything, restated to the audience:** wade in and out in stages; add only if the
  prior purchase works; "I never average down and add good money to bad."
- **The honest simplification offered to non-traders:** "the best strategy for me might be just
  to own the SPY, using Stage Analysis to time entries and exits," with leveraged S&P ETFs (SDS,
  UPRO, SH, SPXU) for larger appetite and the both-directions risk warning. And: "**have an exit
  plan for every trade.**"
- Context: cash in the trading accounts through the debt-limit standoff while the pension stayed
  invested (indexes in Stage 2) — the two-speed design working through a news event.
