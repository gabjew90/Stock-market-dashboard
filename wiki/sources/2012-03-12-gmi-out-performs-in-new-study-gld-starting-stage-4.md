# WW 2012-03-12 — The GMI out-performs in a new study — and a backtest he ran with a student

**Source:** [GMI out-performs in new study; GLD starting Stage 4 decline?](../../raw/posts/2012-03-12-gmi-out-performs-in-new-study-gld-starting-stage-4.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2012/03/gmi-out-performs-in-new-study-gld-starting-stage-4/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

Two pieces of evidence about his own signal. First, live: since the GMI buy signal on 12/23, QQQ +15.9% and QLD +33.9%; AAPL managed +35.2% over the same stretch, "**but riding a single stock is much more dangerous to me than riding an index that reflects a basket of stocks**." Second, and new to this wiki: an independent site, dark-liquidity.com, had posted GMI performance applied to QQQ/QLD, and "using the GMI to trade the QLD has greatly outperformed all other strategies on the chart. **The strategy employed uses the same decision rules that my student and I backtested to 2006 with incredible success.**" So a formal backtest of the GMI rules existed by 2012, run with a student, back to 2006 — though the rules, the parameters and the results are not reproduced in the post. The standing caveat rides along: "leveraged ETFs, because of daily compounding of results, **can be extremely dangerous when a decline sets in**."

## Key claims

- **A GMI backtest to 2006 was run by Dr. Wish and a student** — referenced but never published in detail on the blog.
- A third party (dark-liquidity.com) independently charted GMI-driven QQQ/QLD performance.
- AAPL beat QLD over the same window, and he still prefers the index: single-stock risk, not return, is the argument.
- Daily compounding makes leveraged ETFs "extremely dangerous when a decline sets in."
