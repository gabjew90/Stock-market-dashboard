# WW 2025-04-13 — The weekly recovery sequence, and the gap that closes

**Source:** [Blog Post: Day 34 of $QQQ short term down-trend; As I had suggested $SPY closed its gap on weekly chart and kissed its 4wk avg., see weekly chart; Indexes remain in a 4wk<10wk<30wk down-trend; Cash is King.](../../raw/posts/2025-04-13-blog-post-day-34-of-qqq-short-term-down-trend-as-i-had-suggested-spy-closed-its-gap-on-weekly-chart-and-kisse.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2025/04/blog-post-day-34-of-qqq-short-term-down-trend-as-i-had-suggested-spy-closed-its-gap-on-weekly-chart-and-kissed-its-4wk-avg-see-weekly-chart-indexes-remain-in-a-4wk/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

**How a weekly down-trend turns, in order.** "**To indicate emerging strength, the index must first close the
week above its 4wk average. Then when the 4wk avg rises above the 10 wk avg and then the 10 wk avg rises above
the 30 wk average we have a possible up-trend.**" Three ordered steps, the mirror of the Stage 4 breakdown, and
the diagnosis of where the market stood: "right now the 4wk (red dotted) is well below the 10wk (blue dotted)
which is declining below the 30 wk (solid red)." **A gap pattern with a mechanism**: "when a stock in a
down-trend has a **gap below the declining 4 wk average** it often means **the stock is extended on the down side
and it will often rebound to close the gap**" — which had just happened in SPY, QQQ and DIA. Note the framing:
the gap-close is *extension unwinding*, not a trend change, which is why he pairs it with a warning: "**it takes
a lot of discipline to refrain from buying the sudden sharp bounces in declining stocks way below their highs.
The most successful traders know how to do this. I prefer to wait to buy the first stocks that come through**"
the decline to new highs.

## Key claims

- **The weekly recovery sequence**: close above the 4-week, then 4wk > 10wk, then 10wk > 30wk. Ordered, and the mirror of the breakdown.
- **A gap below a declining 4-week average signals downside extension** and tends to be closed by a rebound — an unwind, not a turn.
- The rebound it produces is exactly the one not to buy: "it takes a lot of discipline to refrain from buying the sudden sharp bounces in declining stocks."
- "I prefer to wait to buy the first stocks that come through" — the post-decline leadership screen restated as the alternative.
