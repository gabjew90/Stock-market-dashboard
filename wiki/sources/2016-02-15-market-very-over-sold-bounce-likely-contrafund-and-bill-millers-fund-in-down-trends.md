# WW 2016-02-15 — GMMA charts applied to mutual funds — the pension's missing instrument

**Source:** [Market very over-sold; bounce likely; Contrafund and Bill Miller’s fund in down-trends](../../raw/posts/2016-02-15-market-very-over-sold-bounce-likely-contrafund-and-bill-millers-fund-in-down-trends.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2016/02/market-very-over-sold-bounce-likely-contrafund-and-bill-millers-fund-in-down-trends/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

**The pension is held in mutual funds, and here is how he times them.** "I thought I should show you that
**the GMMA charts I use to monitor the market indexes and individual stocks can also be used to time entry and
exit from mutual funds**" — worked on Fidelity Contrafund and Bill Miller's fund, both then in down-trends. That
closes an obvious gap in the two-speed design: the pension's decisions are described throughout the corpus in
terms of index averages (the 30-week, Stage 4), but the assets themselves are funds, and a fund has a price
series like anything else. The RWB/BWR reading transfers directly. It also explains the June 2025 re-entry, where
a **green line breakout on the Contrafund's own weekly chart** was one of the conditions he named — the same
technique applied to the same instrument nine years later. Context: GMI at 0 of 6, "way too early to determine if
this bear has died or is just hibernating.

## Key claims

- **GMMA/RWB charts work on mutual funds**, which is how the pension's holdings are timed rather than only its benchmark index.
- Named examples: Fidelity Contrafund and Bill Miller's fund, both in down-trends in February 2016.
- This is the technique behind the 2025 re-entry, where a GLB on the Contrafund's weekly chart was a stated condition.
- The two-speed design's slow half is not benchmark-only — the actual instrument gets charted.
