# WW 2017-07-16 — O'Neil's shopping list — the watch-list procedure, and the discipline of not trading

**Source:** [Some of William O’Neil’s wisdom exemplified by the great traders I watched; GMI is Green](../../raw/posts/2017-07-16-some-of-william-oneils-wisdom-exemplified-by-the-great-traders-i-watched-gmi-is-green.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2017/07/some-of-william-oneils-wisdom-exemplified-by-the-great-traders-i-watched-gmi-is-green/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

He traces his own entry procedure to a passage in O'Neil's *How To Make Money in Stocks* (1st ed. revised,
1988, p. 197) and quotes it in full. The procedure, in summary: review a graph book each week, list the stocks that meet your technical
**and** fundamental criteria, write down the pivot price at which you would buy and each stock's average daily
volume, carry that list for the next couple of weeks, and act when a stock trades at your price on volume you
judge will finish at least 50% above average — "the more demand for a stock at the buy point, the better."
His gloss is the important part: "**The approach here is very different than just scanning the market real time
and buying something that looks good.** This more reasoned approach suggests that during **the unemotional calm
of the weekend or night before trading**, review stocks that are setting up… **This strategy assumes that one's
trading is rule based and systematic, guided by specific criteria.** Then make a small list of candidates to
monitor for a break-out on above average volume. **One can then pull the trigger quickly.** (**I use TC2000 to
alert me when a stock on my list is having a GLB on unusually high daily volume — volume buzz.**)" The
discipline half comes from watching Mark Minervini and David Ryan trade live at Minervini's October 2016
workshop: "both men showed extreme discipline, and **when neither of them found a stock on their watch lists
that met their criteria that day, they just sat tight** — Wow!!! **How many of us think we have to buy
something every day and get seduced by the action?**"

## Key claims

- **The watch-list-and-alert workflow is O'Neil's, and he says so** — the pivot price and the volume condition are set the night before, in calm, not found intraday.
- **The list is built to make the decision fast, not to make it later**: pre-stated criteria are what allow him to "pull the trigger quickly."
- The TC2000 GLB-plus-volume-buzz alert is documented in **July 2017**, a year before the intraday ATH condition set he published in October 2018.
- **Not trading is the skill being demonstrated.** Two of the best traders he has watched sat out a full day for want of a qualifying setup.
- Volume is a *demand* measurement at the buy point — the reason he flags a quiet breakout as defective.
- Off-blog teaching context: an AAII DC Metro workshop with options educator Dr. Alan Ellman.
