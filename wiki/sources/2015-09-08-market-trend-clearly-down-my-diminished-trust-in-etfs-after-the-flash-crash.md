# WW 2015-09-08 — Market trend clearly down; diminished trust in ETFs after the flash crash

**Source:** [Market trend clearly down; my diminished trust in ETFs after the flash crash](../../raw/posts/2015-09-08-market-trend-clearly-down-my-diminished-trust-in-etfs-after-the-flash-crash.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2015/09/market-trend-clearly-down-my-diminished-trust-in-etfs-after-the-flash-crash/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

GMI at 0 on a Sell since August 24, QQQ Day D-18, nearly 100% cash in the trading accounts and most of the pension back in money market — while new pension contributions keep dollar-cost-averaging into funds. The teaching is the August 24 mini flash crash: his conservative preferred-stock ETF PFF opened at 38.52 vs a 39.01 close, traded to 32.32 within ten minutes, and closed at 38.26. ETF prices are set by order flow, not intraday NAV; a market sell order or a standing sell-stop would have been filled at a huge, unwarranted loss. "This experience is enough to keep me away from most ETFs."

## Key claims

- "Imagine what would have happened if I had placed a market order to sell or had a standing sell stop order in place to protect me against a small decline! I might have been sold out at a huge loss."
- "The ETF basket of holdings is not calculated real time during the day. Orders are executed according to the buy and sell orders that come in and not according to the intrinsic value of the ETF's holdings."
- Mutual funds price once at the close on computed NAV — the argument for keeping the pension in funds rather than ETFs.
- "My *new* pension contributions keep buying mutual fund shares as they fall (dollar cost averaging)."
