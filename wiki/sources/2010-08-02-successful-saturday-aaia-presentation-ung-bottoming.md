# WW 2010-08-02 — Why commodities are the exception to buying all-time highs

**Source:** [Successful Saturday AAIA presentation; UNG bottoming?](../../raw/posts/2010-08-02-successful-saturday-aaia-presentation-ung-bottoming.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2010/08/successful-saturday-aaia-presentation-ung-bottoming/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

**Why commodities get a different method — the clearest statement of it.** "**I usually only buy stocks at
all-time highs. Commodities are different, however, because they have a true value. Natural gas is not likely to
go to zero. I therefore think that commodities go back and forth between oversold and overbought.**" That is a
principled split: equities get trend-following because a company can go to zero and a price has no floor;
commodities get mean-reversion because the thing has use value and therefore a floor. Applied to UNG: bought on
an extreme stochastic low followed by a buy signal, with the monthly chart showing UNG "closed above its **5
month moving average** for the first time since the decline began in July, 2008," and the scaling rule attached —
"if I am right and UNG continues to strengthen, **I will add to my position**." **And a personal base rate on the
market gate**: "the QQQQ and SPY ETF's have closed above their critical 10 week averages for two weeks. **I tend
to make money trading on the long side only when these indexes close above their 10 week averages.**" T2108 at
79%, "near overbought territory, **where it can often remain for months**." He offers the underlying PCF —
"oversold stochastic followed by a buy signal" — to anyone who emails.

## Key claims

- **The equity/commodity split is principled, not stylistic**: a commodity has "a true value" and a floor, so it oscillates; a stock can go to zero, so you follow its trend.
- **"I tend to make money trading on the long side only when these indexes close above their 10 week averages"** — the market gate stated as an observation about his own results.
- The monthly 5-month average used as the commodity's trend marker, at a scale matching the instrument's cycle.
- Scaling in on confirmation applies to the mean-reversion trade too — a small starter, added to only if it strengthens.
- An overbought T2108 "can often remain for months" — level as a condition, not a trigger.
