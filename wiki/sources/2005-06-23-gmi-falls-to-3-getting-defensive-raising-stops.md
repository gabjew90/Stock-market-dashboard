# WW 2005-06-23 — Raising stops before going to cash; 'I am a chicken'

**Source:** [GMI falls to +3;  Getting defensive-raising stops](../../raw/posts/2005-06-23-gmi-falls-to-3-getting-defensive-raising-stops.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2005/06/gmi-falls-to-3-getting-defensive-raising-stops/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

The defensive sequence written out as it happens, at GMI +3 on day 34 of a rally. The steps are ordered:
stops first, cash later. "I was stopped out of NDAQ and BOOM today. **I am moving all of my stops up close to
today's lows.** I think the odds of having successful longs is decreasing." Then the trigger for the last step,
stated precisely and keyed to the trend indicator rather than to the GMI: "**When my QQQQ daily index definitely
turns negative, I go to 100% cash.**" The temperament behind it is put plainly: "**I have told you that I am a
chicken. I would rather be sold out even if I am wrong, preserve my capital, and live to trade another day.**"
Supporting readings that day: 17% of the Nasdaq-100 advancing, 21% of the S&P 500, 7% of the Dow 30; the share of
his universe above the 10-week average down from 80% to 73%; and the QQQQ declining "**on the biggest volume
since its decline on April 20**" — volume confirming the direction of the break.

## Key claims

- **Raising stops to just under the day's lows is the intermediate defensive step**, taken before the decision to hold cash and independent of it.
- **The 100%-cash trigger is the QQQQ short-term trend turning negative** — not the GMI, which was already at +3 and did not move him.
- Being stopped out while wrong is the accepted cost: "I would rather be sold out even if I am wrong."
- Heavy volume on a decline is read as confirmation; he dates it against the previous comparable session.
- Breadth read as a *change* (80% → 73% above the 10-week) rather than as a level.
