# WW 2008-11-24 — The cost of missing the sell signal, measured

**Source:** [GMI: 0; GMI-R: 0; S&P500 double top or market bottom?](../../raw/posts/2008-11-24-gmi-0-gmi-r-0-sp500-double-top-or-market-bottom.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2008/11/gmi-0-gmi-r-0-sp500-double-top-or-market-bottom/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

**The "no need to anticipate" doctrine, measured.** Fifty-nine days into the down-trend he prices what
lateness actually cost: "Since that day (**September 2**) the QQQQ has declined 41%. During that time, **99% of
the Nasdaq 100 stocks** (all but APOL — the proverbial needle in the haystack — sought by those addicted to the
bullish side) **have declined, and 44% have declined 50% or more. Even if we had missed the initial sell signal
and waited until a failed bounce on September 19, still, 99% of the Nasdaq100 component stocks have declined
since then, one half by more than 41%. So you see, one could have missed the first sell signal by about 2 weeks
and still have saved a lot of money by going into cash or short.**" A two-week delay on the exit still captured
almost all of the avoidable damage — which is the quantitative case for a lagging indicator being good enough.
**And an honest note on his own oversold gauge**: T2108 "rebounded from 5% to 7%, and is still in what *used to
be* indicative of an imminent bottom" — the italics are his, flagging that the historical threshold was not
working in 2008. **The long-cycle frame**: on the monthly S&P 500 he reads "a major multi-year double top" — the
1970s channel, the 1982 breakout, the 2000 top, the 2002 low, and a 2003–2007 advance ending "at about the same
level as in 2000." Two scenarios offered without a preference: "we could be back to a decade of a flat market
like we had in the 70's. On the other hand, we could have a **huge correction of the 1300%+ advance that began in
1982**." His position: "the current market shows *none* of the key signs evident to me in prior bear market
bottoms… **given that no one knows a bottom before it has occurred, it is best for me to remain in cash and a
little short until the market tips its hand**." Breadth: 21 new highs against 1,835 new lows.

## Key claims

- **Being two weeks late to the exit cost almost nothing**: 99% of Nasdaq-100 components declined from either date, half by more than 41% even from the later one. The strongest single argument for tolerating a lagging signal.
- **A single survivor named** (APOL out of 100) — and characterised as what "those addicted to the bullish side" go looking for.
- **He flags his own indicator failing in real time**: T2108 at 5–7% is "in what *used to be* indicative of an imminent bottom."
- **A bottom is defined by signs, not by level**: "the current market shows *none* of the key signs evident to me in prior bear market bottoms."
- The monthly S&P 500 read as a multi-year double top, with the 1970s flat decade and a correction of the whole 1982–2000 advance offered as alternatives, neither preferred.
- "No one knows a bottom before it has occurred" — so the stance is cash and a little short "until the market tips its hand."
