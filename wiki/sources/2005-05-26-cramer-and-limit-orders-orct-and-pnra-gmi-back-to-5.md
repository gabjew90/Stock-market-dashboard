# WW 2005-05-26 — BUY AND SELL AT THE MARKET; the standing buy-stop that re-enters for you; Peter Lynch's method

**Source:** [Cramer and limit orders; ORCT and PNRA; GMI back to +5](../../raw/posts/2005-05-26-cramer-and-limit-orders-orct-and-pnra-gmi-back-to-5.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2005/05/cramer-and-limit-orders-orct-and-pnra-gmi-back-to-5/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

Order-type doctrine the wiki had nowhere, plus the cleanest worked example of automated
re-entry after a shakeout — the mechanism that turns the "buy it back" discipline into a
standing order rather than a act of willpower. And the Peter Lynch observation method with
three personal cases.

## Key claims

- **The market-order doctrine, in capitals:** against Cramer's limit-order advice — "fortunes
  have been lost by persons trying to hold out for the last 1/8th of a point... **I therefore
  almost always trade using market orders**... It could be suicide to put a limit order in on a
  sell and not be able to sell because the stock never traded at my limit price. **BUY AND SELL
  AT THE MARKET!**" A few cents of slippage is the price of never missing the move or the exit.
- **The ORCT sequence — automated re-entry:** bought on a moving-average bounce, stopped out
  5/23 for a small loss, **but left a buy-stop standing at 22.95** (above the prior peak). Days
  later, "without my having to pay any attention to it," the order triggered at 22.98 and the
  stock rocketed to close 24.45. "How many times have I been shaken out of a stock only to have
  it immediately turn around and go up... **Some of my best trades have come from buying back
  shares that I had recently been scared out of.**" The re-entry discipline as a *resting
  order*, not a decision.
- **Why not a stop-*limit* to re-enter:** a stop-limit "might have missed climbing aboard" — the
  same never-miss-the-move logic applied to entries.
- **Emotional posture:** "I did not get angry or discouraged at the way the stock had acted. I
  simply automatically took my small loss."
- **Peter Lynch's method, with receipts:** watch what people around you embrace — the Panera
  line at his own IBD Meetup (PNRA 14× in 2000–03 *during* the Nasdaq collapse, then a 15-month
  plateau and breakout); Microsoft spotted as a Mac user in the 80s but fumbled at IPO ("How
  little I knew back then"); GOOG not fumbled; XMSR bought ~$2 on his son's enthusiasm.
  "Research the company immediately" — observation supplies candidates, charts supply timing.
