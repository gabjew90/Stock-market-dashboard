# WW 2021-08-11 — Why buying stocks over $200 works better, revisited

**Source:** [Why buying stocks over $200 works better–revisited, examples $BNTX and $GS](../../raw/posts/2021-08-11-blog-post-why-buying-stocks-over-200-works-better-revisited-examples-bntx-and-gs.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2021/08/blog-post-why-buying-stocks-over-200-works-better-revisited-examples-bntx-and-gs/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

**Supersedes the price-level threshold in [WW 2018-01-21](2018-01-21-why-buying-stocks-over-100-is-more-profitable.md).** Dr. Wish explicitly revisits his own 2018 "$100+" post and raises the working threshold to $200, and — the operationally important part — attaches it to a specific scan rather than leaving it as a preference. The underlying Livermore rationale (points gained, not percentage gained) is unchanged; only the number moves.

## Key claims

- "I now find more success trading stocks over $200." Stated as a revision of the 2018 position, which is linked directly from the post.
- "I therefore have added a filter to my scans for **oversold bounces** that now requires a current price > $200." The threshold change lands in the [OSB](../methodology/oversold-bounce.md) scan specifically.
- Rationale: "Stocks that can rise to $200 have attracted considerable buying and allow me more flexibility to control the trade" — and a large point move gives "a lot of space to pyramid into the stock and to control my risk."
- The 2018 empirical finding is carried forward unchanged, not re-derived: "Most large point moves occur in stocks already in triple digits, see my 2018 post."
- **BNTX worked example:** flagged by the OSB scan on 2021-07-16 at ~$231; rose to over $430 — a 200-point move. A VCP (volatility compression pattern) before the breakout was additional encouragement.
- **GS worked example:** after retesting its GLB at ~$275 in February, rose into the $400s, with "many green and black dot buy signals throughout the move."
