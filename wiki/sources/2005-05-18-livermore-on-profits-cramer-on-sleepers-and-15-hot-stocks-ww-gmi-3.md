# WW 2005-05-18 — Livermore's track money; the anti-sleeper argument; the first full rocket scan

**Source:** [Livermore on profits, Cramer on sleepers and 15 Hot Stocks — WW-GMI: +3](../../raw/posts/2005-05-18-livermore-on-profits-cramer-on-sleepers-and-15-hot-stocks-ww-gmi-3.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2005/05/livermore-on-profits-cramer-on-sleepers-and-15-hot-stocks-ww-gmi-3/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

Three things: the **"track money" principle** — risk profits freely, never capital — via
Livermore's racetrack story to Walter Chrysler; the argument *against* buying laggards
("sleepers"), aimed directly at Cramer; and the earliest fully-specified rocket scan in the
corpus, run live with its 15 survivors published.

## Key claims

- **Track money vs capital.** Livermore's bettor loses his $100k of winnings on the last race and
  says "'Not bad — I lost two bucks'" (his starting stake). The lesson: "Fear was not a valid
  reason for selling; **the only good reason to exit a profitable trade... is a technical signal
  resulting from the stock's behavior.**" And: he "averaged up on profitable trades and was not
  afraid to risk his profits. **Risking your capital, however, was suicide for a trader.**" Loeb
  cited for the same: average up slowly so little original money is ever at risk. This is the
  risk-accounting that makes pyramiding coherent.
- **The anti-sleeper argument.** Cramer had advised buying stocks that hadn't moved as
  "bullet proof." His response: Livermore, O'Neil and Darvas all bought demonstrated strength —
  "**Why would anyone attempt to seek winners by finding stocks that are not moving in a
  rally!**... The big money is to be made in buying the leading stocks — the ones that burst to
  new highs as soon as the market gains strength." The rockets-on-the-launch-pad image recurs.
- **The first full rocket scan, criteria explicit:** universe of 4,000 → new 52-week high within
  10 days (371 hits) → quarterly EPS gain ≥100% → at least doubled in the past year → near
  all-time highs → **15 survivors**, published in a ranked matrix with EPS across three periods,
  revenue growth, MoneyStream, price ÷ price-250-days-ago, and volume-this-month ÷
  volume-6-months-ago ("a rocket often has extraordinary increases in volume as it rises").
- **A valuation observation rare for him:** the 15 survivors' "PE ratios are all below their
  earnings growth rates. This may be symptomatic of a market that has not yet bid up growth
  stocks to astronomical levels" — PEG-style reasoning used as a *market-temperature* read, not
  a stock-picking criterion.
