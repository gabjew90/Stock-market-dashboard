# WW 2005-08-17 — GMI declines to +5; only 27% of stocks in an up-trend — the short-term-uptrend breadth statistic

**Source:** [GMI declines to +5;  only 27% of stocks in up-trend; Cramer commits hara-kiri over DKS; a polar bear?](../../raw/posts/2005-08-17-gmi-declines-to-5-only-27-of-stocks-in-up-trend-cramer-commits-hara-kiri-over-dks-a-polar-bear.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2005/08/gmi-declines-to-5-only-27-of-stocks-in-up-trend-cramer-commits-hara-kiri-over-dks-a-polar-bear/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

A breadth statistic he had begun tracking at the end of June — the percentage of his 4,000 stocks in a short-term up-trend — fell to 27% from 61% on 7/29 and 68% at the July 14 peak, below the 48% seen at the July 7 bottom: "the market weakness we are seeing now — near the beginning of a decline — is greater than we saw at the end of the previous decline — something to ponder." Only 24% of the 511 stocks that made new highs ten days earlier closed higher: "buying stocks at new highs 10 days ago has not worked out well."

## Key claims

- % of the universe in a short-term up-trend as a breadth gauge (from June 2005).
- Weakness at the *start* of a decline exceeding that at the *end* of the last one as a warning.
