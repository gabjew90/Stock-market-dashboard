# WW 2006-10-23 — The ultra-ETF case, quantified — in October 2006

**Source:** [GMI: 6; IBD 100 performance; AAPL and GOOG](../../raw/posts/2006-10-23-gmi-6-ibd-100-performance-aapl-and-goog.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2006/10/gmi-6-ibd-100-performance-aapl-and-goog/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

The leveraged-index argument, already measured, nearly three years before the study the wiki had as its earliest. Since August 16 the QQQQ had risen 8.9% and 73% of Nasdaq-100 stocks had advanced (41% by more than 10%, 25% by more than 15%); over the same stretch **QLD rose 16.9%**, SSO 10.9% and the Dow ultra 11.7%. His conclusion: "**by simply buying the QLD when the GMI signaled a rise and holding on during this rise, one could have received a 17% return — a return that beat 84% of the Nasdaq 100 stocks. By buying the Ultra QQQQ ETF, I can beat 84% of the Nasdaq 100 stocks. Why try to find the few individual stocks that outperform the QQQQ, when I can**" simply hold the leveraged index. The 45th day of a QQQQ up-trend, ten weeks above the 10-week average.

## Key claims

- **The ultra-ETF thesis is quantified in October 2006**, not 2009: QLD +16.9% beat 84% of Nasdaq-100 stocks over the same advance.
- The entry condition is already the GMI: "buying the QLD when the GMI signaled a rise and holding on."
- The comparison is made against the *distribution* of component stocks, not an average — the form he uses ever after.
- SSO and the Dow ultra measured alongside, so the choice of the Nasdaq-100 is a comparison, not an assumption.
