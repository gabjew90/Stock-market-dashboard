# WW 2014-09-14 — A weak peak in T2108; the bond math sketched

**Source:** [Market technicals weakening](../../raw/posts/2014-09-14-market-technicals-weakening.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2014/09/market-technicals-weakening/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

**The pendulum failing to reach its usual top is a warning in itself.** "The Worden T2108 Indicator… **has
peaked again and started down**… **T2108 has peaked recently at 65%, indicating the last rise was not even strong
enough to get the T2108 to the more typical 70-80% level.**" That is the mirror of the failed-recovery reading he
uses at lows: an advance that cannot lift breadth into the ordinary overbought zone is an advance without
participation, and the *peak* carries the information rather than the level. He reads it on a weekly T2108 chart,
which smooths the daily noise out of the pendulum. **And the rate mechanism sketched again**: "interest rates on
bonds are moving up, as reflected in the decline in **IEF**, an ETF composed of 7-10 year U.S. treasury bonds.
**Falling bond prices = rising rates.** (Think of it this way. **Current interest rate or yield = constant
pay-out / changing current price.**)" The compact algebraic form of the arithmetic he would work out numerically
three months later.

## Key claims

- **A low peak in T2108 is a warning**: failing to reach the customary 70–80% zone means the advance lacked breadth.
- The *peak* is the observation, not the level — the mirror of the failed-recovery reading at lows.
- T2108 read on a **weekly** chart to strip daily noise from the pendulum.
- **IEF (7–10 year Treasuries) named as the intermediate-rate proxy**, alongside TLT for the long end and SHY for the short.
- "Yield = constant pay-out / changing current price" — the relationship in one line.
