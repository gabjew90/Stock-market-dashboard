# WW 2008-05-05 — The kiss-of-death study

**Source:** [GMI: 4; GMI-R: 8; IBD100 stock list — the kiss of death?](../../raw/posts/2008-05-05-gmi-4-gmi-r-8-ibd100-stock-list-the-kiss-of-death.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2008/05/gmi-4-gmi-r-8-ibd100-stock-list-the-kiss-of-death/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

His most thorough IBD100 audit: twelve months of monthly list snapshots, run to answer the
trader folklore that appearing on the list marks a top. The verdict is two-sided and precise —
the list is a leader-finder, not a portfolio.

## Key claims

- **The method:** "Once each month I create a watch list containing the IBD100 stocks published
  that Monday and periodically examine how the various lists have performed" — his own
  snapshots, immune to IBD's survivorship-biased index (the same critique as the 2007 audit).
- **The sobering half:** "only 32-62% of the stocks on each list closed higher on Friday than...
  the day their respective list was published... **One cannot just buy any of the IBD100 stocks
  and expect a gain over time.**"
- **The vindicating half — new-high concentration:** just 2 Nasdaq 100 stocks and 2% of the S&P
  500 hit a new high Friday, versus **11–16 per IBD100 list**; 19%+ of the January-onward lists
  sat within 5% of their 52-week highs vs 7% of the Nasdaq 100.
- **The year-later returns that kill the folklore:** from the list published 5/14/2007, "28% of
  the stocks... are up 25% or more; an amazing 15% are up from 50%-315% (MTL +315%, POT +185%,
  BIDU +182%, RIMM +160%). Appearing on the IBD100 list was apparently ***not* the kiss of
  death**."
- **The caveat that carries the lesson:** "one must not buy them and forget them. Many of the
  IBD100 stocks will weaken and the trader must remain vigilant" — the 4/21 list underperformed
  because it was loaded with the agricultural/metal/oil names that had just cracked: sector
  concentration is the list's failure mode.
- Context readings: GMI 4, GMI-S 100%, T2108 at 77%, Day 29 of the up-trend, long QLD with calls
  written — "the Nasdaq market is in an uptrend and **it will last until it ends**."
