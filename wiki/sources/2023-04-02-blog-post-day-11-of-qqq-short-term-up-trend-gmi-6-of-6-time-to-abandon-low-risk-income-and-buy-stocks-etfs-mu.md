# WW 2023-04-02 — The rate cycle as a loop — the April 2023 re-entry, mirroring the 2021 exit

## What it covers

**The exit and the re-entry stated as one mechanism, seventeen months apart.** "**I told you in November, 2021
that I was exiting the market because the Fed would raise interest rates and that would suck the \$\$\$ out of
stocks. Now get ready for the reverse. We are closer to a decline in rates and that will eventually cause money
managers and the public to return to stocks again. It really is that simple. The Fed tightens too much and then
slashes rates to get the economy going again.**" The rate thesis is a **loop**, not a one-directional warning —
the same argument that got him out is the one that gets him back in, and he says so while pointing at the same
source: "read the late **Martin Zweig's** classic book." **The re-entry executed across both accounts**: "**In my
university pension I have begun to transfer out of money market funds and back into mutual funds. In my trading
IRA I will begin to buy ETFs and a few growth stocks.**" With the entry rule unchanged by the eighteen-month
absence: "**I only buy stocks that are above their last green line and trading near their ATHs. The great winners
bought by Darvas and O'Neil were stocks going to ATHs.**" Context: the GMI at 6, and the observation that
everyone was hiding in 4%+ money-market accounts — the same yield that made cash comfortable now marking the
top of the rate cycle.

## Key claims

- **The rate thesis is a cycle, not a warning**: tighten too much, then slash — so the exit signal and the re-entry signal are the same mechanism read at opposite phases.
- **Both accounts move together on the re-entry**, pension out of money market and IRA into ETFs and growth stocks.
- **The entry rule survives eighteen months in cash unchanged**: above the last green line, near all-time highs.
- Zweig cited as the source for the rate–equity relationship at both ends of the cycle.
- A 4%+ risk-free yield is read as a late-cycle condition rather than as a reason to stay in cash — the inverse of the 2006 note.
