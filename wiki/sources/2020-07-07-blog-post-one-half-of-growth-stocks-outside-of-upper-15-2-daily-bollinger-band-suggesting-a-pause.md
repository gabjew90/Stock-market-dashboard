# WW 2020-07-07 — Why Bollinger Bands work — the statistics, as he teaches them

**Source:** [Blog post: One half of growth stocks outside of upper 15.2 daily Bollinger Band, suggesting a pause](../../raw/posts/2020-07-07-blog-post-one-half-of-growth-stocks-outside-of-upper-15-2-daily-bollinger-band-suggesting-a-pause.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2020/07/blog-post-one-half-of-growth-stocks-outside-of-upper-15-2-daily-bollinger-band-suggesting-a-pause/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

**The rationale behind every Bollinger reference in the corpus, given as he gives it to students.** "One of the
indicators I teach my university students to track on all their daily charts is Bollinger Bands (**BB, avg=15, 2
sd**). **By definition, 95% of the time a stock's prices should fall within the upper and lower bands.** (This is
introductory statistics and **you do not have to really understand it to use it**.) **Thus when a stock closes
above the upper band or below the lower, prices often revert to the average** (in this case its 15 day moving
average)." Then the two rules that fall out of it: "**I do not want to buy anything outside its upper band unless
it is during a break-out. Rather, I look for bounces off of the lower band to buy.**" And the invitation to
verify: "consider how you might time your buys and sells if you knew that a stock or index typically trades
within the 2 bands. **Check it out on your past trades.**" The settings are confirmed as 15-period with 2
standard deviations, matching the "15.2" shorthand used throughout.

## Key claims

- **The bands are a 95% containment interval** — two standard deviations around a 15-day average — so a close outside one is a statistically unusual event that tends to revert.
- **Two rules follow**: do not buy above the upper band *except on a breakout*; look for bounces off the lower band.
- The breakout exception is what keeps the rule from contradicting the GLB — a genuine breakout is supposed to exit the band.
- "You do not have to really understand it to use it" — the statistics are offered as justification, not as a prerequisite.
- Settings confirmed: 15-period average, 2 standard deviations.
