# WW 2006-02-27 — The rally people love to hate — when sentiment stops being contrary

**Source:** [GMI: +5; The rally people love to hate](../../raw/posts/2006-02-27-gmi-5-the-rally-people-love-to-hate.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2006/02/gmi-5-the-rally-people-love-to-hate/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

He catches his own contrarian instrument behaving backwards, and rather than explain it away he writes the
anomaly down. Four of five indexes were in short-term up-trends near yearly highs, yet "the reluctance of the
pundits to accept the recent up trends… is striking and makes me think that **this is the rally that people love
to hate**." The numbers: Investors Intelligence at 45.3% bullish and 29.5% bearish, against "more than 60%
bullish in early January when this rally began and only about 20% bearish." Then the question the model cannot
answer: "**Do the newsletter writers actually become less bullish as the market rises? I thought sentiment was
typically a contrary indicator with people being most bullish at the top, after a rise.**" His resolution is to
back the technicals: "I just hate to denigrate this market when there appears to be so much agreement that it
must decline — **even in the face of the considerable technical evidence to the contrary**." Also here: the
**WPM** ([glossary](../methodology/glossary.md)) used to isolate the laggard — only the QQQQ below its 30-day average, with 49% of its components
above theirs against 60–68% for the other four indexes — and a MACD "very close to reversing" read as underlying
strength inside a fifteen-day down-trend.

## Key claims

- **Sentiment did not behave as a contrary indicator here, and he says so** — bullishness fell as the market rose, which is the opposite of the model he had been applying.
- When sentiment and price disagree, price wins: the technicals are backed against "so much agreement that it must decline."
- The WPM's purpose demonstrated — five indexes side by side to find which one is lagging, here the Nasdaq-100.
- A MACD approaching a reversal is read as underlying strength while the day count still says down-trend.
- The semi-inverted yield curve and terrorism are named as what the crowd was reacting to — and set aside as unactionable.
