# WW 2008-01-14 — Darvas on bear markets; the blog's origin story; GMI 0 as 2008 opens

**Source:** [GMI:0; GMI-R:0; Nicolas Darvas on staying clear of bear markets](../../raw/posts/2008-01-14-gmi0-gmi-r0-nicolas-darvas-on-staying-clear-of-bear-markets.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2008/01/gmi0-gmi-r0-nicolas-darvas-on-staying-clear-of-bear-markets/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

Written in the second week of 2008 with the GMI at zero for six straight days — the year the
wiki had almost nothing from. Carries the **blog's origin story in his own words**, and a long
excerpt from Darvas's rare 1977 sequel on why he sat out bear markets entirely.

## Key claims

- **Why the blog exists:** "It is because I was angry at how these mental midgets misled the
  public in the 2000-2002 debacle that I began this blog. **I want to educate the little person
  as to how I have protected myself from the major market declines since 1995.**"
- **The January 2008 tape:** 40 of 4,000 stocks at new highs vs 232 at new lows; day 7 of the
  QQQQ down-trend; GMI below 3 for eight days, at zero for six. "I only play when the odds are
  in my favor."
- **Darvas, from *You Can Still Make It In The Market* (1977)**, written after the 1970 and 1974
  bears: exceptional stocks do rise in bear markets, "**but my temperament is such that I would
  rather be safe than sorry. So I keep out in a bear market and leave such exceptional stocks to
  those who don't mind risking their money against the market trend.**"
- **Darvas on buy-and-hold**, the sharpest version of the argument anywhere in the corpus:
  "Presumably they get some **masochistic pleasure from seeing their capital melt** before their
  eyes every two or three years in successive bear cycles... **I prefer to sleep soundly at
  night, even if it means going into cash for long periods.**"
- **The recurring re-entry comfort:** "no one knows when the turn will come and **there is plenty
  of time to jump on a real bull move**."
- On the pundit incentive problem: "I must assume they make no money and have no audience if the
  public stays away during bad markets."
