# WW 2018-03-11 — Green Dot strategy defined

**Source:** [Green Dot strategy defined](../../raw/posts/2018-03-11-green-dot-strategy-defined.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2018/03/green-dot-strategy-defined/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

The full definition of the **green dot** — formula, prerequisites, exit — which the glossary
previously carried only as a one-line reference. More significantly, it states the
**bounce-over-breakout preference in 2018**, five years before the post the wiki had been
citing as the origin of that doctrine.

## Key claims

- **The preference, dated 2018:** "I have learned over the years that I have more success
  trading strong up-trending stocks that are turning up after they have had a small decline
  **than buying break-outs**." And the reason — Proposition 5 in miniature: "if I think a stock
  has bottomed and turned up, **I know exactly where to get out if I am wrong**."
- **The asymmetry restated:** "that is the secret of success in this business — many small
  losses and a few large gains."
- **The formula:** a **10.4.4 daily stochastic**; look for the fast (10.4) line crossing above
  the slow (10.4.4) signal line, "at an oversold stochastics value, **below 50**."
- **The dot's placement is itself information:** co-instructor **David** (McCandlish) wrote a
  TC2000 program that "places a green dot on the chart on the day the cross-over occurs and
  places the dot **at the 5 day low as a likely support level**." He also mirrors the logic into
  a TC2000 column to scan watchlists quickly.
- **Prerequisites — the dot alone is not a signal:** "Green dots can occur in declining stocks
  but many of them will fail." He first confirms a strong up-trend via **RWB daily or
  Yellowband**, and requires the stock to be "above its rising 30 week average (Weinstein Stage
  II)." A pattern of *rising* green dots is preferable but not required.
- **The exit, two triggers:** "I must exit quickly if the stock **closes back below its green
  dot** or **the fast stochastic closes back below its signal line**."
- **Works on indexes too** — QQQ, DIA and SPY green dots "foreshadowing the advances we had."
- **Never one indicator:** "I try not to rely on a single indicator to make a decision. It is
  always best to look at other indicators (MACD, Bollinger Bands, moving averages) for
  confirmation," and plot the stochastic itself "to see the level at which it is crossing."
- Honest worked examples — NKTR and AAPL, "note that some signals did not work out as well as
  others" — plus a counter-example showing green dots failing in a down-trend.
- Teaching context: "It takes 14 weeks to teach my undergraduate students these strategies."
