# WW 2005-04-17 — The first post — Darvas, distance, and 'I want a life, too'

**Source:** [APRIL 17, 2005–Short or in Cash](../../raw/posts/2005-04-17-april-17-2005-short-or-in-cash.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2005/04/april-17-2005-short-or-in-cash/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

The blog's first post, and its opening complaint is the one the whole method answers: "**it is amazing to me
that people I talk with who are in the market and are more than casually interested in trading continue to look
for stocks to buy. We are in a serious decline and I have been mainly short or in cash since early this year.
People refuse to accept what the market is saying — they only want to buy.**" Then the principle, and the story
he uses to make it: "**I have always done best when I ignore the media and just look at my charts.** The great
Nicolas Darvas made 2 million dollars while traveling around the world. **When he camped out in Manhattan, he
lost his objectivity and consistently made losing trades. He had to leave the country to get back on track. You
can't make good judgments if you are glued to CNBC and tipsters. I want a life, too.**" Distance from the market
is presented as a *precondition for judgement* rather than as a lifestyle preference — and Darvas's biography is
the evidence. The macro frame is the four-year cycle and the post-election year, both of which recur through
2005–2006.

## Key claims

- **The blog opens on the observation that people only want to buy** — the behaviour the market gate exists to override.
- **Distance is a precondition for judgement, not a luxury**: Darvas traded well from abroad and badly from Manhattan.
- "I want a life, too" — the method has to fit a person with a full-time job, which shapes everything from the stops to the 3:45 PM habit.
- He was already short or in cash before the blog existed; the record does not begin with a bullish position.
