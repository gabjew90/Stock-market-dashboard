## What it covers

**An execution failure reported in full, with the cost.** "**I confess I did buy some TQQQ at the beginning of
the new QQQ short term up-trend** (green arrow on chart) and posted about it, **but allowed myself to get shaken
out on 1/19** (red arrow) **and did not buy back in. Not buying back in compounded my first mistake. I need to
let my computer trade for me because I let myself be shaken out even though my objective rules said that the
short term up-trend was still intact** :-(." Three separate admissions in one sentence — he exited against his
own rules, he failed to re-enter, and he identifies the second as the larger error. The remedy he proposes is
automation ("I need to let my computer trade for me"), which is the same conclusion the
[resting buy-stop](../methodology/risk-and-cash.md) reaches from the other direction: the machinery exists
because the discipline is unreliable. The cost is quantified by what he missed: "**TQQQ even beat all but 4 of
the 668 stocks in my aggregate watchlist** of recent IBD50 and MarketSmith Growth 250 stocks."

## Key claims

- **He exited a position his own rules said to hold**, and names failing to re-enter as the larger of the two mistakes.
- **"I need to let my computer trade for me"** — automation proposed as the remedy for his own unreliability, not as an optimisation.
- The forgone gain is measured: TQQQ beat all but 4 of 668 watchlist stocks over the period.
- Published with the entry and exit marked on the chart, so the failure is auditable rather than described.
- Being shaken out is a recurring theme he treats as a design problem — see the weekly chart and the mental stop.
