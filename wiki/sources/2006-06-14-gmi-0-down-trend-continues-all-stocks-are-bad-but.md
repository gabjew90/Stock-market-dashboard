# WW 2006-06-14 — Value is a myth — dated to 2006, and attributed to O'Neil

**Source:** [GMI: 0; Down-trend continues; All stocks are bad, but](../../raw/posts/2006-06-14-gmi-0-down-trend-continues-all-stocks-are-bad-but.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2006/06/gmi-0-down-trend-continues-all-stocks-are-bad-but/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

The "value is a myth" doctrine, nineteen years before the 2025 statement the wiki dates it to — and here it is
attributed. "By now, everyone should see through the widely proclaimed conventional nonsense that stocks have
intrinsic value. **Stocks are only worth what someone else will pay for them. And each person's assessment is
driven more by mob psychology and greed and fear, than by rational logic.**" The mechanism is then applied to
the leader tell: "How else can you explain why a stock like **GS can release great earnings and be punished** like
it was on Tuesday. **In a down-trend, all events are seen through a dark glass that is perceived to be half
empty. Everyone sells quickly because they think that the next guy will sell.**" That is the leader-on-good-news
diagnostic explained rather than merely observed — the news is constant, the interpretation is regime-dependent.
The famous line is O'Neil's: "**As William O'Neil says, all stocks are bad unless they are going up.** But I say
that '**bad**' stocks can make a trader a lot of money if s/he shorts them correctly." Calibration that day: 13
new highs against 292 new lows; only 15% of his universe above their 10-week averages, "**a new low since I began
recording this statistic in 2005**"; a TC2005 scan returning **232 submarines and only 2 rockets**; and a
quantified distance before he would change his mind — "**the Dow could rally some 240 points before I would
consider that a new up-trend had begun.**" The IBD 100 again worse than the universe on the day, but with twice
as many still in a Stage 2 up-trend (53% vs 26%).

## Key claims

- **"Stocks are only worth what someone else will pay for them" is a 2006 position, not a late one** — and the line "all stocks are bad unless they are going up" is O'Neil's, quoted, not his own coinage.
- **Why leaders get punished on good news, mechanically**: in a down-trend every event is read pessimistically and everyone sells early because they expect the next holder to.
- The short side as the corollary: "'bad' stocks can make a trader a lot of money if s/he shorts them correctly."
- **A re-entry distance stated in points** — the Dow needed ~240 points before he would call a new up-trend.
- Extreme-low calibration: 15% above the 10-week average, a new low for the series since 2005; 232 submarines to 2 rockets.
- The growth-list split again: worse than the universe day to day, better on long-term trend persistence (53% vs 26% in Stage 2).
