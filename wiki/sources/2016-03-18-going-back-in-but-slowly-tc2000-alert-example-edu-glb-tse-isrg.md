# WW 2016-03-18 — Going back in, slowly — T2108 at 3.8%, and an honest confession about single digits

**Source:** [Going back in, but slowly; TC2000 alert example: $EDU- GLB; $TSE, $ISRG](../../raw/posts/2016-03-18-going-back-in-but-slowly-tc2000-alert-example-edu-glb-tse-isrg.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2016/03/going-back-in-but-slowly-tc2000-alert-example-edu-glb-tse-isrg/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

The January 2016 bottom read after the fact: "**The Worden T2108 indicator again called the bottom, in late January, with an extremely oversold reading of 3.8%, a low level only surpassed by the bottoms in 1987 and 2008.**" Then the admission that qualifies his own rule: "Again, I promise that the next time the T2108 goes into single digits I will buy an index ETF. **But I know I will be too scared.** Whatever causes most people to dump stocks requires a new fear" — the contrarian rule is easy to state and, by his own account, hard to execute. Meanwhile the pension moves: "for the first time in months, I moved some of my most conservative university pension money back into a mutual fund from a money market fund" with the GMI at 6 and all major indexes back above their 30-week — "I will re-enter the market slowly as long as the upward trend continues. If it fails, I will exit again." And on why bottoms feel impossible: "the market is always looking forward and we humans are always looking backward."

## Key claims

- T2108 at **3.8%** in late January 2016 — surpassed only by the 1987 and 2008 bottoms.
- "I promise that the next time the T2108 goes into single digits I will buy an index ETF. But I know I will be too scared."
- Pension re-entry is staged and reversible: slowly while the trend holds, out again if it fails.
- "The market is always looking forward and we humans are always looking backward."
