# WW 2009-06-07 — The ultra-ETF study off the March 2009 bottom

**Source:** [Why search for individual stocks when we can ride the ultra ETFs?](../../raw/posts/2009-06-07-why-search-for-individual-stocks-when-we-can-ride-the-ultra-etfs.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2009/06/why-search-for-individual-stocks-when-we-can-ride-the-ultra-etfs/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

The **earliest** of his leveraged-ETF-vs-stock-picking studies (the wiki had 2011 and 2013
versions), run off the March 2009 bottom, and framed as a finding that surprised even him. Also
documents the staged pension re-entry beginning, with the never-average-down rule applied at
the account level.

## Key claims

- **The numbers:** March bottom → early June: QQQQ +42.9%, **QLD +99.2%, TYH +179.4%**. Only
  **16% of Nasdaq 100 stocks (and 23% of S&P 500) rose 80%+**; the top five individual Nasdaq
  performers ranged +138.8% to +180.6% — i.e. the 3X ETF matched the *best stock in the index*.
  "The results blew me away."
- **The framing that recurs for a decade:** "the choice before us is to search for the **needle
  in the haystack** individual stock... or to buy one of these 2X or 3X ultra long ETF's and
  ride a basket of stocks with a lot more diversification and probably less risk."
- **The precondition, stated with it:** "The key is to **discern the trend accurately** and to
  then ride the ultra ETF with the most potential for following that trend." The leverage
  argument is downstream of the timing argument.
- Also a rebuttal of the era's claim that leveraged ETFs "fail to achieve their goals" — true in
  chop, not in a sustained trend.
- **The staged re-entry, live:** June 2009, breadth improving (54 new highs in a day, the most
  since September; 75% of Nasdaq 100 above their MACD signal lines) → "I am beginning to
  reinvest my university pension money in the growth mutual fund... **The key is to go in slowly
  and only add more money if the ETF climbs above my original purchase price. I *never* average
  down.**" The pilot-buy/pyramid discipline applied to accounts, not just stocks.
