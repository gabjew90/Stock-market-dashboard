# WW 2015-07-12 — Buying on GMI Sell signals would have worked — and why he doesn't mind

**Source:** [Schizoid Mr. Market](../../raw/posts/2015-07-12-schizoid-mr-market.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2015/07/schizoid-mr-market/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

Five months after publishing the GMI's whipsaw limitation, he restates it on a chart and in blunter terms. The
red arrows on his weekly QQQ chart mark the GMI Sell signals, and "[they] indicate that **during this bull
market one would have done well by buying stocks when the GMI issued a Sell**." That is the limitation stated as
its inversion — not merely that the Sells were premature, but that fading them would have paid. What he does
about it is the two-part answer the system already contains: "Over a long multi-year period **the GMI has kept
me out of bear markets. I don't mind exiting with a GMI Sell and going back in if the market rally resumes.
This is a benefit of trading in a tax deferred IRA where I can go in and out without any tax consequences.**"
The whipsaw is affordable because of *where* the trading happens — the account structure is what makes the
signal's error rate tolerable, not any property of the signal. Technical context: the QQQ closing back above its
30-week average after dipping below during the week, "the 4th time that the QQQ has found support at this
average since 2014"; the weekly GMMA still RWB but with price below all the shorter red averages; a stochastic
recovery from below 20 and two bounces off the lower Bollinger Band, qualified by "it is a negative, however, to
see the lower BB in a steep decline"; and a rally to 109 that "would paint a very nice symmetrical triple top."
J.P. Morgan supplies the title's thesis — asked what the market would do, he said it will fluctuate.

## Key claims

- **"One would have done well by buying stocks when the GMI issued a Sell"** during this bull market — the 2015 limitation restated as an inversion, and more damaging in that form.
- **The whipsaw is tolerable because the trading is tax-deferred.** The account structure, not the indicator, is what makes a false signal cheap — which is why the same rule would be poor advice in a taxable account.
- The defence of the signal is its tail behaviour, not its hit rate: "over a long multi-year period the GMI has kept me out of bear markets."
- Support at the 30-week average counted as a repeated event — the fourth test since 2014.
- A bounce off the lower Bollinger Band is discounted when the band itself is falling steeply.
- Price below all six shorter GMMA averages while the weekly RWB is still intact — weakness inside an up-trend, not a reversal.
