# WW 2017-07-04 — The GMI's QQQ bias demonstrated live; biotech position sizing

**Source:** [Schizoid market; GMI  neutral; Mainly in cash; $PIRS recent GLB + yellowband](../../raw/posts/2017-07-04-schizoid-market-gmi-neutral-mainly-in-cash-pirs-recent-glb-yellowband.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2017/07/schizoid-market-gmi-neutral-mainly-in-cash-pirs-recent-glb-yellowband/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

**The GMI's known bias demonstrated live, and named in the same sentence.** T2108 "climbed to its highest level
since April, while the QQQ continued to break support" — 58% of all US stocks rose while "only 27% of the Nasdaq
100 stocks advanced." His comment: "**My GMI (General Market Index), which is heavily weighted toward the QQQ**,
rose one and just avoided a Red signal." That is the 2008 QQQ-centricity blind spot happening in real time, with
the broad-market gauge and the index gauge pointing opposite ways. **Position and sizing**: "I have retreated in
my *trading* account mainly to cash and am a little short the QQQ with put options. **The only stocks I may
nibble at are biotechs, which can explode upward on good news**… **Biotechs are very speculative and I only take
very small initial positions in them.**" PIRS is the example — a recent IPO with "a recent high volume GLB… near
its all-time high," plus "a nice weekly yellowband rocket pattern" — and the hold rule is the *close* version:
"**I will hold some PIRS as long as it *closes* above its green line (\$4.40), which is currently near its 10
week average.**" He also bends his own price-level preference for it: "**I don't like cheap stocks, but someone
is buying PIRS.**" Two borrowed frames: "many of my market gurus have written that **they shoot the leaders
first, and then the troops**," and "as **Martin Zweig** rigorously showed in his book, **rising interest rates by
the Fed eventually slay the bull**." Against the bearish read, one seasonal counter: "the market often falls
during the **post quarterly earnings release lull**, only to come roaring back when the next good earnings
appear."

## Key claims

- **"My GMI… is heavily weighted toward the QQQ"** — the blind spot stated as a live caveat while the index and broad-market breadth diverged.
- **Biotechs get an explicit sizing exception**: eligible when nothing else is, because they can gap up on news, and therefore "only very small initial positions."
- **The GLB hold rule in its close-based form (2017)** — "as long as it *closes* above its green line" — against the intraday "no exceptions" version of 2016.
- The green line sitting near the 10-week average is noted as a convergence of two exit levels.
- The price-level preference is a preference, not a rule: "I don't like cheap stocks, but someone is buying PIRS."
- Zweig's finding invoked for the rates thesis; "they shoot the leaders first" attributed to "my market gurus" rather than claimed.
