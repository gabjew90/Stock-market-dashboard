# WW 2005-12-29 — My apology; the short-term down-trend defined — the 30-day close rule in 2005

**Source:** [My apology; GMI: +2; Short term down trend defined; Why fight the odds?](../../raw/posts/2005-12-29-my-apology-gmi-2-short-term-down-trend-defined-why-fight-the-odds.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2005/12/my-apology-gmi-2-short-term-down-trend-defined-why-fight-the-odds/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

The rule that later becomes his '30-day close rule at full strength', stated at the end of 2005: "**when the indexes close below their 30 day averages, I tend to get in cash or go short.**" The reading that week: GMI +2; 71 new highs; only 37% of the stocks that made new highs ten days earlier closed higher; QQQQ, SPY and DIA all below their 30-day; 32% of QQQQ stocks in a short-term up-trend; **only 6% of the doublers hit a new high — "further evidence that the leaders are weak"**; the longer-term up-trend still intact (61% above their 10-week). Also a second weakness sign: the QQQQ's 10-day average rolling over.

## Key claims

- "When the indexes close below their 30 day averages, I tend to get in cash or go short" (2005).
- The doubler indicator used as a leader-health read: 6% at a new high = leaders weak.
- Short-term and long-term trends can disagree; the 30-day governs the trading stance.
