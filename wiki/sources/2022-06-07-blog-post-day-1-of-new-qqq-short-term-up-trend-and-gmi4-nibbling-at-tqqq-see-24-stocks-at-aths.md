# WW 2022-06-07 — 1990–2021: 23% of QQQ up-trends last 5 days or less

**Source:** [Blog Post: Day 1 of new $QQQ short term up-trend and GMI=4; Nibbling at $TQQQ, see 24 stocks at ATHs](../../raw/posts/2022-06-07-blog-post-day-1-of-new-qqq-short-term-up-trend-and-gmi4-nibbling-at-tqqq-see-24-stocks-at-aths.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2022/06/blog-post-day-1-of-new-qqq-short-term-up-trend-and-gmi4-nibbling-at-tqqq-see-24-stocks-at-aths/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

The most precise duration statistic in the corpus, with its study period stated: "I do not trust a change in the QQQ short term trend count until it reaches the fifth day. **Our analyses from 1990-2021 showed that 23% of QQQ short term up-trends lasted 5 days or less, or 77% lasted longer. After 5 days these short term trends tend to last.**" Note "our" — the work was done with students or a collaborator, and the window (1990–2021) is far longer than the 2006-onward tabulations published earlier. The trade that follows: "for now, **I buy a little TQQQ and will slowly accumulate more on the way up. If QQQ falters I will quickly sell.**" On the consensus: "most people are betting that the Fed will overly tighten and bring on a recession and the market will go lower. **But Mr. Market tends to fool most of the people.**" And 24 watchlist stocks at an ATH, ranked by gain over 250 days — AMR at 7.73× its year-ago price, eight already doubled.

## Key claims

- **1990–2021: 23% of QQQ short-term up-trends lasted ≤ 5 days; 77% lasted longer** — the quantitative basis for the Day-5 rule, over a 31-year window.
- "After 5 days these short term trends tend to last."
- Attributed as "our analyses" — collaborative work, not a solo count.
- Day-1 tranche then accumulate on the way up; sell quickly on a falter.
- "Mr. Market tends to fool most of the people."
