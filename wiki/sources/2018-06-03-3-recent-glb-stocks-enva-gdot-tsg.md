# WW 2018-06-03 — Three recent GLB stocks — 'stocks that have doubled often go on to double again'

**Source:** [3 Recent GLB stocks: $ENVA $GDOT $TSG](../../raw/posts/2018-06-03-3-recent-glb-stocks-enva-gdot-tsg.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2018/06/3-recent-glb-stocks-enva-gdot-tsg/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

The GLB restated compactly with two additions. The definition: "I draw a green line at the peak price not surpassed for at least 3 months and wait for a close above the green line." The filter: "all three have doubled over the past year, another important sign I look for. **Stocks that have doubled often go on to double again.**" The entry menu: "I like to buy such stocks on the day of the break-out **or afterwards when they consolidate or bounce off of support or give a green dot signal**" — the GLB and the bounce entries as one workflow rather than alternatives. And the re-entry doctrine, stated as a source of his best trades: "I never hold a GLB stock that closes back below the green line but will buy it back if it retakes it. **Some of my best gains have come from stocks that have shaken me out for a small loss and then produced another buy signal.**" Above-average volume on the breakout day preferred; the three had re-tested their green lines and held.

## Key claims

- "Stocks that have doubled often go on to double again" — the doubling criterion as a forward filter, not just a screen.
- Entry options in one sentence: the breakout day, or a later consolidation, support bounce, or green dot.
- "Some of my best gains have come from stocks that have shaken me out for a small loss and then produced another buy signal."
- A successful re-test of the green line is a positive, not a warning.
