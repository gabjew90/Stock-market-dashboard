# WW 2007-05-22 — Covered calls as the boomer strategy; QLD over stock-picking

**Source:** [GMI: 6; No longer post daily—but when GMI changes; the ideal boomer strategy—writing covered calls](../../raw/posts/2007-05-22-gmi-6-no-longer-post-daily-but-when-gmi-changes-the-ideal-boomer-strategy-writing-covered-calls.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2007/05/gmi-6-no-longer-post-daily-but-when-gmi-changes-the-ideal-boomer-strategy-writing-covered-calls/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

Two durable methodological positions stated early: the **covered-call income strategy** (later revisited on GLD in 2014) and the **leveraged-ETF-over-stock-picking** argument (later restated with TQQQ in 2011 and 2013). Also documents a deliberate change in blogging cadence justified on signal-to-noise grounds.

## Key claims

- **Sampling frequency as a methodological choice:** "It is not necessary and is, perhaps, detrimental to take the pulse of the market too often." The medical analogy — blood pressure measured minute by minute would produce wrong decisions. He switches to posting when the GMI *changes* rather than daily.
- **The market-trend odds, quantified:** with GMI at 6 since April 3, the Nasdaq 100 rose 6.5% and 69% of its components advanced. "By focusing on the GMI, I could have had a 69% chance of picking a winning stock." And the general figure: "Roughly 70-80% of stocks follow the relevant market averages."
- **QLD beat 76% of Nasdaq 100 stocks** over the same stretch (+12.3%) — the earliest version in the corpus of the argument he repeats with TQQQ in [2011](2011-01-04-2010-etf-performance-why-search-for-individual-stocks-when-one-can-just-ride-the-leveraged-etfs.md) and [2013](2013-11-24-gmi-based-strategy-using-3x-etfs-beats-ibd-50-stocks.md): "I do not have to hope that I have picked a winning individual stock."
- **The covered-call method, with numbers:** one-month calls written on up-trending stocks he already owns, spread across many names to limit single-stock risk; returns of **2–4% per month** versus 0.4% monthly in a money-market fund; most positions called away at expiration, at which point he reassesses the trend and re-selects. The rationale is the house's: "If it is true that 70-90% of all options expire worthless each month, why not behave like the casino?"
- Framed as age-appropriate risk reduction: "Because I am a boomer and have acquired a sizable trading capital in my IRA, I have become more conservative... I am now reluctant to pile into a break out stock."
- Honest caveat: "Like any trading strategy, this not the Holy Grail, and requires study and on the job training."
