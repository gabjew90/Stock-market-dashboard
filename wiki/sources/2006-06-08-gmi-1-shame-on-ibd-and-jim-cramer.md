# WW 2006-06-08 — Shame on IBD and Cramer; puts in an IRA; the IBD 100 audited mid-decline

**Source:** [GMI: +1; Shame on IBD and Jim Cramer!](../../raw/posts/2006-06-08-gmi-1-shame-on-ibd-and-jim-cramer.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2006/06/gmi-1-shame-on-ibd-and-jim-cramer/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

A polemic that carries three durable claims. **The short-side argument**, in the form he would repeat four
days later: "People short change themselves… to only trade on the bull side of the market. **Buying puts in an
IRA (or taxable account) is one way to take advantage of the market's decline while limiting potential
losses. We have all been brainwashed into avoiding the short side of the market.**" He aims the same charge at
Jim Cramer, "who used to short the market while running his hedge fund, but refuses to advocate that his CNBC
audience short stocks," and states the alternative as odds: "**Why not take the easy way and trade the 80%+ of
stocks that go with the major market trend?**" **An IBD 100 audit taken in real time**: since the list was
published on 5/15, "only 14% of these stocks have risen. Thus, **86% of the IBD 100 stocks have declined or
gone nowhere**, along with the market" — and that day the list underperformed his own universe on every
measure he tracks (30% vs 38% advancing; 16% vs 32% above the 10-day; 18% vs 26% above the 30-day). **And a
scoring of IBD's market call**: after weeks of calling a rally, IBD conceded the market "is in a downtrend,
which has been the case for nearly a month" — "IBD is a great newspaper, but they should have the integrity to
admit that they have been repeatedly wrong… the GMI and my indicators have been calling the down trend for 19
straight days."

## Key claims

- "**Trade the 80%+ of stocks that go with the major market trend**" — his stated figure has risen from the ~70% he used through 2005.
- Puts in an IRA are the retail route to the short side: participation in a decline with the loss capped at the premium.
- A growth-stock list is only as good as the market it is published into: 86% of an IBD 100 list went nowhere or fell in the three weeks after publication.
- He scores his own indicators against IBD's published call and dates the divergence precisely (19 days).
- "The market has been in a significant decline where it has been relatively easy to make money, as long as one stayed with the short trend."
