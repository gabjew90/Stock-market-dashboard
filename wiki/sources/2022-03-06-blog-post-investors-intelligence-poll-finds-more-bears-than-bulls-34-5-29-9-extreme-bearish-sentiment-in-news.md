# WW 2022-03-06 — Bears outnumber bulls, but T2108 says no bottom; the ATH scan's relative-strength condition

**Source:** [Blog post: Investors Intelligence poll finds more bears than bulls (34.5/29.9%) =extreme bearish sentiment in newsletters, typically found at bottoms; 18 stocks from my ATH scan; GMI=Red.](../../raw/posts/2022-03-06-blog-post-investors-intelligence-poll-finds-more-bears-than-bulls-34-5-29-9-extreme-bearish-sentiment-in-news.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2022/03/blog-post-investors-intelligence-poll-finds-more-bears-than-bulls-34-5-29-9-extreme-bearish-sentiment-in-newsletters-typically-found-at-bottoms-18-stocks-from-my-ath-scan-gmired/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

Contrarian sentiment against breadth: more bearish than bullish newsletters is "a very rare event [that] suggests that at least a bounce may be coming. **But T2108, at 37%, is far above the level where bottoms occur. And the p/c ratio, in the .80s, is not extreme.**" So: cash, and watch. On what to buy when it turns: "growth stocks are underperforming now and few of the fallen leaders will retake their ATHs. **We need to stop looking for bargains.** The leaders will show up on the new 52 week high list, preferably reaching all-time-highs." A component of his ATH scan is disclosed — "one component of my scan requires the stock to have a weekly RS (SPY) at a 20 week high" — and 13 of the 15 stocks it produced the prior week *rose* during a declining market. Sector clustering (agriculture, fertilizer, metals, defense; six oil and gas, five agriculture/food of eighteen) is read as information, as is running the same scan on industry indexes.

## Key claims

- Sentiment extremes are overruled by breadth: II bears > bulls, but T2108 37% and p/c .80s = no bottom.
- "We need to stop looking for bargains" — leaders come from the new-high list, not the fallen.
- The ATH scan requires **weekly relative strength vs SPY at a 20-week high**.
- Sector clustering among ATH hits, and running the scan on industry indexes, as second-order reads.
