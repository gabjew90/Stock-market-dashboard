# WW 2011-06-20 — No one knows when this market will bottom

**Source:** [No one knows when this market will bottom](../../raw/posts/2011-06-20-no-one-knows-when-this-market-will-bottom.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2011/06/no-one-knows-when-this-market-will-bottom/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

"Once a decline begins, no one, including all of the media pundits, can accurately predict the bottom." The market-timing case in his own terms: the missing-best-days studies "fail to note that one did the best if s/he was out of the market during the days with the worst declines"; and "if I get out of a mutual fund at say, 80, and it falls to 60, as long as I get back in before it goes back above 80, I have managed the risk and done well." He had not exited the pension funds "because of their unacceptable rules against market timing." The trading account doubled from the September 2010 re-entry (GMI 5 on September 7 and "a long stream of 5's and 6's") and was still up ~70% — "think how well I could do if I only stayed in the market when the GMI was strong!" D-19 against a 29-day decline in June 2010 and 69 days in December 2008. A weekly 10.4.4 stochastic of the QQQ near 20 as a relative-oversold gauge — approaching prior bottoms, but "other big declines ended *below* the 20% level."

## Key claims

- The best-days rebuttal, 2011 form; the 80 → 60 → re-enter-below-80 arithmetic.
- "Almost all of my indicators are designed to discern the current trend of the market. Once I know that, the correct strategy becomes clear to me."
- Weekly 10.4.4 stochastic on the index as a relative-oversold gauge across declines.
- Duration base rates: 29 days (June 2010), 69 days (December 2008).
