## What it covers

Two mechanics explained. First, the Stage 4 trigger in arithmetic: "a declining 30 week moving average occurs when the latest week's price being added to the average is less than the 31st week being dropped. In other words, the current week's closing price is less than its closing price 31 weeks ago, a serious sign of weakness." Not the case then — the 30-weeks were still rising. Second, why the indexes looked better than the market: SPY and QQQ are capitalization-weighted, so "the few huge cap stocks like AAPL, MSFT etc. account for most of the movement in the index," while the price-weighted DIA was *not* at all-time highs — "the strength in the few very popular huge cap stocks are causing SPY and QQQ to not reflect the decline in most of their component stocks." Written a week before his November 2021 exit on breadth divergence.

## Key claims

- A declining 30-week average = this week's close below the close 31 weeks ago — the Stage 4 trigger stated as arithmetic.
- Cap-weighted SPY/QQQ vs price-weighted DIA as a breadth cross-check.
- Mega-cap concentration as the mechanism that hides internal decline.
