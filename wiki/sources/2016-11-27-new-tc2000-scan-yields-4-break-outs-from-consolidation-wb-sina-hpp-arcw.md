# WW 2016-11-27 — The weekly consolidation-breakout scan; short interest as breakout fuel

**Source:** [New TC2000 Scan yields 4 break-outs from consolidation: $WB, $SINA, $HPP, $ARCW](../../raw/posts/2016-11-27-new-tc2000-scan-yields-4-break-outs-from-consolidation-wb-sina-hpp-arcw.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2016/11/new-tc2000-scan-yields-4-break-outs-from-consolidation-wb-sina-hpp-arcw/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

A fully-specified weekly-chart scan the wiki's scan catalogue lacked, and — via the annotated WB
chart — his chart-header data legend, including a concept absent from the wiki entirely: **short
interest read as buying-pressure fuel for a breakout.**

## Key claims

- **The scan, criteria explicit:** growth stocks emerging from a **multi-week consolidation** on
  a weekly chart, requiring a minimum weekly volume, **above-average volume on the breakout
  week**, and latest quarterly EPS **≥ +50%**. Selectivity: **4 of ~4,900** US stocks — in a
  strong tape, precisely because "many stocks have already broken out."
- **Short interest as fuel:** WB's short interest ratio of 3.6 — "it would take about 3.6 days
  to cover all of the shares speculators have sold short... **The higher the number, the greater
  the buying pressure from a break-out.**" Shorts are read as *future forced buyers*, making the
  ratio a breakout amplifier, not a bearish datum.
- **The doubled-in-a-year check on the header:** WB at 2.64× its price 250 days ago — "Stocks,
  like people, tend to repeat their past behavior."
- **Earnings-date on the chart** (new in TC2000 v16) — the earnings-risk check built into the
  header rather than looked up separately.
- **Volume qualifies the signal:** WB's breakout week was its highest weekly volume since
  September 2014 — "It could signify the resumption of the up-trend **or it could mean
  nothing**," the honest epistemic tag he attaches even to his own scan hits.
- The weekly-chart rationale restated: weeklies "more clearly show me the trend and are less
  likely to make me exit a strong stock too soon."
