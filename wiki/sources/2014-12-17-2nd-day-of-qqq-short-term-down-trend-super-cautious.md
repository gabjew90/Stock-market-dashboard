# WW 2014-12-17 — The duration rule restated; the pension constraint biting

**Source:** [2nd day of $QQQ short term down-trend; super cautious](../../raw/posts/2014-12-17-2nd-day-of-qqq-short-term-down-trend-super-cautious.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2014/12/2nd-day-of-qqq-short-term-down-trend-super-cautious/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

**The Day-5 rule restated with the same caveat, nine months after the study**: "Since 2006, about **40% of
short term down-trends (the way I define them) have lasted 5 days or less. Once one passes 5 days, it often goes
a lot further.**" The second sentence is the operational half and is often dropped — the rule is not only "wait 5
days," it is "past 5 days, expect more." Position: "I own mainly **SQQQ** and **will accumulate more if this
decline lasts 5 days**." **The pension constraint biting, in one line**: "**If I could frequently trade my mutual
funds in my university pension, I would have transferred some money to money market funds**" — the administrative
limit, not the signal, is what kept the pension invested. He dates his experience at "**45+ years of trading**"
here (2014). The bearish case is demographic and calendar rather than technical: five years of gains having
taught people the market only rises, selling deferred into a new tax year, "**all of the retiring boomers who
will panic from the fear of their imminently needed retirement savings diminishing**," and "**I just remember a
lot of significant market tops occurring in January**." And a caution about scale on the monthly SPY: the decline
"hardly registers. **This could mean that the current decline will be tiny *or that it is yet to begin*.**" 

## Key claims

- **"Once one passes 5 days, it often goes a lot further"** — the other half of the Day-5 rule, and the reason it is an accumulation trigger rather than merely a filter.
- **The pension stayed invested because of transfer limits, not conviction** — the clearest single-sentence statement of the administrative constraint.
- A monthly chart cuts both ways: a decline that barely registers may be trivial *or* may not have started.
- The bear case here is demographic and seasonal, and he presents it as intuition ("I just remember…") rather than as measurement.
- "45+ years of trading experience" as of December 2014 — consistent with the 40-year figure used from 2005.
