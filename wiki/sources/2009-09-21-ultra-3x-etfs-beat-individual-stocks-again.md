# WW 2009-09-21 — The ultra-ETF case stated as probabilities of beating it

**Source:** [Ultra 3X ETF’s beat individual stocks again!](../../raw/posts/2009-09-21-ultra-3x-etfs-beat-individual-stocks-again.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2009/09/ultra-3x-etfs-beat-individual-stocks-again/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

**The clearest framing of the leveraged-index argument: not returns, but your odds of beating it.** Over a
bounce beginning 1 September, QQQQ +8%, QLD +17%, TYH +21%. Then the part that matters: "**If I had been trying
to pick the specific NASDAQ 100 stock that would outperform these ETFs, I would only have had a little better
than even chance (57%) of beating the QQQQ. But only 13% of the NASDAQ 100 stocks beat the QLD and 11% beat the
TYH.**" So stock-picking against the *unleveraged* index is close to a coin flip, and against the 2× and 3×
funds it is a 1-in-8 proposition. "**Why try to find the few stocks that can beat these ETFs when the odds are so
low?**" The measurement form is the one he has used since 2006 — score the instrument against the *distribution*
of the components rather than their average — but converting it into a hit rate is what makes the conclusion
unavoidable: the leverage does not need to be right about anything, it just needs the index to trend.

## Key claims

- **Stock-picking against QQQQ was 57% — near a coin flip.** Against QLD it was 13%, against TYH 11%.
- **The argument is about odds, not returns** — which is what makes it robust to any single period's numbers.
- The leveraged fund requires no security selection at all; it only requires the index to trend.
- Same comparison form as every ultra-ETF study here, restated as a probability of beating the instrument.
