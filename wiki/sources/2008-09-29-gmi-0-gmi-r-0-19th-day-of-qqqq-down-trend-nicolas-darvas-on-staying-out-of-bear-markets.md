# WW 2008-09-29 — Darvas's 1977 book on staying out of bear markets

**Source:** [GMI: 0; GMI-R: 0; 19th day of QQQQ down-trend; Nicolas Darvas on staying out of bear markets](../../raw/posts/2008-09-29-gmi-0-gmi-r-0-19th-day-of-qqqq-down-trend-nicolas-darvas-on-staying-out-of-bear-markets.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2008/09/gmi-0-gmi-r-0-19th-day-of-qqqq-down-trend-nicolas-darvas-on-staying-out-of-bear-markets/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

Written on day 19 of the 2008 collapse, with the GMI at zero, and it reaches past the famous book to the late
one. "A lot of people think highly of **Nicolas Darvas, who made a fortune in about two years of trading in the
1950's**… **In his later book written in 1977, Darvas wrote**" — quoting him on the necessity of standing aside
in declining markets. The 1977 book is the "little known and rare final book" he points readers to elsewhere in
the corpus, and citing it here is characteristic: the argument for cash is not his own invention but a
transmission from someone who had already made the money and then wrote about what he could not do. Market
context: 5 new highs against 189 new lows, T2108 at 22%. The post belongs with the other bear-market entries in
which he is doing nothing and explaining why doing nothing is the position — the Darvas quotation is the
authority he leans on when the temptation is at its strongest.

## Key claims

- **Darvas's rare 1977 book quoted on staying out of declining markets** — the later work, not the famous 1960 one.
- The case for cash is presented as inherited rather than invented, from someone who had already made the money.
- Written at the point of maximum temptation — day 19 of the 2008 decline, T2108 at 22%, 189 new lows to 5 highs.
- Consistent with his habit of citing the sources of his rules rather than presenting them as his own.
