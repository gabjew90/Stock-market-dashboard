# WW 2009-12-27 — The naive participant, at both poles

**Source:** [My post from the bear market bottom; weak TJX; GMMA shows QQQQ in strong up-trend](../../raw/posts/2009-12-27-my-post-from-the-bear-market-bottom-weak-tjx-gmma-shows-qqqq-in-strong-up-trend.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2009/12/my-post-from-the-bear-market-bottom-weak-tjx-gmma-shows-qqqq-in-strong-up-trend/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

A retrospective that quotes his own bottom-day post and produces the **naive-participant tell at both poles**.
From 8 March 2009, with the GMI at 0 and T2108 at 7%: "**Last week, a person who knows nothing about the market
asked me how to short stocks.** This is reminiscent of the stories of the shoeshine boys providing stock tips,
near the roaring 20's market's top. The sentiment is just too negative right now. **Does this mean the market has
to turn up? Not necessarily, but the market is always an assessment of competing probabilities.**" Nine months
later he scores it: "**when people around me who never traded asked me how to short stocks it turned out to be a
key contrary signal that the market decline was near an end.** Similarly, I remember when a friend who knows
little about the market **asked me if he should refinance his house to invest in the market — back in 2000 near
that market's top**." Same instrument, both directions: the inexperienced reaching for leverage marks a top, the
inexperienced reaching for shorts marks a bottom. Also here: **analyst promotion read as distribution** — with
pundits recommending TJX while its daily GMMA showed short-term averages below long-term ones, "**you do know
that analysts sometimes tout a stock so that their big clients can unload their long positions to the
unsuspecting public**" — and a T2108 caution at 76%, "we could be in for a short term decline if this indicator
goes much above 80%." He held QLD with the QQQQ weekly in a strong up-trend.

## Key claims

- **The naive-participant tell works at both ends**: an outsider asking how to short marks a bottom; an outsider proposing to remortgage to buy marks a top (2000).
- **"The market is always an assessment of competing probabilities"** — the epistemic frame under every contrarian read: a signal shifts odds, it does not compel an outcome.
- A single anecdote is treated as evidence *because* it is combined with a screaming quantitative extreme (T2108 at 7%), not instead of one.
- **Analyst recommendations read as distribution** — touting that lets large holders sell to the public — with the technicals used to check the story.
- T2108 at 76% flagged as approaching the level where a short-term decline becomes likely ("much above 80%").
- This GMMA variant is drawn with *black* short-term and *red* long-term lines, a notation different from the usual red/blue.
