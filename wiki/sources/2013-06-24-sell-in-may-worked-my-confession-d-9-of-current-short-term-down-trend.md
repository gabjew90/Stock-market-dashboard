# WW 2013-06-24 — Sell in May worked; my confession — a trend-count criterion tried and reverted

**Source:** [Sell in May worked?  My confession; D-9 of current short term down-trend](../../raw/posts/2013-06-24-sell-in-may-worked-my-confession-d-9-of-current-short-term-down-trend.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2013/06/sell-in-may-worked-my-confession-d-9-of-current-short-term-down-trend/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

A rare methodology self-correction. "I was trying to minimize the risk of being whip-sawed in my short term trend count. I adopted a new criterion for a change in the short term trend of the QQQ. In doing so, I missed the beginning of the current decline. So I went back to my original method and counted the down trend from where it would have begun." He re-states the corrected count in full: the up-trend that began April 23 ended June 5 after 31 days (U-31), then a 2-day down-trend and a 2-day up-trend, then a new down-trend from June 12 reaching D-9 — "so I did have two short term trends that lasted for only 2 days." Readers had questioned the published count, and he corrected it publicly. Also the *Sell in May* pattern on a monthly SPY chart (2010, 2011, 2012 held; 2009 the exception).

## Key claims

- The short-term trend count uses his **original** criterion; an anti-whipsaw variant was tried in 2013 and abandoned because it missed the start of a decline.
- Two-day trends are accepted output of the original rule — the whipsaw is tolerated rather than filtered.
- Counts published on the blog are corrected in public when readers challenge them.
- Sell-in-May noted as a recurring seasonal in 2010–2012.
