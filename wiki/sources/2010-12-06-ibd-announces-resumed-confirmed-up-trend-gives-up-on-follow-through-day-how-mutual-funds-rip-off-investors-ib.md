# WW 2010-12-06 — IBD gives up on the follow-through day; mutual funds restrict timing; IBD100 top-10 portfolios beat the indexes

**Source:** [IBD announces “resumed confirmed up-trend”, gives up on follow-through day; How mutual funds rip off investors; IBD100 list top 10 outperform indexes](../../raw/posts/2010-12-06-ibd-announces-resumed-confirmed-up-trend-gives-up-on-follow-through-day-how-mutual-funds-rip-off-investors-ib.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2010/12/ibd-announces-resumed-confirmed-up-trend-gives-up-on-follow-through-day-how-mutual-funds-rip-off-investors-ibd100-list-top-10-outperform-indexes/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

Three things: IBD declares a "resumed confirmed up-trend" without a high-volume follow-through day ("even if the classic sign of a bottom has eluded the major indexes"); a reader is barred from re-buying a mutual fund after selling — the fund families' anti-timing rules — prompting "I do not trade my university pension mutual funds often, but when my indicators tell me that a big down-trend is likely, I move to cash… There ought to be a law against the prohibition of market timing for mutual funds. The only good alternative right now is to get out of mutual funds and invest in suitable ETF's"; and one-share portfolios of four IBD100 top-10 lists each beat the Nasdaq-100 and S&P 500 (the June 28 list +44% vs +19% / +13.9%; BIDU and NFLX on all four). SPY/QQQQ above their 10-week for 14 straight weeks; U-10; 56% of Nasdaq-100 with MACD above signal.

## Key claims

- IBD abandons the FTD requirement in December 2010, on low-volume days.
- Mutual-fund anti-timing restrictions as a hard constraint on the pension approach; ETFs as the alternative.
- IBD100 top-10 portfolios beat both indexes after each of four publication dates.
