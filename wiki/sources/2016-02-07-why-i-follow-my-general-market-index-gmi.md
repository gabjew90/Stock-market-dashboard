# WW 2016-02-07 — Why I follow my GMI: the December 2015 Sell signal audited

**Source:** [Why I follow my General Market Index, GMI](../../raw/posts/2016-02-07-why-i-follow-my-general-market-index-gmi.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2016/02/why-i-follow-my-general-market-index-gmi/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

A live audit of one specific GMI Sell signal (December 10, 2015) two months on, with the
component-level numbers that justify it, plus two doctrine statements the wiki lacked in this form:
the anguish-avoidance framing of timing, and the age-based case against shorting.

## Key claims

- **The audit:** since the December 10 Sell signal, "between **78%-92%** of the stocks in the
  three ETFs I follow most closely declined." QQQ −13.5% with **92% of its components down and
  60% down 10%+**. He notes the circularity honestly: "Because many of the GMI components are
  based on the action of the QQQ, it is not surprising to me that the QQQ... performed the
  worst."
- **The learning-the-hard-way line, again:** "I learned the hard way that the same buy set-ups
  and rules that worked in a rising market **failed repeatedly** in a declining market."
- **The anguish framing — timing's real payoff:** "I never worry about missing the market's
  eventual recovery. **If I get back in lower or even equal to where I exited, I did well enough
  and avoided a lot of mental anguish.**" Break-even timing is a win, because the alternative
  was riding the decline.
- **The age-based case against shorting:** "I find it very difficult to stay in and trade on the
  short side, and **leave that strategy to younger people** with greater ability to handle the
  risks and losses from shorting." Mostly-cash, not short, is his bear-market default.
- Context: GMI at 0 of 6, in cash — written in the depths of the early-2016 decline the January
  BWR-sweep post (already ingested) opened.
