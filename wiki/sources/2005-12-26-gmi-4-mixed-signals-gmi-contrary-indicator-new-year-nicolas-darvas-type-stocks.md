# WW 2005-12-26 — The 10-day / 30-day cross as the short-term trend test (2005)

**Source:** [GMI: +4; Mixed signals; GMI: contrary indicator?  New Year Nicolas Darvas type stocks](../../raw/posts/2005-12-26-gmi-4-mixed-signals-gmi-contrary-indicator-new-year-nicolas-darvas-type-stocks.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2005/12/gmi-4-mixed-signals-gmi-contrary-indicator-new-year-nicolas-darvas-type-stocks/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

The short-term trend defined by a moving-average relationship rather than a day count: "the QQQQ closed Friday just below its 30 day average, and **its declining 10 day average is just above the 30 day average (41.52/41.49). The last time the QQQQ's 10 day average was below its 30 day was on October 31 at the beginning of the rally.** Tomorrow's close should be very important for defining the short term trend." Two breadth readings alongside: only 47% of the 160 stocks that made new highs ten days earlier closed higher, and "**only 10% of stocks that have doubled in the past year hit a new high. Thus, there is some pressure on the leaders**." Against that, 168 new highs, 67% of stocks above their 10-week, and three times as many stocks within 5% of a high as of a low.

## Key claims

- The 10-day/30-day average relationship used to define the short-term trend in 2005 — before the day-count became the published measure.
- The doubler indicator at 10% read as pressure on the leaders.
- Conflicting breadth readings held simultaneously rather than resolved — leaders weak, participation still broad.
