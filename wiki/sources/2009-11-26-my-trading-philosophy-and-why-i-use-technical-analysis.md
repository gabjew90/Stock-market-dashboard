# WW 2009-11-26 — My Trading Philosophy and Why I Use Technical Analysis

**Source:** [My Trading Philosophy and Why I Use Technical Analysis](../../raw/posts/2009-11-26-my-trading-philosophy-and-why-i-use-technical-analysis.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2009/11/my-trading-philosophy-and-why-i-use-technical-analysis/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

The philosophical foundation of the whole system, set out as **five numbered propositions**
that each follow from the previous one. Written as a Thanksgiving piece, drawing on "40+ years
of experience" and on Darvas, Weinstein, O'Neil, the Turtles and Livermore. A version had
appeared under the pseudonym **Sir Silent Knight** in the Worden TC2007 daily journal.

This is the source for the new [trading-philosophy.md](../methodology/trading-philosophy.md)
page. Trading psychology and first principles had no owner in the wiki before this.

## Key claims

- **P1 — unpredictability.** "No one can consistently predict changes in the market or stocks."
  Corporate news "can be inaccurate or intentionally misleading."
- **P2 — but trends persist.** They form identifiable patterns "probably because humans react
  to trend patterns in repeatable ways," though "their length and continuation are also
  unpredictable."
- **P3 — so: identify trends once begun and stay with them until they end.** He accepts the
  consequence that any individual trade is a coin flip: "Some successful traders have asserted
  that only about 50% of their trades are profitable."
- **P4 — asymmetry is the whole game.** With a ~50% hit rate, winners must be much larger than
  losers. Limit losses "by setting stop losses and by making small initial trades"; increase
  profits "by riding the trend as long as possible AND by systematically increasing one's
  position as the trend continues."
- **P5 — exits dominate entries.** The strongest claim: "one could probably **select stocks at
  random** as long as losses are kept at a minimum and profits are maximized... the highest
  priority must be given to the rules for exiting the trade."
- **The disposition:** "*If one enters each trade assuming that it will fail, one will be
  better prepared to handle losses.*"
- He frames technical analysis instrumentally — it does not predict, it improves the odds by
  keeping you aligned with the general market's trend.
