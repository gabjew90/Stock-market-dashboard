# WW 2020-02-09 — Day 81, chasing the record — two weeks before the COVID top

**Source:** [81st day of $QQQ short term up-trend, heading for record? GLB: $AMZN](../../raw/posts/2020-02-09-81st-day-of-qqq-short-term-up-trend-heading-for-record-glb-amzn.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2020/02/81st-day-of-qqq-short-term-up-trend-heading-for-record-glb-amzn/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

Written fifteen days before the top, and worth keeping for what it shows about reading a trend's age in real
time. "**Since 2006, the longest QQQ short term up-trend has lasted 88 days. The longer term trends are still up,
so this shorter term trend indicator may shatter the record.**" It did — the up-trend ran to **91 days**, ending
24 February 2020, and became the record he cites afterwards. So the post captures the moment the two readings
conflicted: the trend was historically old (day 81 of a population whose maximum was 88), and every other
indicator was fine. He resolved it the way his rules require — noting the age, staying with the trend, and
declining to anticipate. The sentiment reading pointed the same way: "**only 47.6% of newsletters are bullish, a
sign that there is still a wall of worry for this bull to climb.**" Both readings were reasonable and both were
about to be overtaken. AMZN's GLB to an all-time high on above-average volume is the trade noted alongside.

## Key claims

- **The record-setting up-trend, observed at day 81 with the prior maximum at 88** — and it ran to 91 before ending on the COVID top.
- An unusually old trend is read as *notable* rather than as a signal: he stays with it and does not anticipate.
- Sentiment agreed with staying long (47.6% bulls, "a wall of worry"), and both readings were about to be overtaken.
- A useful check on hindsight: nothing in his framework was flashing on 9 February 2020 except the trend's age.
