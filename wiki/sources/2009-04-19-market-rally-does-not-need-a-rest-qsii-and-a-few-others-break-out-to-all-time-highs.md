# WW 2009-04-19 — 'The market needs to rest' is nonsense; QSII breaks a three-year base

**Source:** [Market rally does not need a rest; QSII and a few others break-out to all-time highs](../../raw/posts/2009-04-19-market-rally-does-not-need-a-rest-qsii-and-a-few-others-break-out-to-all-time-highs.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2009/04/market-rally-does-not-need-a-rest-qsii-and-a-few-others-break-out-to-all-time-highs/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

A named fallacy and a worked breakout. **The fallacy**: "The media pundits claim the market needs to rest. The
market gets tired and needs to consolidate for a while — **such utter nonsense!**" His counter is not rhetorical
but a base rate: the Dow was up only ~24% from the bottom against roughly 100% snap-backs from the 1930s bottoms,
and "the QQQQ is only in the 22nd day of its up-trend. **The up-trend that ended July 27, 2007, lasted 80 days!**
The current rally may have a lot longer to go." Duration statistics used to resist a narrative. **The breakout**:
QSII, "a 40 bagger from 1999-2006, then formed a three year base," breaking from that base to a new high on
increased volume — "**when a stock breaks from a multi-year base to an all-time high, I become interested.**"
The Darvas rationale for why an all-time high is the safest place to buy: he "liked to buy stocks that were at
their highest levels ever. These were rockets in which **everyone who owned them had a profit**" — no trapped
holders waiting to sell into strength. Confirmation from the fundamental side: QSII on both the IBD New America
list and the IBD 100. Also: T2108 at 92%, "as high a reading as it ever gets. **This does not mean that the
market is overbought and must decline, only that it is rebounding from deeply oversold territory**"; and the
scarcity argument — with no more than 15 new highs on any day that week, "**the fact that few stocks are at
their 52 week highs makes these winners stand out.**"

## Key claims

- **"The market needs to rest" is rejected as a category error** — markets do not get tired; the answer to how far a rally can run is a duration base rate, not a metaphor.
- **A multi-year base broken to an all-time high on volume is the setup he wants** — QSII's three-year base after a 40-bagger.
- **Darvas's no-overhead-supply argument, in one line**: at an all-time high everyone who owns the stock has a profit, so nobody is waiting to sell at break-even.
- T2108 at its practical ceiling read as recovery from oversold rather than as an overbought warning — the same context rule as two weeks earlier at 84%.
- Scarcity as a screening advantage: fewer new highs makes the ones that appear more informative.
- Portfolio context: back in the black for 2009 while still working off a 5% loss from 2008, via long stocks plus QLD calls and cash-secured puts written in the IRA.
