# WW 2005-04-21 — The 10,000 bounce — 'bear market' is a dangerous habit of speech

**Source:** [The 10,000 Bounce](../../raw/posts/2005-04-21-the-10000-bounce.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2005/04/the-10000-bounce/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

A discipline about vocabulary, stated in the blog's first week: "**I have been using the term bear market — and this habit is dangerous. It is much safer to say that the market is in a down trend.** Once I choose to use terms like bull or bear market it" commits him to a scenario the tape has not yet confirmed. The Dow had fallen 10,507.97 → 10,012.36 in six days and bounced 200 points off the round number: "does a 200 point rally nullify a 500 point decline? Is this a bottom? To me, little has changed. The market is still in a down trend. **The Dow would have to close 300 points higher for me to start to consider the bull case.**" The new-high index rose from 12 to 28, "still a paltry number." He notes that a *quick* break of 10,000 would probably have produced the climactic sell-off that ends a decline — and it did not come.

## Key claims

- "'Bear market' is a dangerous habit… it is much safer to say that the market is in a down trend" — naming a regime commits you to a scenario.
- A price level named in advance for reconsidering the bull case (Dow +300).
- Round numbers matter psychologically: a fast break of 10,000 could have produced the capitulation.
- A 200-point rally does not nullify a 500-point decline.
