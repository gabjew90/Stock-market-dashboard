# WW 2011-11-28 — GMI performance since April 2011; the two-day confirmation rule adopted

**Source:** [6th day of QQQ short term down-trend; GMI performance since April; Stage 4–CMG?](../../raw/posts/2011-11-28-6th-day-of-qqq-short-term-down-trend-gmi-performance-since-april-stage-4-cmg.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2011/11/6th-day-of-qqq-short-term-down-trend-gmi-performance-since-april-stage-4-cmg/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

The origin of the two-day rule. He plots every GMI regime change on a daily QQQ chart since April 19, 2011 — bullish = close at 4 or more, bearish = below 4 — and counts 12: the GMI turned bearish for most of the June, August and October declines, but three changes lasted one day (2→3, 5→6, 9→10). "*If I had used a rule that said that a change in trend had to persist for two days to be valid, I would have avoided these false signals.* I will use that rule when I write about future changes in the market trend." Also: 86% of Nasdaq-100 with a daily stochastic under 20 and T2108 at 24% — "not the time to initiate shorts"; CMG closing under its 30-week after quadrupling since 2009 — a short if the 10-week crosses under the 30-week; a student running the same audit over five years.

## Key claims

- Bullish/bearish regime defined as GMI ≥ 4 vs < 4; 12 changes April–November 2011; three one-day whipsaws.
- The two-day persistence rule adopted here (November 28, 2011); first used for signal #13 on December 1, 2011.
- Oversold breadth (86% of Nasdaq-100 with stochastic < 20; T2108 24%) as a reason not to start shorts.
- Stage 4 candidate rule: close under the 30-week after a long Stage 2, short if 10-week crosses under 30-week.
