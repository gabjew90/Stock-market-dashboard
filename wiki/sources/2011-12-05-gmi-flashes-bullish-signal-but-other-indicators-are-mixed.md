# WW 2011-12-05 — GMI signal #13 under the new rule; the 'major innovation' — enter oversold inside a Stage 2 up-trend

**Source:** [GMI flashes bullish signal, but other indicators are mixed; LNG: Judy’s latest concept](../../raw/posts/2011-12-05-gmi-flashes-bullish-signal-but-other-indicators-are-mixed.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2011/12/gmi-flashes-bullish-signal-but-other-indicators-are-mixed/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

Two things dated here. First, the GMI "flashed a new bullish signal (signal #13) on Thursday, using my new criterion… of needing 2 days with a GMI of at least 4 to signal a new up-trend" — while IBD still called a correction for want of a follow-through day. Second, the entry doctrine that later became BOS and OSB, stated as new: "**This is a major innovation in my trading. Instead of trying to enter equities when they break out, I seek to enter when an equity is in a weekly Stage 2 up-trend and has become oversold on the daily time frame. I go long and then place a stop (or buy a put) just below support in case the decline resumes.**" The market as a rubber band stretched to an extreme and snapping back; he bought QLD at the oversold extreme with a protective put — "if one can get out of the market near the top of a range and then wait for one of these oversold periods to re-enter on the long side — that is the key" — and holds until a sign of a new decline or an overbought point, then sells or writes a call. Judy's LNG concept; 6 of 19 new-high stocks in natural gas.

## Key claims

- Two-day GMI ≥ 4 Buy criterion in use: signal #13, December 1, 2011.
- The oversold-entry doctrine dated: weekly Stage 2 + daily oversold → long with a stop or put under support (December 2011).
- Rubber-band model of the index; buy the oversold extreme, exit at overbought or on a new decline; sell or write a call.
- GMI Buy while IBD (no FTD) still says correction.
