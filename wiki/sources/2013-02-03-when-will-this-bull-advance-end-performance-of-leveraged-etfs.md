# WW 2013-02-03 — When will this bull advance end? — the yearly S&P chart and the 1970s lesson

**Source:** [When will this bull advance end? Performance of leveraged ETF’s](../../raw/posts/2013-02-03-when-will-this-bull-advance-end-performance-of-leveraged-etfs.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2013/02/when-will-this-bull-advance-end-performance-of-leveraged-etfs/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

A yearly-bar chart of the S&P 500 built in TC2000 to answer a question O'Neil and (newly converted) Cramer had both raised — whether a multi-year advance was starting. His own experience supplies the counter-case: "for most of the 70's, when I first traded in the market, the market went up and down in a trading range and could not break out. The Dow Jones average kept hitting 1,000 and backing off. **I would make money and then give it all back and more.**" And the framing that opens the post: "**We all tend to steer our cars (lives) with our hands on the rear view mirror rather than by holding the steering wheel and looking ahead.** We look back to the horrific markets of 2002 and 2008… and are afraid that the current market rise is phony and doomed to fail."

## Key claims

- The yearly-bar chart as the longest timeframe in his ladder — used for regime, not trades.
- The 1970s range-bound market is his personal reference for why trend-following matters: "I would make money and then give it all back and more."
- The rear-view-mirror metaphor for why investors distrust new advances.
- A multi-year breakout above a decades-long range is the bullish case being tested.
