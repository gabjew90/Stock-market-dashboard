# WW 2018-07-16 — The weekly RWB hold rule — hold until the white band disappears; never buy BWR

**Source:** [RWB weekly strategy can keep me in a good position; $QQQ $SPY $GE $CAT $SQ](../../raw/posts/2018-07-16-rwb-weekly-strategy-can-keep-me-in-a-good-position-qqq-spy-ge-cat-sq.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2018/07/rwb-weekly-strategy-can-keep-me-in-a-good-position-qqq-spy-ge-cat-sq/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

The clearest statement of why the weekly chart governs selling: "Daily stock charts are good for determining entry set-ups and initial stops, but daily charts often mislead me and scare me out of good positions. Before I sell out of a long position I should remember to look at the longer term trends evident in the *weekly* RWB charts." The hold rule: "once my entry set-up holds and I am not stopped out, I could actually just hold onto any equity in an RWB weekly up-trend pattern until the white band in the middle (the W in RWB) disappears." QQQ's weekly RWB had not disappeared since 2017; weekly closes dipping below the red lines do not matter while red stays above blue — "in a strong up-trend the equity repeatedly closes the week above all 12 averages." And the negative rule: "never buy and… sell or sell short any equity with the opposite BWR weekly down-trend pattern" (GE from April 2017 as the example).

## Key claims

- Hold rule: stay in any equity whose weekly RWB persists, until the white band disappears.
- Weekly closes below the red lines are noise while red remains above blue.
- "Never buy and… sell or sell short any equity with the opposite BWR weekly down-trend pattern."
- Daily charts for entries and initial stops; weekly charts for the sell decision.
