# WW 2013-11-18 — Bollinger Bands as support and resistance for index ETFs

**Source:** [27th day of QQQ short term up-trend; Bollinger Band constrains QQQ and gives me an edge](../../raw/posts/2013-11-18-27th-day-of-qqq-short-term-up-trend-bollinger-band-constrains-qqq.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2013/11/27th-day-of-qqq-short-term-up-trend-bollinger-band-constrains-qqq/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

**The bands used as levels rather than as a volatility read.** "**I find that the 15 day 2 standard deviation
Bollinger Bands are very useful for identifying short term support and resistance levels for the index ETFs.**
Just look at the daily chart below to see how prices stay within the bands." Applied that day: "QQQ fails to
break above top Bollinger Band (15,2) and bounces down… **I look for support at the lower band, around 81.77.**"
A named price target derived from the band, in advance. **And the qualification that keeps it honest**: "**a move
outside the bottom band is often a sign that the index will reverse up. However, if the lower band is broken and
st[ays]**" below it, the reversion expectation fails. So the bands supply *levels* to trade against and a
*probabilistic* expectation of reversion — but a decisive break through one is a different event from a touch,
which is the distinction that separates the bounce setup from the breakout exception.

## Key claims

- **The 15,2 bands are used as short-term support and resistance levels on index ETFs**, with specific prices quoted in advance.
- A rejection at the upper band projects a move to the lower band, and vice versa — bounded, both ways.
- **A touch reverts; a decisive break does not** — the qualification that separates the bounce setup from the breakout exception.
- Consistent with the 95%-containment rationale he teaches students, applied to an index rather than a stock.
