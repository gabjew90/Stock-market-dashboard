# WW 2019-01-25 — 'I never have the courage to buy the SPY when T2108 reaches single digits'

**Source:** [T2108 back above 70% –may have called bottom again!](../../raw/posts/2019-01-25-t2108-back-above-70-may-have-called-bottom-again.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2019/01/t2108-back-above-70-may-have-called-bottom-again/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

Seven words of self-assessment that qualify one of the wiki's cleanest rules. His documented instruction at a
sub-10% T2108 is to buy SPY, start small, and accumulate on the way up. Here, having watched T2108 run from
single digits back above 70: "**I never have the courage to buy the SPY when T2108 reaches single digits** 🙁."
He repeats it in February 2022 in almost the same words — "if T2108 falls below 10%, **I hope to have the courage
to buy a little SPY. I never do and I miss the bottom.**" Two independent admissions, three years apart, that the
rule exists on paper and not in his behaviour. That belongs beside the rule wherever it appears: the below-10%
instruction is the best-evidenced buy signal in the corpus *and* the one he says he has never acted on. It is the
same gap as the [day-1 leveraged entry](../methodology/leveraged-etf-default.md) — the entries with the highest
expected value are the ones that feel worst, which is precisely why they retain their edge.

## Key claims

- **"I never have the courage to buy the SPY when T2108 reaches single digits"** — stated in 2019 and again in 2022.
- The below-10% instruction is the best-evidenced buy signal on the wiki **and** the one he admits he does not execute.
- Same shape as the day-1 leveraged entry: the highest-expected-value action is the one that feels worst at the time.
- The admission is unprompted and repeated, which is what makes it a documented limitation rather than a passing remark.
