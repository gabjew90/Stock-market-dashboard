# WW 2010-06-21 — Stocks near all-time highs; IBD100 stocks 3× more likely to rise 10%+

**Source:** [Some Stocks Near All-Time highs; Since June 4, IBD100 Stocks 3x more likely to rise 10%+](../../raw/posts/2010-06-21-some-stocks-near-all-time-highs-since-june-4-ibd100-stocks-3x-more-likely-to-rise-10.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2010/06/some-stocks-near-all-time-highs-since-june-4-ibd100-stocks-3x-more-likely-to-rise-10/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

A new-high scan with its criteria (new 52-week high, near the all-time high, most recent quarterly EPS ≥ +50%; the table adds prior-quarter EPS, annual EPS, P/E, one-year price change, revenue change, P/S — all 11 had rising revenue; 64% had appeared on his IBD100/New America lists) and a fresh IBD100 performance check: from the June 7 list, 91% advanced through 6/18, median +6.5%, 21% up ≥10%, versus the Nasdaq-100's 90% / +4.8% / 8% — "three times more likely to advance 10% or more," against the claim that the list is too late to buy (with the standing caveat that such stocks fall faster in declines). Eight of the top ten advanced. Also: two GMI components graded "?" and left unchanged ("those of you who prefer a rigid adherence to the criteria can go with readings of 3 and 6"); T2108 at 50% — "once the pendulum moves out of oversold territory, it typically goes back to around 80%"; 95% of Nasdaq-100 with MACD above signal; wants Day 5 and QQQQ above its 10-week before confidence; the rear-view-mirror fear at the start of an up-trend.

## Key claims

- IBD100 (6/7 list) vs Nasdaq-100 through 6/18: 91% vs 90% advanced; median +6.5% vs +4.8%; 21% vs 8% up ≥10%.
- New-high scan criteria: new 52-week high, near ATH, latest quarterly EPS ≥ +50%.
- GMI grading is not mechanical at the margin: components "too close to call" get a "?" and the prior reading stands.
- T2108: "once the pendulum moves out of oversold territory, it typically goes back to around 80%."
- "At the beginning of a new up-trend, we often are scared to go long because we are… looking through the rear view mirror."
