# WW 2010-04-26 — TRLG: the round trip back to an all-time high, expressed in calls

**Source:** [True Religion (TRLG) break-out?](../../raw/posts/2010-04-26-true-religion-trlg-break-out.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2010/04/true-religion-trlg-break-out/) · tier: trade_example

## What it covers

A complete worked example of the pattern that would later be formalised as the green line breakout, three
years before he named it. TRLG "came to my attention when it hit a 52 week high a few weeks ago" — the
52-week high is the *screen*; the monthly chart is where the decision is made. There he found two things: the
stock was about to break its **all-time** high of 31.82 set in September 2008, and it was a 2005 IPO, which
satisfies "William O'Neil['s]… look for great stocks among those that have come public in the last 8 years or
so." The stock had fallen to 7.80 in March 2009 and closed at 33.11. His statement of the pattern: "**When a
stock can regain all of its lost ground and push to an all-time high, I become very interested.**" He is
equally explicit about the flaw: "**The only thing that was missing was unusual high volume when it broke
out**," and about the risk: earnings were due May 4, so "maybe the stock is rising because insiders know
earnings will be great, or maybe the company will miss and the stock will dive." The resolution is the
instrument, not the thesis — "I have bought some May **call options**… If I am wrong and TRLG comes back down,
**the most I can lose is the cost of the calls**." A short ratio of 11.7 is noted as squeeze fuel.

## Key claims

- **The round trip is the setup**: an ATH recovered after a full bear-market drawdown is the version of the all-time-high pattern he likes most.
- **52-week high screens; the monthly chart decides.** The two-step is stated as procedure here.
- A breakout without unusual volume is bought anyway, but the missing volume is named as the defect — not silently ignored.
- **Calls as the risk-capping expression** for a breakout sitting in front of an earnings report: the loss is the premium, and no stop can be gapped through.
- O'Neil's ~8-years-public rule and IBD's New America column are both used as confirming filters.
- Regime context, stated plainly: T2108 at 81% and "above 77% since March 5th… acts like a pendulum of the market. The market tends to hit tops around 90% and bottoms below 20%, but not always."
- The two-account split in one line: pension fully invested on the up-trend, individual stocks and options in the IRA.
