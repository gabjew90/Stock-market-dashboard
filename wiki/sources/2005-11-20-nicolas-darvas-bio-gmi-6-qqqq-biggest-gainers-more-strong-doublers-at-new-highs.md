# WW 2005-11-20 — Darvas sourced; the trend-odds measured a third time (81%)

**Source:** [Nicolas Darvas bio; GMI: +6; QQQQ biggest gainers; More strong doublers at new highs](../../raw/posts/2005-11-20-nicolas-darvas-bio-gmi-6-qqqq-biggest-gainers-more-strong-doublers-at-new-highs.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2005/11/nicolas-darvas-bio-gmi-6-qqqq-biggest-gainers-more-strong-doublers-at-new-highs/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

Two things. **The Darvas sourcing question answered**: "Some of my readers have asked for more information
about this amazing dancer-trader… the best source on Darvas is the article (available for a small fee from the
TIME magazine archive) that made him famous — **'Pas de Dough,' May 25, 1959**," alongside his own piece on
Darvas's "little known and rare final book." **And the trend-odds measurement run a third time**: since the
up-trend began on November 1 the QQQQ had risen 6.6%, over which "**81% of the 100 stocks in the Nasdaq 100 have
advanced. This again shows that if we traded with the up-trend in the QQQQ average, we had an 81% chance of
having a profit.**" Thirty-three of the hundred gained 10% or more. Note the inference in that sentence — a
participation *rate* is being read as a *probability* for an individual trade, which holds only for a
randomly-chosen component held for exactly that window. The WPM shows the small caps lagging (73% in a
short-term up-trend against 82%+ elsewhere; 61% above their 30-week averages). Also a compact rocket screen: the
stocks that "hit a new high Friday, have **strong recent quarterly earnings** and are **up at least 60% in the
past year**."

## Key claims

- **The primary Darvas source he recommends is the 1959 TIME article "Pas de Dough,"** plus a rare final book he wrote about separately.
- **A third instance of the trend-odds measurement**: 81% of Nasdaq-100 components advanced over a 6.6% index gain, 33% of them by 10%+.
- **The inference is looser than the measurement.** "An 81% chance of having a profit" converts a participation rate into a per-trade probability — true only for a random component held over exactly that window.
- The screen in one line: new high today, strong recent quarterly earnings, up ≥ 60% over the past year.
- Small caps identified as the laggard through the WPM even with all five indexes above their 30-day and 30-week averages.
