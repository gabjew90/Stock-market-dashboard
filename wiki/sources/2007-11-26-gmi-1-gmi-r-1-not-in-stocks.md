# WW 2007-11-26 — 'What's the need to hurry?' — three durations against the fear of missing out

**Source:** [GMI: 1; GMI-R: 1; Not in stocks](../../raw/posts/2007-11-26-gmi-1-gmi-r-1-not-in-stocks.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2007/11/gmi-1-gmi-r-1-not-in-stocks/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

The argument against re-entering early, made with numbers rather than temperament. "**The media pundits make
us think that we must be invested all of the time so that we will not miss the next rise. But this is horrible
advice. Any advance worth trading lasts months and one can wait for the train to leave the station and still hop
on for a long trip. The advance that just ended lasted 50 days, the prior advance lasted 80 days, and the one
last December lasted 86 days. So what's the need to hurry?**" Three measured durations, used to show that the
cost of confirming a turn is small relative to what remains. And the gate argued from participation on the same
day: since D-1 on 8 November the QQQQ had fallen 3.7%, over which "**87% of the Nasdaq 100 stocks have
declined**… 75% of the stocks in the S&P500 declined… along with 80% of the Dow 30." His stance: "I guess some
of you probably thought that now that I am 100% in cash, this must be the bottom! It could be, but **my track
record in avoiding major declines has been quite good. I am a chicken and would rather get out and be proven
wrong than stay in and suffer a large loss.**"

## Key claims

- **Three advance durations (50, 80, 86 days) used as the argument against hurrying** — if the move lasts months, a few days of confirmation costs little.
- "**Any advance worth trading lasts months and one can wait for the train to leave the station and still hop on for a long trip.**"
- The always-invested advice named as "horrible" — with the reason being the asymmetry, not the arithmetic of returns.
- The gate measured again on the downside: 87% / 75% / 80% of the three indexes' components down over one 3.7% index decline.
- He anticipates being mocked as a contrary indicator and answers with his record rather than an argument.
