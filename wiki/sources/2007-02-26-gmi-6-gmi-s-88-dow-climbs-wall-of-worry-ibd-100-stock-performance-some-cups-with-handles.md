# WW 2007-02-26 — GMI 6, GMI-S 88, GMI-L 100; the Dow's multi-year base; O'Neil's 8% rule

**Source:** [GMI: 6; GMI-S: 88; Dow climbs wall of worry; IBD 100 stock performance; some cups with handles](../../raw/posts/2007-02-26-gmi-6-gmi-s-88-dow-climbs-wall-of-worry-ibd-100-stock-performance-some-cups-with-handles.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2007/02/gmi-6-gmi-s-88-dow-climbs-wall-of-worry-ibd-100-stock-performance-some-cups-with-handles/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

All green: 223 new highs to 16 lows; SPY above its 10-week for 27 weeks, DIA 30; GMI-L above 90 since September — "in my major pension account where I use a longer term trading strategy, I have stayed 100% invested in mutual funds since last fall." Against the hand-wringing pundits, the Dow's monthly chart: peak 2000, bottom 2002, five years to new highs — "when a market declines and then builds a large multi-year base and has the buying strength to overcome the overhead supply (from persons who bought at higher levels and sell as soon as they break even) and pushes through to new highs, it often portends a considerable rise"; the Dow's 1,000 ceiling of the 60s–70s finally broken in 1982 at the *start* of the bull. IBD100 lists performing about like the Nasdaq-100 lately. Cup-with-handle candidates; RVSN's pivot ~21.75; "according to O'Neil, a stock bought at the proper pivot point should not decline 8% below that point. If it does, it should be sold. I like to buy IBD 100 stocks that break from a base on high volume because I am confident that the stock has also passed IBD's stringent fundamental criteria."

## Key claims

- The overhead-supply logic applied to a multi-year index base in 2007 — the GLB idea on the Dow, five years before 'green line' was coined.
- GMI-L > 90 since September 2006 as the pension's stay-invested condition.
- O'Neil's 8% stop below the pivot, cited as a rule.
- IBD100 breakouts on high volume: technical trigger + IBD's fundamental screen.
