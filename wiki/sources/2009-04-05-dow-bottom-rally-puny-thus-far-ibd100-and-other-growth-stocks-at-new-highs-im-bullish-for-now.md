# WW 2009-04-05 — Why component 6 exists — 'when these fund managers can make money, so can I'

**Source:** [Dow “bottom rally” puny thus far!; IBD100 and other growth stocks at new highs; I’m bullish for now](../../raw/posts/2009-04-05-dow-bottom-rally-puny-thus-far-ibd100-and-other-growth-stocks-at-new-highs-im-bullish-for-now.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2009/04/dow-bottom-rally-puny-thus-far-ibd100-and-other-growth-stocks-at-new-highs-im-bullish-for-now/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

The clearest single-sentence rationale for the GMI's sixth component anywhere in the corpus: "**The IBD Mutual Fund Index is above its 50 day average, indicating to me that growth mutual funds are starting to make money. When these fund managers can make money buying growth stocks, so can I.**" The component is a proxy for whether professional growth managers — running his strategy with more resources — are currently able to make it work. Also O'Neil's IPO-age criterion, cited: CPSI and TNDM "came public within the past 8 years, **a prime criterion of past winning stocks, according to IBD founder William O'Neil**." And a scan: 105 stocks within 10% of their 52-week highs with promising technicals, narrowed to IBD100 names with last-quarter earnings up 50%+ (AIPC, GMCR, ALGT, QSII, SXL, JJSF, NFLX, PNRA). T2108 at 84% heading to overbought "but not a concern to me, given that the market is snapping back from very over-sold readings."

## Key claims

- **Component 6's rationale in one line:** if professional growth-fund managers cannot make money, neither can he.
- O'Neil's criterion that winners are typically companies public within the last ~8 years.
- A screen: within 10% of the 52-week high + good technicals + last-quarter EPS up ≥ 50%, run against IBD100 names.
- An overbought T2108 reading is discounted when it comes off a very oversold extreme — context beats level.
