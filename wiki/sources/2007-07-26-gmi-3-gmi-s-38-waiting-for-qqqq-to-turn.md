# WW 2007-07-26 — A component with its own two-day rule, in 2007

**Source:** [GMI: 3; GMI-S: 38; Waiting for QQQQ to turn](../../raw/posts/2007-07-26-gmi-3-gmi-s-38-waiting-for-qqqq-to-turn.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2007/07/gmi-3-gmi-s-38-waiting-for-qqqq-to-turn/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

**Evidence that persistence requirements predate the 2011 signal rule — inside a component.** Correcting an
earlier post: "I posted '3?' on one day since then but **I subsequently corrected it to 4 because I had jumped
the gun on one indicator that required 2 days down before turning**." So at least one GMI component carried its
own two-consecutive-days condition in July 2007, four years before the two-day persistence rule was adopted for
the *signal*. That reframes the 2011 change: it generalised to the composite a discipline already operating at
the component level. It is also a published correction of his own table, which is worth noting on its own. **And
the cost of discretion, priced**: "**By following my gut I did miss out on further gains in GRMN and AAPL, but I
am coming away with almost all of my profits from the past three months.**" The trade-off stated in both
directions — what the early exit cost, and what it preserved. T2108 at 31, "still not in the range of past market
bottoms, typically below 25."

## Key claims

- **A GMI component required two consecutive days before turning, in 2007** — persistence existed at component level four years before the signal rule.
- He publishes a correction to his own previously posted GMI reading rather than leaving it.
- **The cost of the discretionary exit is stated with the benefit**: gains missed in GRMN and AAPL against "almost all of my profits" kept.
- T2108's bottom range given as "typically below 25" in 2007 — consistent with the 14–25 band assembled from cases elsewhere.
