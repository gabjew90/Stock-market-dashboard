# WW 2006-01-17 — The advance measured across three indexes; the inverted-yield-curve worry

**Source:** [GMI: +6; Market steady; Go with the trend; Leading stocks and sectors; Inverted yield curve–this time is different?](../../raw/posts/2006-01-17-gmi-6-market-steady-go-with-the-trend-leading-stocks-and-sectors-inverted-yield-curve-this-time-is-different.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2006/01/gmi-6-market-steady-go-with-the-trend-leading-stocks-and-sectors-inverted-yield-curve-this-time-is-different/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

The clearest early statement of *why* he gates every trade on the market's trend, done as arithmetic rather
than assertion. Since the QQQQ closed above its 10-week average on October 18, the index had climbed 13.4% —
and over that same stretch **86% of the Nasdaq-100 stocks advanced** (42 of them by more than 14%, the top
twenty between 27% and 64%), **88% of the S&P 500** and **87% of the Dow 30**. His conclusion: "Over and over
again, we find that **by trading consistent with the trend of the key market averages, we have considerable
odds of profiting**." Against that, the reservation he keeps returning to: the short-term interest-rate
indicator "has been on a steady rise since June, 2004 and has broken to new highs the past two weeks. There is
no sign of a plateau in short rates… **The market cannot climb an inverted wall of interest rates.** We may be
at the point when the seers will claim, 'but this time is different.'" Also tabulated: 65% of all stocks in a
short-term up-trend, "not far from the maximum percentage (68%) reached since I began counting it last April."

## Key claims

- **The market-gate, quantified**: in a confirmed index up-trend, 86–88% of the component stocks of all three major indexes rose. This is the same argument form — and the same measurement — that the ultra-ETF posts later run against the distribution of components.
- The % of stocks in a short-term up-trend has a **practical ceiling near 68%**, established over the first nine months of the series.
- The rate worry is about the *trend and the absence of a plateau*, not a level, and it is aimed at the yield curve inverting.
- "This time is different" is flagged as the phrase to fear, not to be reassured by.
