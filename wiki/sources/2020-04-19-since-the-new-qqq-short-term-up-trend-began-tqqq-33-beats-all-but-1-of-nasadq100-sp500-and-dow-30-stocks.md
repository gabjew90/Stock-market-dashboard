# WW 2020-04-19 — TQQQ against 809 growth stocks — the widest test

**Source:** [Since the new $QQQ short term up-trend began, $TQQQ (+33%)  beats all  but 1 of NASADQ100, S&P500 and Dow 30 stocks.](../../raw/posts/2020-04-19-since-the-new-qqq-short-term-up-trend-began-tqqq-33-beats-all-but-1-of-nasadq100-sp500-and-dow-30-stocks.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2020/04/since-the-new-qqq-short-term-up-trend-began-tqqq-33-beats-all-but-1-of-nasadq100-sp500-and-dow-30-stocks/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

The same measurement run across the widest universe in the corpus, off the April 2020 bottom. "Since my QQQ
short term trend indicator turned up on **April 7, TQQQ has advanced 33%.** Of all stocks in the major indexes
(DIA, QQQ, SPY) **only TSLA advanced more (+38%)**. In my big watchlist of IBD and MarketSmith type growth
stocks, **only 2% (18/809) beat TQQQ.**" One stock out of roughly 1,100 index members, and 2% of a curated growth
watchlist — the growth list does better than the indexes, as it should, and still leaves 98% behind. His
conclusion is a statement about where to spend effort: "**for my time and money, I would rather buy TQQQ in an
up-trend than try to** [identify the exceptions]." The measurement is dated from *his own trend signal* rather
than from a calendar date, which is what makes it a test of the strategy rather than of the instrument.

## Key claims

- **2% (18 of 809) of a curated growth watchlist beat TQQQ**; among the three major indexes' members, only TSLA did.
- The window is defined by his own trend signal (7 April), so it tests the strategy rather than the fund.
- A curated growth list beats the broad indexes at this and still leaves 98% behind — selection helps, and not enough.
- "For my time and money" — the argument is about effort allocation, not only about return.
