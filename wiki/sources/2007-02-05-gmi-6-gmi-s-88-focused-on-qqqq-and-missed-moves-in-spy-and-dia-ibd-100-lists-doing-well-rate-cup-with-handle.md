# WW 2007-02-05 — The QQQ blind spot as a missed opportunity, a year early

**Source:** [GMI: 6; GMI-S: 88; Focused on QQQQ and missed moves in SPY and DIA; IBD 100 lists doing well; RATE- cup with handle?](../../raw/posts/2007-02-05-gmi-6-gmi-s-88-focused-on-qqqq-and-missed-moves-in-spy-and-dia-ibd-100-lists-doing-well-rate-cup-with-handle.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2007/02/gmi-6-gmi-s-88-focused-on-qqqq-and-missed-moves-in-spy-and-dia-ibd-100-lists-doing-well-rate-cup-with-handle/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

**The QQQ-centricity problem stated as a personal cost, eleven months before the post the wiki credits for
it.** "However, **by focusing *solely* on the QQQQ, I ignored the terrific performance of the SPY and DIA. The
SPY and DIA have closed above their 10 week averages for 24 consecutive weeks!**" The 2008 statement frames the
blind spot analytically — the GMI can read optimistic when tech leads. This one frames it as money left on the
table, with the arithmetic: over that stretch "the ProShares ultra ETFs have zoomed: **SPY +11% (SSO +22%) and
DIA +11.5% (DDM +23%)**," while he had exited QLD in late December. So the leveraged-ETF argument he makes for
the QQQQ applies equally to the other indexes — and the cost of watching only one of them is a 22–23% move
missed. Context: GMI back to 6, U-1 of a new QQQQ up-trend, and a flat-averages observation worth noting —
"the 10, 30 and 50 day averages of the QQQQ are flat and fairly similar, indicating little movement in this index
over the past 50 days."

## Key claims

- **The blind spot is dated to February 2007** and expressed as a missed 22–23% move, not as an analytical caveat.
- **The ultra-ETF case is not QQQ-specific**: SSO returned 22% on an 11% SPY move, DDM 23% on 11.5% in DIA.
- Watching one index because your indicator is built on it is a self-inflicted narrowing — the WPM exists to prevent exactly this.
- Flat 10/30/50-day averages read as "little movement over the past 50 days" — the trendless-market diagnosis in miniature.
