# WW 2011-01-31 — The Zweig template abroad; the pre-decline warning stack

**Source:** [Warning: India stocks; QQQQ short term up-trend in jeopardy; IBD50 list performance](../../raw/posts/2011-01-31-warning-india-stocks.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2011/01/warning-india-stocks/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

The Martin Zweig rate-hike framework applied to a *foreign* market (India, 2011) — price
weakness noticed first, the rate-hike explanation found afterwards — plus a five-part stack of
early-warning signals weeks before trouble, and the first IBD50-era performance test.

## Key claims

- **Price first, explanation second:** "I found this article *after* the weakness in India
  stocks prompted me to search for any explanation... the putative rationale for the decline is
  not as important to me as the price action. **The bad news usually comes out long after a
  stock has peaked.**"
- **The Zweig template:** "a series of rate hikes usually kills a market up-trend" — the Fed
  "usually errs by applying the brakes too hard" — here transferred to India's hiking cycle,
  with TTM's 30-day-close sell signal as the worked chart, and puts on India ETFs preferred to
  shorting. A watch list of ominous names below their 30-week averages (INDY, PIN, EPI, HDB...)
  plus the observation that China, Brazil, Turkey, Chile, Thailand looked similar.
- **The warning stack, published while US indicators still read strong** (GMI 5, 21 weeks above
  the 10-week): (1) very few bearish advisers — **19.1%**, the *inverse* of the bears-exceed-
  bulls re-entry signal; (2) "sudden selling in previously strong stocks" (FFIV, CREE, AMZN...);
  (3) **AAPL and GOOG failing to make new highs after great earnings** — good news that doesn't
  lift a leader is bearish; (4) the municipal-bond worry he'd already sold on; (5) the
  post-earnings calendar lull. He was mainly cash/short in the trading accounts while the
  pension stayed invested — the two-speed design visible again.
- **The IBD50-era test:** the first IBD50 list (Jan 2011) *underperformed* the Nasdaq100 in a
  flat-to-down window — 32% of its stocks rose vs 42%; 10% fell >10% vs 4% — consistent with
  his standing caveat that momentum lists lag "during a falling market." He promises
  replication, which the April 2011 study (already ingested) delivers in an up-market where the
  list wins.
