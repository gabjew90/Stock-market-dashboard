# WW 2006-06-05 — GMI-S and GMI-L share one construction — 4 indicators × 4 index ETFs

**Source:** [GMI: +2; Indicators still improving; New leaders?](../../raw/posts/2006-06-05-gmi-2-indicators-still-improving-new-leaders.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2006/06/gmi-2-indicators-still-improving-new-leaders/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

A single sentence settles how both companion indexes are built: "63% of the GMI-L indicators are now positive as well as 44% of the GMI-S indicators. **Each index measures 4 trend indicators each for the DIA, IJR, SPY and QQQQ.**" So GMI-L is not a different construction from GMI-S — both are 16 tests, four per index ETF, reported as a percentage; they differ only in the horizon of the trend indicators. Also the component-1 logic worked through on a tiny sample: "55% of the 11 stocks that hit a new high 10 days ago closed higher… **while this is a small number, it does show that one had a 55% chance of making a profit by buying new highs 10 days ago**" — against 40% for the short side. And the gate: "I am most likely to make money trading when the QQQQ is consistently above its 10 week moving average."

## Key claims

- **GMI-S and GMI-L share a construction: 4 trend indicators × 4 index ETFs (DIA, IJR, SPY, QQQQ) = 16 tests**, reported as a percentage.
- They differ by horizon, not by design.
- Component 1 read as a probability of profit on both sides — 55% long against 40% short that week.
- A denominator of 11 acknowledged as small — the observation that later produced the min-20 floor.
