# WW 2016-02-28 — The up-trend duration distribution; averaging up slowly; refusing to quantify

**Source:** [5th day of $QQQ short term up-trend; whither the general market?; strong stocks: $HA, $ELLI, $HOFT, $WBMD, $JBSS, $JBT, $VLRS](../../raw/posts/2016-02-28-5th-day-of-qqq-short-term-up-trend-whither-the-general-market-strong-stocks-haellihoftwbmdjbssjbtvlrs.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2016/02/5th-day-of-qqq-short-term-up-trend-whither-the-general-market-strong-stocks-haellihoftwbmdjbssjbtvlrs/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

**A duration statistic the wiki does not have**: "short term up-trends, once they reach 5 days, can often
persist for many days, **the majority go on from 11-44 days, based on an analysis I did of trend changes between
2006-2013**." So the Day-5 filter is backed on the up-trend side by a *central range*, not just a survival rate.
**The tactic**: "I sometimes buy the leveraged 3X QQQ bullish ETF, TQQQ, on the **first or second day** of a QQQ
short term up-trend and try to ride the up-trend… I may buy some TQQQ at the beginning of a QQQ short term
up-trend and **average up *slowly*** if the trend continues." **And a refusal worth recording**: "I therefore
**wager very little money on such counter-trend bounces**. (**Don't ask me to define little or how I average up,
it depends on one's personal account size and tolerance for risk.**)" He declines to publish a position-sizing
number *on principle* — consistent with the 2014 comment that he has no specific sizing rules. **The psychology
of a turn**, stated memorably: "when a trend changes from down to up, **it is very difficult to accept it because
there has often been a long down-trend prior to the change. We all have our hands on the rear view mirror as we
try to navigate the market (and life!).**" Context: a counter-trend bounce inside a GMI Sell, with T2108 having
hit 4%.

## Key claims

- **The majority of up-trends run 11–44 days** (2006–2013 analysis) — the central range behind the Day-5 rule on the long side.
- **Buy TQQQ on day 1 or 2, then average up *slowly*** — the entry is early and the sizing is gradual, which is what makes an early entry survivable.
- **He refuses to quantify "little"** on principle: sizing depends on account size and risk tolerance, so publishing a number would be false precision.
- A counter-trend bounce inside a GMI Sell is traded with deliberately small size — the setup is allowed, the size carries the doubt.
- "We all have our hands on the rear view mirror" — why turns are hard to accept, and why the day count exists.
