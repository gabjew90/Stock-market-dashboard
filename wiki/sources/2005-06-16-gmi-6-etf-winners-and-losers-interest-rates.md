# WW 2005-06-16 — Ranking ETFs across a trend to find where the strength was

**Source:** [GMI: +6, ETF winners and losers; Interest rates](../../raw/posts/2005-06-16-gmi-6-etf-winners-and-losers-interest-rates.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2005/06/gmi-6-etf-winners-and-losers-interest-rates/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

A technique rather than a call: with the QQQQ up 5.2% over a 29-day rally, he ranks the **ETF universe** over
exactly that window to see where the money went. "I used TC2005 to run a scan of ETF's when the QQQQ advanced
5.2%." Winners: OIH +10.5% (oil service), EWW +9.5% (Mexico), IGW +9% (semiconductors), IYE +8.8% (energy), PWT
and XLE +8.6% (energy), IIH +8.5% (internet) — "**clearly the oil and energy sectors were the place to be. Will
they continue to lead this rally?**" Losers: EZA −5% (South Africa), BHH −4.5%, EWK −2.8% (Belgium), EWJ −1.6%
(Japan), plus the defensive sector funds PPH, IXP, XLP and XLV. The pharmaceutical basket draws a jab with a
point in it: PPH "includes all of those wonderful pharmaceutical stocks… **that the pundits have been
recommending to conservative investors in difficult times (like these??)**" — the stocks sold to the cautious
fell while the cyclical ones led. Breadth that day was the strongest of the young blog: 291 new highs to 12 new
lows, "the greatest ratio of new highs to new lows since I posted the GMI on April 26." The short-term rate
indicator "cracked today. **Speculators are betting less on interest rate hikes, at least today they did.**"

## Key claims

- **The sector read done as a ranked ETF scan over a defined window** — the trend's own start date as the measurement period, not a calendar month.
- Both tails are reported: the leaders and the laggards, with the laggards carrying the editorial point.
- **Defensive sector funds underperforming during an advance** is treated as evidence against the advice to hold them, not as a defensive virtue.
- The new-high-to-new-low *ratio* tracked as its own series and compared to the blog's own history.
- The rate indicator read in both directions — "cracked" on a single day, with the one-day caveat attached.
