# WW 2008-04-07 — GMI 3; 9th day of QQQQ up-trend; QLD; URBN — why component 1 is unstable; the truck

**Source:** [GMI: 3; GMI-R: 7; 9th day of QQQQ short term up-trend; QLD; URBN](../../raw/posts/2008-04-07-gmi-3-gmi-r-7-9th-day-of-qqqq-short-term-up-trend-qld-urbn.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2008/04/gmi-3-gmi-r-7-9th-day-of-qqqq-short-term-up-trend-qld-urbn/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

The GMI slipped to 3 because the Successful 10-Day New High indicator turned negative — "this indicator is very unstable when only a few stocks hit new highs 10 days ago" (the reason a minimum count later appears in the table). QQQQ above a rising 10-week: "the best chance of profiting from buying IBD 100 type growth stocks hitting new highs." The 55-day down-trend in numbers: QQQQ −11%, 72% of components down, 31% down >15%; "when the QQQQ is in a down-trend, the odds are 2 to 1 that an individual stock will decline"; QLD −24% while QID +22% — "if one can identify market trends early, all that one has to do is to trade these two ultra QQQ ETF's." To students resisting the up-trend on bad news: the truck — "one must get out of the way or hop on board… the news is usually worst *after* the market has already turned"; Baruch in 1929; in an IRA, no tax or wash-sale cost to going in and out "until I catch the primary wave." URBN: 12× in 2003–05, a 3-year consolidation, back on the IBD 100 and out to all-time highs on the highest volume since 1994 — "one can often identify major break-outs by looking at a stock's monthly chart" (FDG the prior example, $49.06 → $57.75).

## Key claims

- Component 1 is unstable when few stocks made new highs 10 days ago — the rationale for the minimum-count floor.
- Down-trend odds 2:1 against any long; QLD/QID as the trend vehicle (2008).
- The truck metaphor (April 2008); "the news is usually worst after the market has already turned."
- Monthly-chart ATH breakouts on record volume after multi-year consolidations (URBN, FDG) — the GLB before the name.
