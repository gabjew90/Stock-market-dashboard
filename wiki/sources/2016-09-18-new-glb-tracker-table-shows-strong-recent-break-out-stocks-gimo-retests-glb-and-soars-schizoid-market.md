# WW 2016-09-18 — The GLB Tracker's curation admitted; the daily chart as a whipsaw machine

**Source:** [New GLB tracker table shows strong recent break-out stocks; $GIMO retests GLB and soars; Schizoid market](../../raw/posts/2016-09-18-new-glb-tracker-table-shows-strong-recent-break-out-stocks-gimo-retests-glb-and-soars-schizoid-market.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2016/09/new-glb-tracker-table-shows-strong-recent-break-out-stocks-gimo-retests-glb-and-soars-schizoid-market/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

**What the GLB Tracker Table actually is** — and its selection bias, admitted in the post that introduces the
update. It shows "stocks that evidenced a strong week last week by hitting an all-time high on large volume, and
that had a GLB last week or since 8/29," but: "**This list does not contain all GLB stocks that occurred during
this period. It contains only those that have held their GLB (i.e., successful) and have strong technicals and/or
fundamentals.**" A curated survivor list, which is worth knowing before reading its performance — and he flags
luck too, keeping CLCD, "up 197% since it passed its green line break-out point — **a lucky pick!**" The
rationale for the table: "**one benefit of these stocks is that they are less likely to be extended because they
have *recently* broken out above a prior all-time top and therefore may have been just launched on a new journey
upward.**" **The exit rule as stated here is the intraday version**: "If I buy a GLB stock **I immediately sell it
if it *trades* back below its green line break-out point — no exceptions.** If it then comes back above its green
line on good volume, I might buy it back." That is superseded by the close-only rule he states from 2017 onward.
And the insight that explains why it was superseded: "**I find that a daily chart can often lead me to be
whipsawed and to sell a good stock too soon. It's like checking one's blood pressure or cholesterol too often.**"
GIMO's post-breakout consolidation looked healthy on the weekly chart — up weeks on higher volume than down weeks
— "**no reason to sell out early if I had focused on the weekly pattern**… (luckily I bought it back)." Market:
GMI on a Sell but the QQQ trend indicator just turned up, QQQ back above its 10-week while SPY and DIA stayed
below — "a schizoid market" traded with "**small positions and close stops**."

## Key claims

- **The GLB Tracker Table is a curated list of *survivors*, and he says so** — not every GLB in the window, only those that held and have strong technicals or fundamentals.
- **Its selection rationale is non-extension**: a stock that has only just cleared a prior all-time top has not yet run away from its entry.
- **The 2016 exit rule keys on an intraday trade below the line, "no exceptions"** — superseded by the close-only rule from 2017 onward.
- **"A daily chart can often lead me to be whipsawed and to sell a good stock too soon. It's like checking one's blood pressure or cholesterol too often."** — over-monitoring as a distinct failure mode from bad rules.
- The weekly chart as the whipsaw filter: GIMO's consolidation read as healthy weekly (up weeks on higher volume) where the daily chart shook him out.
- A luck disclosure inside a performance table — CLCD +197% called "a lucky pick."
- A divided market is traded with size and stops rather than avoided: "buying break-outs but very defensive, with small positions and close stops."
