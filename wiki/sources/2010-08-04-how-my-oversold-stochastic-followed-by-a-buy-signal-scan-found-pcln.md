# WW 2010-08-04 — The oversold-stochastic-then-buy-signal scan — and what it found in PCLN

**Source:** [How my “oversold stochastic followed by a buy signal” scan found PCLN](../../raw/posts/2010-08-04-how-my-oversold-stochastic-followed-by-a-buy-signal-scan-found-pcln.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2010/08/how-my-oversold-stochastic-followed-by-a-buy-signal-scan-found-pcln/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

The 2010 scan stated as a two-step sequence: a stock whose **stochastic declines to oversold, below 20**, and which then **closes above its 30-day average** — "indicating a buy signal for me." PCLN had triggered it weeks before exploding on blow-out earnings; he did not buy, "because of the low GMI reading," which is the market gate doing its job. Two caveats stated with it: "**this scan does not typically work so well in a down market, but I have found that in an up-trending market it can work quite well**," and "no technique is perfect and one must always use stops to minimize losses."

## Key claims

- Scan = stochastic below 20, then a close above the 30-day average.
- The market gate overrode a valid stock signal — and he says so with the outcome known.
- The scan is explicitly market-state dependent: good in up-trends, poor in down-trends.
- "No technique is perfect and one must always use stops."
