# WW 2015-11-15 — The GMI Sell signal audited by regime — it works in bears, fails in corrections

**Source:** [GMI near a Sell signal, markets at a critical juncture](../../raw/posts/2015-11-15-gmi-near-a-sell-signal-markets-at-a-critical-juncture.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2015/11/gmi-near-a-sell-signal-markets-at-a-critical-juncture/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

**The sharpest version of the whipsaw finding, because it is conditional rather than flat.** "I have reviewed
recent GMI Sell signals and found that while such a Sell signal on **September 1, 2008, kept me out of the
markets during most of the subsequent huge market decline**, during the market's recovery since then, **a Sell
signal has often come at the bottom of a small decline and the GMI reversed to a Buy shortly afterwards.**" So
the signal is not simply noisy — its behaviour is **regime-dependent**: it earns its keep at the start of a bear
market and misfires inside bull-market corrections, which is precisely when it fires most often. That reframes
the February 2015 demotion: the problem is not that the Sell is wrong on average but that the two cases it
cannot distinguish look identical at the moment of firing. "**I do not know which scenario will unfold this
week**" is the honest position. The oversold context he notes alongside it — 10.2 daily stochastics approaching
very oversold across the indexes, the indexes below their lower 15.2 Bollinger Bands, and a put/call of 1.16 —
is exactly the bounce-imminent configuration that makes a Sell suspect.

## Key claims

- **The GMI Sell is regime-conditional, not merely noisy**: it worked at the 2008 onset and has repeatedly marked the *bottom* of small declines since.
- **The two cases are indistinguishable at the moment of firing** — which is the real limitation, sharper than "it whipsaws."
- "I do not know which scenario will unfold this week" — stated rather than resolved.
- The oversold cluster (stochastics, lower bands, put/call 1.16) is the configuration that most often accompanies a false Sell.
- He had already exited all accounts weeks earlier and "cringed as the market recovered" — the cost of the previous instance, admitted.
