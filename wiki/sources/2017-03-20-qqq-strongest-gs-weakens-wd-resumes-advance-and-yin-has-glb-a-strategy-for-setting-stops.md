# WW 2017-03-20 — RLC in practice: GS at 0, WD at 6 — and where the stop goes

**Source:** [$QQQ strongest; $GS weakens, $WD resumes advance and $YIN has GLB; a strategy for setting stops](../../raw/posts/2017-03-20-qqq-strongest-gs-weakens-wd-resumes-advance-and-yin-has-glb-a-strategy-for-setting-stops.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2017/03/qqq-strongest-gs-weakens-wd-resumes-advance-and-yin-has-glb-a-strategy-for-setting-stops/) · tier: trade_example

## What it covers

The Red Line Count used as the daily read across indexes and stocks: QQQ = 6, SPY = 0, DIA = 1, DJ-20 = 0, GLD = 6; ten Dow 30 stocks at RLC = 0. GS, having failed to hold its GLB, closed below all six red lines: "while this is not the kiss of death, I would not buy GS again (or hold it) until GS can close back above its 6 red lines (RLC=6)." WD is the positive case — a February GLB, an advance, a consolidation, now breaking out with RLC = 6 — and he names two stop choices: "its lowest red line = 41.18… which is around where I might place my first sell stop. However, recent daily lows (shown by purple dots) are around 40 and may be a better place of prior support to set my stop."

## Key claims

- RLC = 0 as a do-not-own state until the stock closes back above all six red lines.
- Two stop references on the daily RWB chart: the lowest red line, or the recent cluster of daily lows (purple dots) — the latter "a better place of prior support."
- RLC applied to indexes and sectors (DJ-20, GLD), not just stocks.
