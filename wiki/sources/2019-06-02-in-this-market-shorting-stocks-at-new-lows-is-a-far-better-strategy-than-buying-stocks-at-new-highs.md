# WW 2019-06-02 — In this market, shorting stocks at new lows beats buying stocks at new highs

**Source:** [In this market, shorting stocks at new lows is a far better strategy than buying stocks at new highs](../../raw/posts/2019-06-02-in-this-market-shorting-stocks-at-new-lows-is-a-far-better-strategy-than-buying-stocks-at-new-highs.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2019/06/in-this-market-shorting-stocks-at-new-lows-is-a-far-better-strategy-than-buying-stocks-at-new-highs/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

The mirror image of GMI component 1, disclosed: alongside the nightly percentage of stocks that hit a new high 10 days ago and closed higher ("successful 10-day highs"), he computes a **successful 10-day new low** percentage — stocks that hit a new low 10 days ago and closed lower. Since May 7 the high-side statistic had been ≥ 50% on only 5 days (30% on Friday) while the low-side had been ≥ 50% on 16 of 17 days. Conclusion: in a down-trend the long-side support rules "fail miserably" and shorting new lows or buying puts on them would have paid. "Ask, do I want to make money or to be smart?" DIA back below its 30-week (possible triple top); GMI 1, T2108 24% — "typically falls below 10% at or near a market bottom."

## Key claims

- Successful 10-day new **low** indicator defined: % of stocks that hit a new low 10 days ago and closed lower than 10 days earlier.
- "When [the market is in a down-trend], the rules for buying stocks on support that worked in the rising market fail miserably."
- "Buying or holding stocks at or near new highs is now a losers game… Ask, do I want to make money or to be smart?"
- "If the 30 week average curves down, the market could be at major risk of a significant decline. That signal saved me from staying long during the 2000 and 2008 market debacles."
- T2108 at 24% "typically falls below 10% at or near a market bottom."
