# WW 2015-10-04 — Three market calls that are not the same thing — IBD's, his trend count, and the GMI

**Source:** [IBD calls market up-trend; $SLP –green line break out worth watching](../../raw/posts/2015-10-04-ibd-calls-market-up-trend-slp-green-line-break-out-worth-watching.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2015/10/ibd-calls-market-up-trend-slp-green-line-break-out-worth-watching/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

A clean separation of signals that are easily conflated. IBD called a follow-through day and a "confirmed up-trend"; his own QQQ short-term count was at D-10 and could turn up on a positive Monday close; but "**my short term trend count for the QQQ is NOT the same as my GMI indicator, which remains on a Sell since the close on August 24. It takes much stronger action for my GMI to signal a Buy.**" He rates IBD's calls as "analogous to my very short term trend indicator," not to the GMI. The chart read stacks the levels he watches: support at the **lower 10.2 Bollinger Band**, the QQQ just above a still-declining 30-day, with resistance at the declining 50-day, the 150-day, and the upper Bollinger Band around 108.50.

## Key claims

- Three distinct signals, ranked by how much evidence they need: IBD's follow-through day ≈ his short-term count << the GMI.
- "It takes much stronger action for my GMI to signal a Buy."
- A **10.2** Bollinger setting used on this chart — a third setting alongside the 20.2 of 2009 and the 15.2 he settled on.
- The resistance stack: 30-day, 50-day, 150-day, upper band — a declining average is resistance, not support.
