# WW 2018-02-11 — How long T2108 stays below 10% — the duration of an extreme

**Source:** [T2108 and put/call ratio suggest bottom forming;12 stocks at all-time highs last week](../../raw/posts/2018-02-11-t2108-and-put-call-ratio-suggest-bottom-forming12-stocks-at-all-time-highs-last-week.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2018/02/t2108-and-put-call-ratio-suggest-bottom-forming12-stocks-at-all-time-highs-last-week/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

**The missing dimension on T2108: not just how low, but how long.** "I show my students each semester this
monthly chart of T2108 going back to when Worden created it in **1986**… **The few times since 1986 when T2108
was below 10% the market was severely oversold and did not remain there long. Once, in 1987, it registered its
lowest, <1%.** I call T2108 **the pendulum of the market**, moving from extremely oversold to overbought levels."
And then the operational fact: "**A maximum of one or two weeks below 10% is the norm.**" That converts the
below-10% level from a threshold into a *window* — it tells you roughly how much time you have to accumulate,
which is what makes the "buy SPY, start small, accumulate on the way up" instruction executable rather than
open-ended. It also explains why he treats a *prolonged* stay below 10% as exceptional rather than as
confirmation. The chart he teaches from marks the extreme levels with red and green lines and runs monthly back
to the indicator's creation — the same chart that supports the "only 7 declines below 10% between 1986 and 2007"
count.

## Key claims

- **A maximum of one or two weeks below 10% is the norm** — the extreme is brief, which bounds the accumulation window.
- T2108 below 10% is rare *and* short-lived; the 1987 reading of under 1% is the outlier on depth.
- "The pendulum of the market" defined against a monthly chart running back to the indicator's creation in 1986.
- The duration fact is what makes the below-10% accumulation instruction actionable rather than open-ended.
- He teaches this chart to students every semester — the monthly T2108 history is a fixed part of the course.
