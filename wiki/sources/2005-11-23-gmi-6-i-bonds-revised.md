# WW 2005-11-23 — I-bonds, nine years earlier than the wiki dates them

**Source:** [GMI: +6; I-bonds revised;](../../raw/posts/2005-11-23-gmi-6-i-bonds-revised.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2005/11/gmi-6-i-bonds-revised/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

**The I-bond recommendation is a 2005 position, not a 2014 one.** "**I wrote a piece about I-bonds several
months ago, suggesting that these bonds were a good way to grow savings with protection**" — so the instrument
was already on the blog by mid-2005, revisited here as the terms changed. The wiki dates his I-bond advice to
December 2014; this pushes it back most of a decade and makes it a standing preference rather than a response to
the 2014 rate thesis. The logic is the same in both places: an inflation-adjusting instrument is the one form of
safe money a rate rise does not mark down — the exact complement of the
[bond arithmetic](../methodology/risk-and-cash.md) he would teach in 2014. Market context that day: GMI +6,
350 new highs, U-16, and **80% of the 94 stocks that hit a new high ten days earlier closing higher** — a
strong component-1 reading on a small denominator, of the kind the 2008 floor was later added to guard.

## Key claims

- **I-bonds appear on the blog in 2005**, described as "a good way to grow savings with protection" — nine years before the statement the wiki cites.
- It is a standing preference across the whole record rather than a reaction to any one rate cycle.
- The reasoning is constant: an inflation-adjusting instrument cannot be marked down by the rate rise it protects against.
- A component-1 reading of 80% on a denominator of 94 — the kind of small-sample high the 2008 floor exists to discount.
