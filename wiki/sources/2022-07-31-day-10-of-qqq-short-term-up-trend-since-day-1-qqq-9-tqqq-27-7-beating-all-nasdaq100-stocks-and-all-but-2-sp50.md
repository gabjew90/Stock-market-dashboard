# WW 2022-07-31 — Day 1 versus Day 5 — the tension, resolved by sizing

## What it covers

**The two rules that appear to contradict each other, reconciled in three sentences.** "If I had bought TQQQ on
Day 1 of the QQQ short term up-trend, I would have beaten almost all stocks… **The key is to buy TQQQ on Day 1
even though the new up-trend is most suspect. I have also written that a change in the short term trend is most
reliable after it reaches Day 5. The key to this strategy is to slowly accumulate the triple leveraged ETF.**"
The wiki carries both rules — enter on day 1 for the best price, and wait past day 5 because 40% of down-trends
abort — and they look incompatible until the resolution is stated: **you do both, by scaling**. A small position
on day 1 buys the price; adding after day 5 buys the confirmation; and the average entry lands between the two.
That is the same structure as the [pilot buy](../methodology/risk-and-cash.md) and the reason the day-1 entry is
survivable at all despite being, in his own words, "most suspect." The measurement: since day 1, QQQ +9%, **TQQQ
+27.7%, beating all Nasdaq-100 stocks and all but 2 in the S&P 500.**"

## Key claims

- **Day 1 and Day 5 are not competing rules** — a small day-1 position captures the price, additions after day 5 capture the confirmation.
- **Scaling is what makes the day-1 entry survivable** despite the trend being "most suspect" at that moment.
- The measurement: TQQQ +27.7% against QQQ +9%, beating every Nasdaq-100 stock and all but two in the S&P 500.
- "I hope some of my readers" — published as instruction, which is why he states the tension rather than only the outcome.
