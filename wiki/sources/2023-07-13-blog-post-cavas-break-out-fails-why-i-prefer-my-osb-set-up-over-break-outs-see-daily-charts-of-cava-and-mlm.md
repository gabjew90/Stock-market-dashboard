# WW 2023-07-13 — CAVA's break-out fails; why I prefer my OSB set-up over break-outs

**Source:** [$CAVA's break-out fails–why I prefer my OSB set-up over break-outs, see daily charts of $CAVA and $MLM](../../raw/posts/2023-07-13-blog-post-cavas-break-out-fails-why-i-prefer-my-osb-set-up-over-break-outs-see-daily-charts-of-cava-and-mlm.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2023/07/blog-post-cavas-break-out-fails-why-i-prefer-my-osb-set-up-over-break-outs-see-daily-charts-of-cava-and-mlm/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

The doctrine-level post on the OSB. Dr. Wish states directly that he **prefers the oversold-bounce setup to buying breakouts**, and gives the reason: stop placement. A breakout has no naturally-defined failure price; a bounce does. Uses a failed CAVA breakout against a clean MLM oversold bounce as the paired illustration.

This is the single most important source for [oversold-bounce.md](../methodology/oversold-bounce.md), and it qualifies the emphasis the wiki had previously placed on the [GLB](../methodology/green-line-breakouts.md) as the entry trigger.

## Key claims

- **The preference is explicit and is in the post title:** "why I prefer my OSB set-up over break-outs."
- The reason is risk control, not hit-rate: "I find it more difficult to control my loss with a failed break-out. Where should one place their stop loss? It is not always easy to designate that in advance."
- The contrast: "with the OSB (oversold bounce) set-up it is much easier to determine the price where the set-up has failed. **Place the stop just below the low of the bounce, the place where the black dot appears.**"
- The failure mode being avoided: CAVA "opened up above the prior day's high and closed back below its break-out line... if someone bought at Thursday's open they would have a large loss."
- "The black dot shows the days of the OSB set-up" — the dot is the setup marker, and therefore also the stop reference.
