# WW 2024-02-27 — The weekly green bar, in plain language

**Source:** [Blog Post: Day 34 of $QQQ short term up-trend; $ANF, an amazing pattern of an advancing growth stock, see weekly chart and explanation of this technical pattern, also $GCT](../../raw/posts/2024-02-27-blog-post-day-34-of-qqq-short-term-up-trend-anf-an-amazing-pattern-of-an-advancing-growth-stock-see-weekly-ch.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2024/02/blog-post-day-34-of-qqq-short-term-up-trend-anf-an-amazing-pattern-of-an-advancing-growth-stock-see-weekly-chart-and-explanation-of-this-technical-pattern-also-gct/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

**The WGB explained without the formula**, which is how it becomes usable. "I have found that **one of the best
patterns for an advancing stock is one where the 4wk average > 10wk avg > 30wk avg for several weeks and the
stock consistently *closes* the week above its 4 week average.**" Then the entry: "in such a stock **one could
buy when the stock closes the week after a bounce above its 4 wk average, designated by green bars** in this
chart. **A green bar signifies that it has traded below or at the 4wk average at some point during the week and
then closed the week back above it.**" And the exit: "**a sign of possible technical weakness would be if such a
stock *closes* the week below the 4 wk average.**" So the green bar is a *weekly shakeout that failed* — price
dipped to or through the fast average intraweek and recovered by Friday — which is why it marks a bounce rather
than a breakout, and why the same average supplies both the entry trigger and the exit condition.

## Key claims

- **The WGB is a failed weekly shakeout**: price traded at or below the 4-week average during the week and closed back above it.
- **The alignment is the precondition** (4wk > 10wk > 30wk for several weeks with consistent closes above the 4-week), the green bar is the trigger.
- **The same average carries the exit**: a weekly *close* below the 4-week is the weakness signal.
- Stated without the TC2000 formula, which is what makes it checkable by eye on any weekly chart.
