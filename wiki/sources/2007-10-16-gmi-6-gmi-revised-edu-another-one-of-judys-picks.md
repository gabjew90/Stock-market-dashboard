# WW 2007-10-16 — The GMI-R introduced — dated

**Source:** [GMI: 6; GMI Revised; EDU – another one of Judy’s picks](../../raw/posts/2007-10-16-gmi-6-gmi-revised-edu-another-one-of-judys-picks.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2007/10/gmi-6-gmi-revised-edu-another-one-of-judys-picks/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

**The GMI-R's introduction, dated.** "After much thought, **I have revised the GMI by adding 4 new indicators,
signified by an '\*'. The GMI-R ranges from 0-10 and may provide a more sensitive measure of the trend of the
general market. For the time being, I will post the original GMI and the revised GMI-R in the table for the
benefit of readers who use the original index.**" This is 16 October 2007 — the first appearance of "GMI-R"
anywhere in the corpus, and the wiki's glossary dated the index to 2005. Two design notes are stated with it: the
extra four are marked with an asterisk in the published table, and the original is kept alongside the revision
*because readers were already using it* — a compatibility decision, not an analytical one. Market context: the
GMI at its maximum of 6, 216 new highs, day 32 of a QQQQ up-trend, and T2108 down to 67% from 82% on 5 October —
three days after the October 2007 top. The post also introduces "**Judy**," a stock-picking friend whose research
on EDU (the "Kaplan of China") preceded a 12.56% earnings-day gain: "**I need to follow Judy's advice much more
often.**"

## Key claims

- **The GMI-R was introduced on 2007-10-16**, not in 2005 — four indicators added to the six, marked with an asterisk in the table, scored 0–10.
- Its stated purpose is sensitivity: "may provide **a more sensitive measure** of the trend of the general market."
- **Both indexes were published side by side deliberately**, "for the benefit of readers who use the original index" — backwards compatibility as an editorial obligation.
- T2108 at 67%, down from 82% on 5 October — the reading in the week of the 2007 top.
- "Judy's picks" begins here as a recurring feature: an outside stock-picker whose calls he tracks and credits.
