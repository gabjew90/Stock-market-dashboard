# WW 2011-08-15 — The August 2011 full exit: rules plus fatigue, sector confirmation, and the addiction line

**Source:** [100% cash in trading accounts and university pension](../../raw/posts/2011-08-15-100-cash-in-trading-accounts-and-university-pension.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2011/08/100-cash-in-trading-accounts-and-university-pension/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

The complete August 2011 exit, documented the day it finished — trading accounts weeks earlier,
the pension last. Unusually honest about the mixture of rules and emotion, and rich in
cross-confirmation technique: sector breadth read against the 2008 template, and the share of
index components below their 30-week averages used as the market-level stage check.

## Key claims

- **Rules first, feelings admitted:** "I know I should have done this in stages, but I just got
  tired of worrying about the risks... **I did not act solely because of my emotional fatigue,
  however. I am mainly acting on my established rules** that say that when the GMI is very weak
  and the major indexes are below their 30 week averages, I am supposed to be in cash." Sold
  into Friday's strength (mutual funds price at the close).
- **Sector confirmation against the 2008 template:** bank indexes breaking below their 2010
  support "just like they did in 2008... The last time I saw similar consistent breakdowns in
  banking stocks was in 2008 near the **beginning** of the financial crisis" — with the same
  pattern across brokerage, trucking, construction, steel, airlines.
- **Component-level stage check, quantified:** "the percentage of stocks closing *below* their
  critical 30 week long term averages are: Dow 30, **83%**; S&P500, **87%** and Nasdaq 100,
  **81%**." And the personal rule it feeds: "**I do not own *any* stock that is below its 30
  week average.**"
- **The pension-lag cost admitted:** the trading accounts exited weeks earlier with gains
  intact; the pension didn't "because of the penalties for market timing, and I did therefore
  lose some money."
- **Bottom mechanics:** "Most bottoms do not end with a sudden burst up. There usually is a
  **multi-week period of backing and filling** as a base is formed."
- **The addiction line:** "**If one cannot exit the market to go to cash, one should not be in
  the market at all.** Too many people are addicted to the rush of trading. But *all* of my
  trading gurus have written that there is a time to be out of the market."
