# WW 2018-06-17 — Two personal top tells; the 38-country ETF watchlist

**Source:** [World markets are topping– can the U.S. be far behind?](../../raw/posts/2018-06-17-world-markets-are-topping-can-the-u-s-be-far-behind.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2018/06/world-markets-are-topping-can-the-u-s-be-far-behind/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

**Two sentiment gauges taken from his own life, stated together as a pair.** "During my **50 years of
investing/trading** I have learned that **when my account grows suddenly very quickly** and when **many people
tell me they are making money in the market (I see this now in tweets and chat rooms), the market often rests or
tops.**" The first is unusual and worth noting: his *own* rate of gain read as a contrary indicator — a fast
account is evidence the tape is easy, and easy tapes end. The second is the crowd he can observe, updated for the
era (tweets and chat rooms rather than the meetup room). He also flags the speculative signature: "**too many
speculative stocks, especially recent IPOs, are quickly doubling** (IQ, HUYA are examples)." **The instrument
that turned mild concern into action**: "I have created a **watchlist of 38 country market index ETFs across the
world**. I decided to look at them this weekend. I **sorted them by whether each closed above its rising 30 week
moving average**" — the stage test applied across a global universe as a single breadth read, and the same
38-ETF list he would run the dual-timeframe RWB filter over four months later. His stance: hold through the
quarter-end window-dressing period, then reduce.

## Key claims

- **His own account rising quickly is read as a top tell** — a fast account means an easy tape, and easy tapes end.
- **The observable crowd, updated**: tweets and chat rooms replace the meetup room as the sample.
- **A 38-country ETF watchlist, sorted by the 30-week stage test** — the stage framework used as a global breadth instrument.
- Recent IPOs doubling quickly named as the speculative signature of a late tape.
- Action deferred to after quarter-end window dressing — the calendar effect used to time the exit, not the decision.
