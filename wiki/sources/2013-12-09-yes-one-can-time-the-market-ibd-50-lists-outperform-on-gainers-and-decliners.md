# WW 2013-12-09 — Yes, one can time the market; the IBD50 downside test

**Source:** [Yes, one can time the market!; IBD 50 lists outperform on gainers and decliners](../../raw/posts/2013-12-09-yes-one-can-time-the-market-ibd-50-lists-outperform-on-gainers-and-decliners.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2013/12/yes-one-can-time-the-market-ibd-50-lists-outperform-on-gainers-and-decliners/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

Three evidence artifacts in one post: the published GMI signal chart (2006–2013), a
**student-prompted downside test** of the IBD50 thesis, and a pointer to third-party tracking of
a GMI-based strategy. Also the sharpest statement of the pension-restriction grievance.

## Key claims

- **The timing thesis, with the whipsaw admission built in:** "I have been able to use a few
  simple indicators to keep me out of the major market declines... Yes, there are times when the
  GMI gets whip-sawed, **but only for a few days**. I do not mind going to cash and then
  re-entering." The red/green signal chart since 2006 is published for readers to "judge for
  yourself."
- **The incentive critique:** pundits say timing is impossible because "by making investing
  appear complex and beyond most people, **they protect their jobs** and the jobs of many
  financial advisers."
- **The downside test — a student challenge answered with data.** An honors student suggested
  the IBD50's extra big gainers might come with extra big decliners. He re-ran the study with a
  decliners column: **2–10% of IBD50 stocks fell 10%+**, versus Nasdaq100 8%, Dow 0%, S&P 3% —
  "the data do not support his hypothesis." The lists carry more 20–30%+ gainers *without* more
  large decliners. He publishes the caveat anyway: IBD-type growth stocks "do tend to decline
  more than other non-growth stocks **during major declining markets**."
- **Third-party verification exists:** he links dark-liquidity.com's tracked GMI→QLD strategy —
  an external record of the signal's performance, not his own bookkeeping.
- **The pension grievance:** "The problem is that my pension plan prevents me from market
  timing. They want me to ride the market down in a large decline. **I refuse to do so.**"
- **Sentiment vs breadth:** bullish sentiment was extreme, "But the Worden T2108 is only 48%!"
  — sentiment alone doesn't trump neutral breadth; he stays 100% invested in the pension until
  extremes show up in *his* indicators.
