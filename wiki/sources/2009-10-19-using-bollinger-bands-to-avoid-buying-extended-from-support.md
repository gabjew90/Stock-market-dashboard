# WW 2009-10-19 — Using Bollinger Bands to avoid buying extended from support

**Source:** [Using Bollinger bands to avoid buying  extended from support.](../../raw/posts/2009-10-19-using-bollinger-bands-to-avoid-buying-extended-from-support.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2009/10/using-bollinger-bands-to-avoid-buying-extended-from-support/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

The mistake he names as the most common among his honors students — and his own: "**the biggest mistake they make in trading is buying a stock when it is too extended from support. They buy when it is extended and then sell as it falls back to support. I did that a lot until I reviewed several losses and discovered that pattern. To make money, all I had to do was to reverse my actions — buy where I had been selling and sell where I had been buying.**" Bollinger Bands are the tool that makes the reversal operational, and here the setting is **20-day, 2 standard deviations** — the textbook setting, before he moved to his own 15.2 — with the statistics spelled out for students: the band is the average deviation of the last 20 closes around their mean, and in a normal distribution ~95% of closes fall within ±2 SD.

## Key claims

- The most common student error: buying extended from support, then selling into support — the exact inversion of the correct behaviour.
- Diagnosed by reviewing his own losses for a recurring pattern.
- **Bollinger setting in 2009 is the standard 20-day / 2 SD**, superseded later by his own 15.2.
- The bands are taught as a confidence interval, not a signal.
