# WW 2020-04-06 — Where the market gate came from — losing it all back in the 1960s

**Source:** [The GMI=0 and on a Red signal](../../raw/posts/2020-04-06-the-gmi0-and-on-a-red-signal.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2020/04/the-gmi0-and-on-a-red-signal/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

**The origin of the conviction, stated as a personal failure.** "**When I began trading in the 60s and knew
nothing about technical analysis, I would make money when the market moved up and then lose the profits and more
when the market receded.** It wasn't until I read **Nicolas Darvas' first book and later William O'Neil's book**
and his newspaper, IBD, **that I learned that to make money, one must trade consistent with the market's trend.
Both of these successful traders realized that most stocks follow the general trend of the market, as measured by
the market indexes.**" That is the whole system's premise with its biography attached — the gate exists because
he spent years giving back in declines what he made in advances, and two books told him why. He also credits IBD
with something unusual among financial publishers: "**Unlike the WSJ, IBD would actually advise readers to exit
the market, and still does.**" Willingness to say *get out* is what he values in a source, and it is what he does
himself on this blog.

## Key claims

- **The gate is autobiographical**: he made money in advances and lost it back in declines through the 1960s before reading Darvas and O'Neil.
- "**Most stocks follow the general trend of the market**" credited to both authors as the reason the gate works.
- **IBD valued specifically for telling readers to exit** — a thing he notes the WSJ does not do, and which he replicates.
- The failure mode he describes is the one every rule on this wiki addresses: giving back the advance's gains in the decline.
