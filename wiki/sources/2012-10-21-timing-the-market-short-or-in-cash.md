# WW 2012-10-21 — The two-tier timing rules, written to settle reader confusion

**Source:** [Timing the market; Short or in cash](../../raw/posts/2012-10-21-timing-the-market-short-or-in-cash.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2012/10/timing-the-market-short-or-in-cash/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

Written to settle reader confusion, and consequently the most explicit statement of the two-tier timing rules
anywhere in the corpus. The accounts first: "I have **two types of money**, my IRA and margin accounts in which I
am free to time the market versus my university pension which is invested in mutual funds that I cannot trade in
and out of at will. The first group I call my **speculative** money and the second group is my more
**conservative** money." Then each tier's trigger, and they are not the same shape. **Speculative — a
disjunction**: "**When my QQQ short term indicator turns down *or* the GMI goes below 3, I start to become more
cautious with my speculative funds. I may raise sell stops, move to cash or go short.**" **Conservative — a
conjunction, and a stricter one**: "**Only after the GMI has been 1 or 2 for a while *and* the longer term moving
averages curve down do I move my conservative money from mutual funds into money market funds.**" Both are
executed gradually — "**I tend to exit and reinvest in stages, not all at once**" — and the flow keeps running
after the balance has moved: "even after I move my conservative monies to a money market fund, **I continue
putting the new allocations into mutual funds so that the new money dollar cost averages**… I do that with
mutual funds, unlike individual stocks, because **I am pretty confident the mutual funds will rise again**."
Also here, an honest note on day-1 entries: had he bought SQQQ on the close of day 1 of the down-trend he would
have been up almost 12%, "**that is difficult to do, however, because on the first day of a new trend one does
not usually believe it will last**." And a third put/call threshold: "the put/call ratio was a little over 1.0 on
Friday. **Readings above 1.0 typically signal too much bearishness and an imminent bounce.**"

## Key claims

- **The speculative trigger is an OR** (QQQ trend down *or* GMI below 3) and admits three responses: raise stops, go to cash, or go short.
- **The pension trigger is an AND, and set lower**: the GMI at 1 or 2 *for a while*, plus the long-term averages curving down. Not "below 3" — 1 or 2.
- **Both tiers move in stages, in and out.** Staging is the default, not a special case.
- **The balance and the flow are separate decisions**: new contributions keep buying mutual funds through a decline because a fund, unlike a company, cannot go to zero.
- **Why day-1 entries are hard, said plainly**: on the first day of a new trend one does not believe it yet. The 12% forgone is the price of the disbelief.
- A third stated put/call threshold — "above 1.0" in 2012, against 1.2 in 2018 and 1.25 later that year.
