# WW 2023-06-06 — TNA — the leveraged default extended to small caps

**Source:** [Blog Post: Small-cap stocks have likely formed a bottom and started to advance, check out these weekly and daily charts for $IWM and $TNA, https://wp.me/pqq9l-6Lr](../../raw/posts/2023-06-06-blog-post-small-cap-stocks-have-likely-formed-a-bottom-and-started-to-advance-check-out-these-weekly-and-dail.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2023/06/blog-post-small-cap-stocks-have-likely-formed-a-bottom-and-started-to-advance-check-out-these-weekly-and-daily-charts-for-iwm-and-tna-https-wp-me-pqq9l-6lr/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

**The same instrument logic applied to a different index.** "**TNA is the triple leveraged bullish ETF for IWM,
the Russell 2000 index ETF. I prefer to trade TNA, like I trade TQQQ when QQQ is in an up-trend.** TNA advanced
+8% on Tuesday while IWM advanced +2.6%." So the leveraged-index default is not a QQQ strategy but a *method* —
identify a trend in an index, then express it through that index's 3× fund — and the arithmetic held on the day
(roughly 3× the move). The timing rationale is the bear-market leadership sequence he described in 2008, read
forward: "**Now that the tech stocks have moved up, it is fitting that people feel more comfortable buying the
small caps.**" Large caps first, speculative and small names later, once buyers have profits — so a small-cap
bottom *following* a tech advance is the expected order rather than a rotation away from tech.

## Key claims

- **TNA (3× IWM) used exactly as TQQQ is used for QQQ** — the leveraged default is a method, not a QQQ-specific strategy.
- Verified on the day: TNA +8% against IWM +2.6%.
- **Small caps bottoming after tech has advanced is the expected sequence**, per the 2008 recovery-leadership model, not a rotation signal.
- The index must be in an up-trend first; the fund amplifies a signal it does not supply.
