# WW 2005-04-20 — STOP THIS MADNESS: the GMI's components, six days before the GMI

**Source:** [STOP THIS MADNESS](../../raw/posts/2005-04-20-stop-this-madness.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2005/04/stop-this-madness/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

Written **six days before the GMI was named**, and it already describes three of its six
components as indicators he was using individually. The GMI was not invented in one go on
2005-04-26; it was a *packaging* of instruments he had already been running. This post is the
pre-history, and it explains the reasoning behind each one more fully than the founding post does.

Also his angriest piece of market writing, aimed at pundits telling people to buy the 2000–2002
decline.

## Key claims

- **Component 2, pre-GMI:** "if there are not at least 100 stocks on the NYSE or NASDAQ that are
  making new 52 week highs, the market is not strong enough."
- **Component 6, pre-GMI — and a fact the wiki did not have:** the IBD mutual fund index "tracks
  the performance of **23 growth mutual funds**." The rationale is stated in full: "if this index
  is below its 50 day moving average, then I cannot make money trading growth stocks. In other
  words, **if the pros running these funds cannot make money I will not.** These managers are the
  ones who drive these growth stocks higher with their huge resources."
- **Component 1's origin story, with numbers:** he had built the Successful 10-Day index in
  TC2005 over the ~4,000-stock universe. "When I first computed this index in **March**, I found
  that over 100 stocks met these criteria. Today, there were only **12 stocks out of 4,000**."
  This is where the "greater than 100" threshold of the founding post comes from — it is the
  March reading, not a derived figure.
- **A meta-signal the wiki did not have:** "**A good indication that things are souring is when
  the types of trades I have been profiting from suddenly produce a string of losses.**" Your own
  hit rate is an indicator.
- **The regime-dependence of any strategy:** "a hard lesson to learn is that **the strategy that
  does beautifully in a bull market fails miserably in a bear market**," citing Darvas.
- **The 2000 exit, stated plainly:** "I exited the market in the fall of 2000."
- **On the lag in public recognition:** "the market seers do not declare a bull or bear market
  until about 6 months after it has begun."
- **A full rebuttal of the "you'll miss the best N days" argument** against market timing — that a
  100-point up day is usually partly retraced, giving you a re-entry; that nobody is unlucky
  enough to miss all the big days; and "There is always time to catch a genuine bull move." He
  calls the standard framing "absurd."
- His own positioning at the time: "I am mainly short and in cash. **Nothing feels better than to
  be in synch with the trend.**"
