# WW 2014-12-23 — The bond–rate relationship, taught with the arithmetic

**Source:** [3rd day of $QQQ short term up-trend; double top in bonds?](../../raw/posts/2014-12-23-3rd-day-of-qqq-short-term-up-trend-double-top-in-bonds.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2014/12/3rd-day-of-qqq-short-term-up-trend-double-top-in-bonds/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

The teaching version of the rate thesis that runs through the whole corpus — written out as he gives it to
students, with numbers. "**Over my 40+ years of trading I have learned that the one thing that can kill a bull
market is the Fed's raising interest rates.**" Then the mechanism, which he notes "most people find difficult to
understand": "Say a bond is issued at \$100 and it pays the holder \$3 per year. That \$3 is **fixed for the
life of the bond**… its yield = 3% each year (3/100). Now say that interest rates rise over a few years to 6%.
Then new \$100 bonds issued will pay \$6 per year (= 6%). **Someone holding the older bonds (that pay \$3 per
year), if he wanted to sell his bond early and get his money back, would likely be offered only about \$50 for
his bond which he had bought for \$100.**" The fixed coupon divided by a falling price is what produces the
competitive yield. This is the arithmetic underneath every "lower bond prices mean higher interest rates"
statement elsewhere — and underneath the rate-reversal thesis, since it is *also* why a rate rise marks down the
bonds retirees hold.

## Key claims

- **The bond–rate inverse relationship worked out numerically**, as he teaches it: a \$100 bond paying \$3 is worth ~\$50 when new bonds pay 6%.
- **"The one thing that can kill a bull market is the Fed's raising interest rates"** — stated as the single most important lesson of 40+ years.
- The fixed coupon is the key: the payment cannot adjust, so the *price* must.
- This is the arithmetic behind reading TLT, IEF and SHY as rate proxies — the ETF price is the mechanism, not a proxy for it.
- It also explains the I-bond preference: an inflation-adjusting instrument is the one form of safe money that does not get marked down.
