# WW 2017-06-25 — How I use Yellowband up- and down-trends

**Source:** [How I use Yellowband up and down trends; $FIZZ, $SHOP, $IGT, $JCP, $QQQ](../../raw/posts/2017-06-25-how-i-use-yellowband-up-and-down-trends-fizz-shop-igt-jcp-qqq.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2017/06/how-i-use-yellowband-up-and-down-trends-fizz-shop-igt-jcp-qqq/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

The definitional post for **yellowband**, and a correction to this wiki. The glossary described
yellowband as an *earlier* term he moved away from; this post — a "My Favorite Posts" entry —
presents it in June 2017 as a **primary holding framework**, the thing he checks to avoid being
shaken out.

## Key claims

- **Definition, precisely:** "A yellowband up-trend is a stock on a weekly chart that closes
  repeatedly **above its rising 10 week average that in turn is consistently above its rising
  30 week average**. A yellowband down-trend is simply the reverse." Note both averages must be
  *rising*, not merely stacked.
- **Provenance:** "a key chart pattern that I noticed in the 1990s"; "I discovered the value of
  the yellowband pattern in the 90s." It predates the GMI and the RWB work.
- **It is a selection filter as well as a holding rule:** "I primarily buy stocks that are above
  or near their last green line break-out (GLB) **that have a yellowband up-trend**."
- **The hold rule:** "I try not to sell as long as the yellowband is intact **unless I see
  unusually high volume selling or a climax top**."
- **The exit:** "A close below the 10 week average is a significant technical signal for me to
  exit or reduce my position."
- **Why it matters to him:** "If I had diligently checked a stock's yellowband pattern I would
  not have been shaken out and exited many great stocks too early during the past few years."
  The pattern's job is preventing premature exits — the opposite failure from the one stops
  address.
- Applied to indexes too: QQQ was "in a yellowband up-trend and recently found support at the
  10 week average, a good place for me to enter or add to a position."
- Worked charts: FIZZ, SHOP, IGT (up-trends), JCP (down-trend), QQQ.

## Tension to note

Six months later ([WW 2017-12-17](2017-12-17-a-strategy-for-decidng-when-to-sell-stocks-gds-nvda.md)) he compares yellowband against
the daily RWB system and concludes a weekly close below the 10-week average "gave back too much
profit," shifting to daily-RWB red-line closes as his primary *exit*. The two are compatible if
read as different jobs — yellowband decides whether a stock is still a hold, daily RWB decides
when to leave — but the wiki previously recorded only the December view, and framed yellowband
as superseded. It was not.
