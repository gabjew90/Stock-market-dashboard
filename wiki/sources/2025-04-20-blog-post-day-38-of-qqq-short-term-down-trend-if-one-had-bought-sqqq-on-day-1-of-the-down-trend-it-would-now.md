## What it covers

The leveraged-ETF default stated symmetrically: "if I can buy TQQQ on Day 1 of a new QQQ short term up-trend and if the up-trend lasts, it beats almost all individual stocks. **Similarly, SQQQ beats almost all individual stocks in a sustained down-trend.** The key is to buy on Day 1 and hold, but to sell quickly if the trend reverses." On waiting out a decline: "if one got out when I said I was in February, one can just calmly wait out the decline. I do not have to reinvest at the bottom, just return in a new up-trend before the market rises to the point I got out. **Stop trying to predict a bottom and wait for it to define itself.**" The horizon caveat: young investors can dollar-cost-average into SPY through a decline; "people near retirement may not have adequate time. It took 25 years for the DOW to come back to the 1929 top." And the selection rule restated: "like Nicolas Darvas and William O'Neil, [I] buy only when a stock trades at or near an ATH. I am not interested in buying the fallen leaders until or **IF** they ever trade at an ATH."

## Key claims

- SQQQ on Day 1 of a down-trend is the mirror of TQQQ on Day 1 of an up-trend — buy Day 1, hold, sell quickly on reversal.
- "Stop trying to predict a bottom and wait for it to define itself."
- Re-entry needs only to precede the level you exited at — not the low.
- Age determines whether riding a decline is affordable (the 1929 → 25-year recovery).
- Stocks reaching an ATH early in an up-trend "are proving themselves worthy of my attention and money"; fallen leaders are not bought until they print an ATH.
