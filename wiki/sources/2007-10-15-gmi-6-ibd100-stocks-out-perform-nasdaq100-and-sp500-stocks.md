# WW 2007-10-15 — It is futile to anticipate market turns — and the price of waiting

**Source:** [GMI: 6; IBD100 stocks out-perform Nasdaq100 and S&P500 stocks](../../raw/posts/2007-10-15-gmi-6-ibd100-stocks-out-perform-nasdaq100-and-sp500-stocks.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2007/10/gmi-6-ibd100-stocks-out-perform-nasdaq100-and-sp500-stocks/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

The cost of his own discipline, stated plainly at a GMI of 6 with breadth already thinning (125 new highs, down from 484 two days earlier; T2108 74% off an 82% high — "this Worden indicator can take a long time to top out as it remains in the 70-80 area"). "**I have learned over the years that it is futile to try to anticipate market turns. It is far better to wait until my GMI signals the turn has occurred. Of course, this means that I lose some profits as the top is formed. However, I prefer to take this approach over trying repeatedly (and mistakenly) to predict the turn.**" Duration context: the up-trend was at day 31, and "the last two recent large up-trends lasted 80 days and 86 days, suggesting that this QQQQ up-trend could have further to go." He also rebuts the claim that IBD 100 stocks top out once listed — "unlike most people with answers, I have data."

## Key claims

- The trade-off named without flinching: waiting for the GMI means giving back some of the top.
- "It is futile to try to anticipate market turns" — the cost of the alternative is repeated wrong guesses.
- T2108 can sit in the 70–80 band for a long time before topping.
- Early duration base rates: two recent large up-trends of 80 and 86 days.
- "Unlike most people with answers, I have data."
