# WW 2022-12-29 — A survivorship critique of the pattern-book literature

**Source:** [Blog Post: Day 9 of $QQQ short term down-trend; GLBs often fail in market down-trends–$CALM is a prime example we can learn from](../../raw/posts/2022-12-29-blog-post-day-9-of-qqq-short-term-down-trend-glbs-often-fail-in-market-down-trends-calm-is-a-prime-example-we.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2022/12/blog-post-day-9-of-qqq-short-term-down-trend-glbs-often-fail-in-market-down-trends-calm-is-a-prime-example-we-can-learn-from/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

**The sharpest methodological criticism in the corpus, and it is aimed at books he otherwise recommends.** "I
have often taught that **the same set-ups that work beautifully for me in a rising market can fail miserably in a
declining market. Model books of rising stocks can be very misleading because, by definition, most set-ups worked
for these winners. With these model stocks that rise for months one could just buy every Monday or every x day
and make money. There is nothing special about the set-ups. The authors never tell you how often the highlighted
set-ups can fail.**" That is a survivorship argument applied to the entire chart-pattern genre: a book of winners
selects on the outcome, so *every* entry rule looks good inside it — including arbitrary ones. The missing
denominator is the failure rate, which no such book supplies. He then supplies one himself, in the same post:
CALM had a GLB to an all-time high (its green line set in October 2015) and was "**one of just 5 stocks trading
Friday at an ATH**"; days later it traded above its upper 15.2 Bollinger Band and reversed to close down. A
failure published beside the rule, which is exactly what he says the books omit.

## Key claims

- **Model books select on the outcome**, so every set-up in them appears to work — "one could just buy every Monday or every x day and make money. There is nothing special about the set-ups."
- **"The authors never tell you how often the highlighted set-ups can fail"** — the missing denominator, named.
- The same criticism applies to his own GLB Tracker, which he elsewhere admits is a survivor list.
- He answers it in the same post by publishing a failure (CALM) rather than only successes.
- The regime qualifier restated: set-ups that work in a rising market "can fail miserably in a declining market."
