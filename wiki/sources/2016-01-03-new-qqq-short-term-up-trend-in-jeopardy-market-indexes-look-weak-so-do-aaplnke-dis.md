# WW 2016-01-03 — Never hold a long position in a BWR down-trend; the market read index by index

**Source:** [New $QQQ short term up-trend in jeopardy; Market indexes look weak; So do $AAPL,$NKE, $DIS](../../raw/posts/2016-01-03-new-qqq-short-term-up-trend-in-jeopardy-market-indexes-look-weak-so-do-aaplnke-dis.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2016/01/new-qqq-short-term-up-trend-in-jeopardy-market-indexes-look-weak-so-do-aaplnke-dis/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

The GMMA framework applied across six charts on one evening, and the rule stated in its most absolute form:
"**I would never hold a long position in a stock or ETF in a BWR down-trend.**" The survey is the method:
Dow Transports in a confirmed BWR after "a strong RWB up-trend [that] lasted for several years, through early
2015" — "an ominous pattern for those who follow the Dow theory"; the NYSE index "definitely in a BWR
down-trend"; the energy ETF XLE confirmed BWR, with "**no one but a gambler would try to pick a bottom here**";
the Dow 30 and SPY out of RWB but not yet BWR; the QQQ "bearly? remains in an RWB up-trend." The conclusion is
a split market — energy and multinationals broken, tech still holding — and the summary line for why the
pattern matters at all: "**How much easier (and more profitable) it is to hold stocks when the indexes are in
strong RWB up-trends!**" The leader tell appears again on NKE, which sold off after a good earnings report:
"**When leaders cannot rise on good news, it is telling us something about the inner strength (or lack thereof)
of the market**" — with DIS unable to rally on the release of Star Wars as the second instance. AAPL is read as
completing a weekly head-and-shoulders top with an objective near 80. The GMI was on a Sell while the QQQ was
on day U-2 of an up-trend — the two disagreeing, with the slower one governing.

## Key claims

- **"I would never hold a long position in a stock or ETF in a BWR down-trend"** — the RWB/BWR rule at its strongest, and applied to ETFs as well as stocks.
- The GMMA is a **survey instrument**: run across index and sector ETFs it shows which parts of the market have broken, which is how he identifies a split market rather than a uniform one.
- A BWR pattern is a bottom-picking prohibition, not merely a hold prohibition.
- Dow Theory is read through the GMMA — the Transports' BWR is treated as the confirming weakness.
- The leader-on-good-news tell restated eleven years after its first appearance, on NKE and DIS.
