# WW 2005-05-04 — A Google Confession

**Source:** [A Google Confession — WW-GMI: +1](../../raw/posts/2005-05-04-a-google-confession-ww-gmi-1.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2005/05/a-google-confession-ww-gmi-1/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

A worked GOOG trade used to teach entry, stop placement and emotional detachment — and framed
as a **confession**, because he took it while telling readers to stay in cash. One of his "My
Favorite Posts." Contains the earliest statement of the **gap rule** that becomes the 2022
gap-up scan, and the clearest explanation of *why* Darvas's already-doubled criterion works.

## Key claims

- **The already-doubled rationale, stated as a principle:** "Darvas wrote that one thing he
  looked for in a stock was a doubling in the past year. **The best predictor of a person's
  behavior is his/her past behavior. The same is true for stocks.** Want to find a stock that
  will double in the next year — find one that has already doubled in the past year." With the
  corollary: "Rockets keep doubling and hitting new highs and **always appear too expensive**."
- **The gap rule — the conceptual origin of the [gap-up scan](../methodology/glossary.md):** "many
  stocks begin their advance or decline with a huge gap in price. **The trick is to wait to see
  if the gap is filled.** If the stock keeps on rising to new heights without closing the gap, it
  is often a sign of tremendous strength." With a counter-example on the same chart: a January
  2005 gap that "was quickly filled — and failed." He scans for gaps in TC2005.
- **Risk/reward stated numerically:** bought GOOG ~$220, stop ~$214 — "willing to take the risk
  of losing about $7 per share in exchange for the possibility of a profit of $50 or more."
- **Why the stop is placed immediately:** "I have placed my wager and **can now separate myself
  emotionally from the stock**. I will either profit or lose a little." And: "**If you are
  unwilling to have a lot of small losses you should not trade stocks.**"
- **Distance as a discipline:** "Darvas made his fortune when he was out of the country and far
  away from the market. **The further one is away from the market, the less the emotion that can
  kill one's judgment**, and the trade." He notes he doesn't watch GOOG much.
- **Stop-order mechanics, with the gap caveat spelled out:** a stop to sell at $214 "becomes a
  market order to sell as soon as the stock trades at or below the stop price. This does not
  guarantee the price I will get" — if it gapped from above $214 to $210, "I would be sold out
  at the next price, probably around $210."
- **The pilot buy and the prohibition on averaging down, in capitals:** "Livermore and Darvas
  would make a small pilot buy and then add to the position only if the stock rose, proving them
  correct in their first purchase. **NEVER BUY MORE OF A STOCK THAT HAS DECLINED — NEVER THROW
  GOOD $$$ AFTER BAD.** Just take your loss, admit you were wrong and learn from your mistake."
- **Honest about the rule-break:** "I have a confession tonight... I could not resist nibbling at
  a stock that was resisting the downtrend."
- Re-entry posture with the GMI at +1: "I am ready to turn on a dime... If it is a real change in
  trend, **I will have plenty of time** to gradually take on my line of stocks."
