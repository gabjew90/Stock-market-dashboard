# WW 2017-12-10 — When to sell — the 13 averages, the gray line, and a graded exit

**Source:** [When to sell–signs an up-trend is weakening; $SQ $IPGP but $SPY is in a daily RWB up-trend](../../raw/posts/2017-12-10-when-to-sell-signs-an-up-trend-is-weakening-sq-ipgp-but-spy-is-in-a-daily-rwb-up-trend.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2017/12/when-to-sell-signs-an-up-trend-is-weakening-sq-ipgp-but-spy-is-in-a-daily-rwb-up-trend/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

**The sell side of the daily RWB chart, specified in full** — and he opens by naming it as the hard problem:
"**the most difficult decision in trading is when to sell a stock that has advanced after purchase.**" The chart
itself: "**my modified GMMA consists of 13 *exponential* moving averages (1, 3, 5, 8, 10, 12, 15, 30, 35, 40, 45,
50, 60). The '1' period average is a gray dotted line and represents the daily close.** The 3-15 shorter averages
are red and the longer averages (30+) are in blue." **The gray line is the signal**: "in an up-trend, the gray
dotted line will be mainly on top, leading all of the red lines higher. **When the gray line *closes* below all
of the red lines it is a sign of weakness and I consider selling or lightening up on my position**… **I try not
to sell a stock when the gray dotted line is holding up.**" **The rules, as he lists them**: "**Only go long
stocks in a daily RWB up-trend, and consider raising stops, selling or reducing my position when the stock
*closes* (preferably 2 consecutive days) below all of the red lines. Sell the entire position when the RWB
pattern totally disappears.**" That is a **graded exit** — reduce on the close below the red lines, liquidate
when the white space goes — matching the two-tier index reading. **And the reason the 3:45 PM habit exists**:
"daily *closes* are stressed for signals because **stocks often trade below the red lines during the day but
recover to close back above them. I therefore like to trade near the market's close so I know how the stock will
likely close.**" Worked examples: SQ (dotted line below the red lines for the first time since a single day in
September, then the white space gone) and IPGP (signal 11/29), with MRK as a Dow 30 stock already in a daily
BWR.

## Key claims

- **All 13 averages listed** (1, 3, 5, 8, 10, 12, 15, 30, 35, 40, 45, 50, 60), exponential, with the "1" as the gray dotted close line.
- **The gray line is the trigger, not the bands**: a *close* below all six red lines is the weakness signal, preferably two consecutive.
- **A graded exit**: reduce or raise stops on the close below the red lines; sell the whole position only when the RWB pattern itself disappears.
- **"I try not to sell a stock when the gray dotted line is holding up"** — a rule against exiting on band compression alone.
- **The 3:45 PM habit is derived, not arbitrary**: signals are defined on closes, so trading near the close is what makes the rule executable.
- Only buy stocks in a daily RWB up-trend, with a weekly RWB preferred underneath it.
- "The most difficult decision in trading is when to sell a stock that has advanced after purchase."
