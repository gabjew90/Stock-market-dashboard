# WW 2015-09-20 — Stage 4 defined, and missed upside priced as insurance

**Source:** [Market Stage 4 down-trend deepens–mainly in cash; $ZLTQ–GLB holding](../../raw/posts/2015-09-20-market-down-trend-deepens-mainly-in-cash-zltq-glb-holding.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2015/09/market-down-trend-deepens-mainly-in-cash-zltq-glb-holding/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

**Stage 4 defined precisely, and the cost of acting on it priced as insurance.** The definition: "An
up-trending market should have the pattern of the 10 week average rising above the 30 week average. **When the 10
week average falls below the 30 week average and the 30 week average turns down, that index is in a Stage 4
down-trend. Once a Stage 4 decline begins, one never knows how long it will last. I therefore exit the market and
sometimes buy the relevant inverse index ETF.**" The precedent: "these charts are exhibiting patterns similar to
those that occurred at the *beginning* of the major declines in 2000 and 2007. **These patterns got me out of the
market early in those major declines and they are telling me to exit now.**" And the framing that makes a false
signal tolerable: "**If the current declines are much more shallow than the others and I miss part of a rebound,
that is fine with me. That is the cost of my insurance against a huge loss. There is plenty of time for me to
re-enter the markets once the coast is clear** and I am confident that a new Stage 2 up-trend has begun. **Only a
stock addict thinks s/he must always be in the game.**" The enabling condition is named in the same paragraph: "I
trade in a tax deferred account and do not worry that selling out will trigger taxable events." Dispersion: DIA
and SPY declining harder than the QQQ, "which is now catching up (or down!)". ZLTQ is the stock held through it,
found by scuttlebutt — a friend who had undergone the procedure — and holding its GLB in a weak tape.

## Key claims

- **Stage 4 requires both conditions**: the 10-week falling below the 30-week *and* the 30-week turning down.
- **Missed upside is priced as a premium, not counted as a loss**: "that is the cost of my insurance against a huge loss."
- "**Only a stock addict thinks s/he must always be in the game**" — being out is a position, not an absence of one.
- The 2000 and 2007 onsets used as the pattern template, with the claim that the same pattern got him out early both times.
- The tax-deferred account named again as what makes a false exit cheap.
- A stock holding its GLB during a market decline is the thing worth watching — and this one was found by talking to a friend who had used the product.
