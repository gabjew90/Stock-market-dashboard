## What it covers

Two charts with the same shape and the same rule. QQQ sits just below its green-line top and above its rising 4-week average, with the 10-week rising toward a nearly flat 30-week — "**something has to give. We could have a definitive signal this week.**" His response: "**Rather than predicting the future, I prefer to react to a break-out or break-down, once it has occurred.**" CRDO shows the same pattern, with a high-volume rise after earnings three weeks earlier — "a promising sign" — and "**CRDO has closed the last 9 weeks above its rising 4 week average, a major sign of a rising stock.**" The GLB rule restated with its strictest wording: "if I buy a GLB, **I must sell it if it closes any day back below its green line**. If it does and I exit, I often buy it back if/when it closes back above its green line." GMI Green at 6.

## Key claims

- The GLB exit at its strictest: sell if it closes **any day** back below the green line.
- Consecutive weekly closes above a rising 4-week average as a strength measure (9 weeks for CRDO).
- A rising 10-week converging on a flat 30-week read as a pending resolution — "something has to give."
- The same green-line analysis applied to an index and a stock in one post.
- High-volume post-earnings rise as a supporting sign before the breakout.
