# WW 2020-01-20 — The GMMA drawn with the prices invisible

**Source:** [Blog post: How the market will show me when this up-trend is over](../../raw/posts/2020-01-20-blog-post-how-the-market-will-show-me-when-this-up-trend-is-over.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2020/01/blog-post-how-the-market-will-show-me-when-this-up-trend-is-over/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

**The naked-chart technique from 2005, applied to the GMMA — and it is deliberate.** "My modified weekly GMMA
has 12 exponential weekly moving averages plus a one period average which indicates each weekly close. The six
shorter term averages are red and the six longer averages are blue. The 13th one period average is a gray dotted
line. **All averages are drawn on a white background with the actual prices in white and therefore invisible.**"
The price bars are not omitted — they are *rendered in white so they cannot be seen*. What remains is the fan and
the closing line, which is exactly the reduction he performed by hand on the 10/30 chart in August 2005: remove
the thing you are tempted to argue with, leave the relationship. It also explains why every RWB rule in the
corpus is phrased in terms of the close relative to the averages rather than in terms of price action. The
framing around it is the standing one: "**the truth is no one knows when an up-trend will end until after it has
ended. That is what trend followers try to discern. We hop on, *after* a defined bottom, and off, *after* a
defined top.**"

## Key claims

- **The prices are drawn in white — deliberately invisible** — so the chart shows only the 12 averages and the closing line.
- The same reduction as the 2005 "naked chart": remove what invites argument, keep the relationship.
- This is why every RWB rule is stated as *close versus averages* rather than as price action.
- "We hop on, *after* a defined bottom, and off, *after* a defined top" — the trend-follower's bargain in one line.
