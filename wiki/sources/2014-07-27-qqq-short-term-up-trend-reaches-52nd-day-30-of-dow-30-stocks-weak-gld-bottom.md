# WW 2014-07-27 — 'Proprietary, so don't ask' — and the conditional duration table

**Source:** [$QQQ short term up-trend reaches 52nd day; 30% of $DOW 30 stocks weak; $GLD bottom?](../../raw/posts/2014-07-27-qqq-short-term-up-trend-reaches-52nd-day-30-of-dow-30-stocks-weak-gld-bottom.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2014/07/qqq-short-term-up-trend-reaches-52nd-day-30-of-dow-30-stocks-weak-gld-bottom/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

**He states plainly that the trend definition is not published**: the up-trend "that, **according to my
definition (proprietary, so don't ask for it)**, began on May 13 has now extended to 52 trading days." That is
worth recording as a fact about the corpus — every duration statistic on this wiki is conditional on a rule he
deliberately withheld, which is why "as I define them" keeps appearing beside the numbers and why the
reconstruction in `src/ww/` can only approximate it. **A conditional statistic**: "while two of the up-trends
since 2006 have lasted 80 and 88 days, **of the 11 up-trends that lasted 50 to 88 days, 45% ended by day 54. So
we are in nosebleed territory at day 52.**" Note what that does — it conditions on having *already* reached day
50, which is the right way to ask "how old is too old" and is a different question from the unconditional
distribution. He also guards against over-reading it: "**a short term up-trend could end, pause and then begin
anew.** The prior short term down-trend lasted just 4 days." Concern that day: **9 of the Dow 30 (30%)** below
their 10-week averages. Trading account mostly in profit-taking mode; pension fully invested.

## Key claims

- **The trend definition is deliberately unpublished** — "proprietary, so don't ask for it" — which is the standing caveat on every duration figure in the corpus.
- **A conditional survival statistic**: given an up-trend has reached day 50, 45% of the 11 such cases ended by day 54.
- Conditioning on having reached day 50 is the correct form of the "is this trend old?" question.
- **An ended trend is not the same as a finished move** — it "could end, pause and then begin anew," which is what makes the table conservative.
- Dow-30 breadth (9 of 30 below their 10-week averages) used as the corroborating worry.
