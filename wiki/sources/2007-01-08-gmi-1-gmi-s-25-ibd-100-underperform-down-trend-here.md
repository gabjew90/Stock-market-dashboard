# WW 2007-01-08 — A low put/call as a topping tell; scaling into inverse ETFs

**Source:** [GMI: 1; GMI-S: 25; IBD 100 underperform; Down-trend here?](../../raw/posts/2007-01-08-gmi-1-gmi-s-25-ibd-100-underperform-down-trend-here.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2007/01/gmi-1-gmi-s-25-ibd-100-underperform-down-trend-here/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

Three things. **The put/call ratio read from its low end**: "according to Monday's IBD, the put/call ratio on
Friday was .41, close to the lowest reading reached in the past 12 months (.38 on March 16, 2006). **A low p/c
ratio suggests relatively fewer people bearish enough to buy puts, a contrary indicator, often signaling
impending market weakness.**" The wiki's other put/call citations are all high readings read as bounce warnings;
this is the same instrument at the opposite pole. **Component 6 stated in the negative**: with the IBD Growth
Mutual Fund Index below its 50-day average, "**when these fund managers are not doing well, I do not make money
buying growth stocks**" — the mirror of the 2009 formulation, and two years earlier. **And the scaling rule for
the short side**, written while taking a loss: "I know I took a loss last week on my prior QID purchases, but
this market appears to be in transition with considerable whipsawing going on… **Right now, the prudent course
for me is to make small bets on the down-trend with close protective sell stops, in case the market moves up. If
the down-trend deepens, I will accumulate the inverse ETF on the weaker index.**" The whipsaw is priced in by
precedent: "Bernard Baruch wrote that he often times bought and sold stocks near the top in 1929, as he tried to
catch the major trend." The IBD 100 audit continues across six lists — 19–26% rising against 32% of the
Nasdaq-100, and only 35% of the 12/18 list above its publication-day price.

## Key claims

- **A low put/call ratio is a topping tell** — too few people frightened enough to buy insurance. The instrument is two-sided and he uses both ends.
- **Component 6 in the negative**: when the growth-fund managers are losing, he expects to lose buying the same stocks.
- **Scale into a new down-trend, do not commit to it**: small inverse-ETF positions with close protective stops, added to only if the decline deepens.
- **Choose the inverse ETF on the weaker index** — the selection rule inside the hedge, not just the sizing rule.
- Repeated small losses catching a turn are accepted as the cost of catching it, with Baruch in 1929 as the precedent.
- GMI-S at 25 across SPY, DIA, QQQQ and IJR, corroborating the 16-test construction.
