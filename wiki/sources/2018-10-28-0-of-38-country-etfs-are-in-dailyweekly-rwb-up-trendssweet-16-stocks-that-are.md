# WW 2018-10-28 — A dual-timeframe RWB filter, and what it returned in a bear market

**Source:** [0 of 38 country ETFs are in daily+weekly RWB up-trends;”Sweet 16″ stocks that are](../../raw/posts/2018-10-28-0-of-38-country-etfs-are-in-dailyweekly-rwb-up-trendssweet-16-stocks-that-are.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2018/10/0-of-38-country-etfs-are-in-dailyweekly-rwb-up-trendssweet-16-stocks-that-are/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

**A dual-timeframe screen built as a TC2000 column**: "**I created a column filter in TC2000 to detect
equities with RWB up-trends on *both* their daily and weekly timeframes.**" Run against two universes in the
October 2018 decline it produces base rates that are themselves the finding. On the 38 world-market ETFs he
follows: "**I was startled to find that none of the 38 world ETFs I follow have both weekly and daily RWB
up-trend patterns**… **34 of the 38 country ETFs (89%) are in weekly BWR down-trends!**" — with China "a major
double top" and the inference that "the U.S. markets are just catching up to them, and may have a long way to
fall." On US stocks: "**just 62 of 4,938 stocks (1.3%) in the US database met my criteria**," of which 16 were up
80%+ from a year earlier — the "Sweet 16." **The teaching admission is the most interesting part.** His students
"are learning to trade virtual margin accounts of \$100,000 and **must stay 90% invested**. They are
experiencing many losses while trying to go long growth stocks using the set-ups I have taught them. **I did
teach them that these set-ups only work in rising markets, and they are learning this valuable lesson**" — a
forced-exposure exercise designed to teach regime-dependence by making it expensive — and he applies the same
caveat to his own filter: "**this filter might work much better when in a rising market!**" **A T2108 base rate**:
with the reading below 10%, "this monthly chart shows how rare it has been for the T2108 to be below 10% since
2008… T2108 did reach 4% in 2016 and 1% in 2008. **From its inception in 1986 to 2007, T2108 had only 7 other
declines that reached below 10%.**" And the countervailing read: "**most bear markets *begin* with the 30 week
average curving down (Stage IV), which has not occurred. If it does, then this decline is just getting
started.**"

## Key claims

- **A column filter requiring RWB on the daily *and* weekly charts simultaneously** — the strictest form of the pattern, expressed as a scan.
- **Its yield is the market reading**: 1.3% of 4,938 US stocks, and 0 of 38 country ETFs, in October 2018.
- **T2108 below 10% is rare in an absolute sense**: only 7 such declines from 1986 to 2007, plus 2008 (1%) and 2016 (4%).
- **Students are required to stay 90% invested** in a virtual account — a deliberate device that teaches regime-dependence through loss rather than assertion.
- He applies the same caveat to his own screen that he taught them about theirs: "this filter might work much better when in a rising market."
- **A bear market is defined by the 30-week curving down**, and its absence is used to argue the decline was *not* yet a bear market.
- Above-average down volume for four consecutive weeks flagged as an ominous sign on the weekly chart.
