# WW 2011-08-22 — Every multi-week Dow decline since 1916, counted

**Source:** [Current market decline is not unusual](../../raw/posts/2011-08-22-current-market-decline-is-nothing-unusual.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2011/08/current-market-decline-is-nothing-unusual/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

**An original historical study, with its definition, its n and its own health warning.** "I decided to look at
the long multi-week declines of the **Dow 30 Index since 1916. I computed the duration and depth of every
multi-week decline** that has occurred. **I defined a multi-week decline as one when the Dow declined at least
15% in a few weeks without a consolidation along the way** — in other words, a steep, unceasing decline. **My
calculations were quick and dirty so do not hold me to them exactly. They still paint a valid picture.**" The
findings: the Dow had just fallen ~15% in 4 weeks, and "since 1916, there were **4 other declines that lasted
just 4 weeks and lost at least 15%** — they occurred in **1916, 1931, 1987, 2001**"; and, across the whole table,
"**30 of these 33 declines were greater than the current 15%. So this decline may have a lot further to go.**"
**The argument it was built to settle**: "one of the most stupid ideas I have heard is to suggest that the
current market decline is unique and caused by high frequency traders or program traders. **Human psychology has
not changed and market behaviors continually repeat themselves**… **How many of these declines that occurred
before the PC can we blame on program trading or high frequency trading!**" And the framing underneath it all:
"**the market is a collection of people and machines voting with their money millions of times each day. Trying
to predict their actions is a foolish endeavor.**" On being out: "sometimes **it is just relaxing to be away from
the market**. In declines I continue to monitor the new high list for potential leaders in the next up-trend." 

## Key claims

- **33 multi-week Dow declines since 1916**, defined as ≥15% in a few weeks with no consolidation — his own dataset, his own definition, and his own caveat that it is "quick and dirty."
- **30 of 33 were deeper than 15%**, used as the argument that a 15% decline is not obviously finished.
- Four precedents for a 4-week, 15% decline: 1916, 1931, 1987, 2001.
- **"This time is different" refuted by counting**: declines of the same shape predate the computers blamed for them.
- "The market is a collection of people and machines voting with their money millions of times each day" — the same anti-prediction premise as "value is a myth," stated mechanically.
- Being out of the market is described as restful, not merely correct — and the new-high list keeps running while he waits.
