# WW 2014-06-08 — What a market top looks like

**Source:** [What a market top looks like](../../raw/posts/2014-06-08-what-a-market-top-looks-like.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2014/06/what-a-market-top-looks-like/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

The doctrine that underwrites the whole system's exit-latency defence: **major tops are slow.**
He publishes monthly GMMA charts of SPY at the 2000 and 2008 tops as visual templates, against
the pundits then predicting an imminent top by fundamentals.

## Key claims

- **The core sentence:** "Market tops take months to develop, **leaving plenty of time for the
  watchful investor to exit the market.**" This is why a trend-following system that never
  calls tops (see the October 2007 and February 2020 snapshots in
  [track-record.md](../history/track-record.md)) can still avoid the majority of every major
  decline — the top itself is a long process, not a day.
- **The template:** on the *monthly* GMMA, a top is the red (short-term) averages converging
  with and falling below the blue (long-term) averages — RWB → BWR at monthly scale. Both the
  2000-01 and 2007-08 tops traced the same sequence.
- **The epistemics:** "let the action of the market reveal its true nature. Ignore the
  interpretations of the media pundits, who are typically most interested in **selling their
  advice**." Pundit top-calls come from scenarios and fundamentals; his top-detection comes
  from a pattern with two historical instances shown.
- The monthly GMMA joins the timeframe ladder the wiki already documents (daily and weekly
  Guppy charts, and the 2018 monthly-GMMA-on-bond-ETFs post) — completing daily → weekly →
  monthly, with the monthly reserved for regime-scale tops.
