# WW 2011-03-15 — Every long-term down-trend begins with a short-term down-trend

**Source:** [GMI at 1, as QQQQ short term down-trend completes 5th day; in cash and short; T2108 still neutral](../../raw/posts/2011-03-15-gmi-at-1-as-qqqq-short-term-down-trend-completes-5th-day-in-cash-and-short-t2108-still-neutral.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2011/03/gmi-at-1-as-qqqq-short-term-down-trend-completes-5th-day-in-cash-and-short-t2108-still-neutral/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

Day 5 of the down-trend, GMI 1, and the rule that follows: "**I never fight the trend of the general market**… I am confident that the best place to be in my short term trading account is on the sidelines or short" — cash plus a small QID position. The market had peaked on February 16, "weeks before the earthquake in Japan. **The events in Japan have merely enhanced the speed and the depth of the decline**" — news accelerating a turn the tape had already made. On the two horizons: "the longer term market trends remain up, for now. **But remember, every long term down-trend begins with a short term down-trend.**" And the lesson's cost: "it is so much easier to profit from owning stocks during an up-trend, why stay long during a down-trend? **It took me many years to learn this valuable lesson.**" T2108 at 44%; "a reading below 20% would encourage me to think the market is near a bottom." The prior up-trend had ended after 64 days.

## Key claims

- "Every long term down-trend begins with a short term down-trend" — why the daily count is watched inside an intact weekly up-trend.
- News accelerates a turn the tape has already made; it does not cause it.
- "I never fight the trend of the general market."
- T2108 below 20% as the bottom hint in 2011 (before the <10% actionable threshold hardened).
