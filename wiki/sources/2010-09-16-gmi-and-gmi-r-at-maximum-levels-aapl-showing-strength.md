# WW 2010-09-16 — Forty people, no hands — the behavioural gap demonstrated

**Source:** [GMI and GMI-R at maximum levels; AAPL showing strength](../../raw/posts/2010-09-16-gmi-and-gmi-r-at-maximum-levels-aapl-showing-strength.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2010/09/gmi-and-gmi-r-at-maximum-levels-aapl-showing-strength/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

A small experiment that makes the whole strategy's premise concrete. Speaking to an investment group of about
forty, "**at the beginning of my talk I polled the audience of about 40 people regarding whether they typically
would buy a stock that was trading at a new price high. Not a single person raised their hand. I then went on to
tell them that I concentrate virtually all of my buys on stocks that are at *all-time* highs!**" The gap he is
exploiting is not subtle or contested — in a room of engaged investors, the base rate for willingly buying a new
high was **zero**. And the argument he answers it with is arithmetic rather than rhetorical: "**If you want a
stock that is going to the moon, the best place to look is the daily new high list. A stock that goes from 20 to
100 hits a lot of new highs along the way!!**" A stock cannot multiply without passing through every new high on
the way, so a rule against buying new highs is a rule against owning multi-baggers.

## Key claims

- **Zero of ~40 engaged investors would willingly buy a stock at a new high** — the behavioural gap measured, not assumed.
- **"A stock that goes from 20 to 100 hits a lot of new highs along the way"** — refusing to buy new highs is refusing to own the winners.
- The pitch is made to a "mostly boomer audience" alongside his standing plea for financial-literacy education.
- The edge is not informational but dispositional: the data is public and the discomfort is what keeps others out.
