# WW 2016-10-30 — The full duration distribution, replicated three years on

**Source:** [New $QQQ short term down-trend–How long do down-trends last?](../../raw/posts/2016-10-30-new-qqq-short-term-down-trend-how-long-do-down-trends-last.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2016/10/new-qqq-short-term-down-trend-how-long-do-down-trends-last/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

**The duration study updated and replicated**, which is the most rigorous thing he does with his own data: "I
updated my analysis from 2013 of how long QQQ short term trends last. **The findings are remarkably similar.**"
The published distribution:

- "**41% of down-trends ended within 5 days, compared to 21% of up-trends.**"
- "**63% of up-trends and 56% of down-trends lasted from 6-47 days.**"
- "**Up-trends were 5x more likely than down-trends (16% vs. 3%) to reach 50+ days.**"

Three things follow. The **asymmetry is the finding** — "it is clear that short term up-trends last longer than
down-trends," which is the quantitative basis for treating the two sides differently (nibble early on the long
side, wait past day 5 on the short side). The **replication** matters more than either individual result: the
same analysis run on 2013 and 2016 data gave "remarkably similar" answers, which is the only stability check
anywhere in the corpus. And he attaches the caveat that limits it: "**keep in mind that any move might last
longer than those in the table if they have a few brief trend changes**" — his definition chops long moves into
pieces, so the table understates persistence.

## Key claims

- **The full distribution, both sides**: 41% of down-trends vs 21% of up-trends end within 5 days; 63%/56% run 6–47 days; up-trends are 5× likelier to reach 50+.
- **The asymmetry justifies asymmetric tactics** — up-trends persist, down-trends often abort, so the two sides warrant different patience.
- **Replicated across 2013 and 2016 with "remarkably similar" results** — the only stability check in the corpus.
- **The table understates persistence by construction**: brief counter-moves split one long trend into several, and he says so.
- Applied immediately: day 1 of a new down-trend, and "I will not wager much on it… until it reaches beyond 5 days."
