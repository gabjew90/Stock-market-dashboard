# WW 2009-01-29 — Why the pilot buy works — risk concentrated after confirmation

**Source:** [GMI: 1; GMI-R: 4  T2108: 61%](../../raw/posts/2009-01-29-gmi-1-gmi-r-4-t2108-61.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2009/01/gmi-1-gmi-r-4-t2108-61/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

**The scaling rule with its rationale, which is the part usually left out.** "I sold my QID and bought a little
QLD. I will accumulate QLD if the up-trend gets going. **The key is to start with a small pilot buy and to
increase my position only if the trend continues. I also set close stops in case the trend weakens. In this way I
have the greatest position (and risk) only after the movement is consistent with my initial purchases. I learned
this strategy from the great Jesse Livermore.**" The middle sentence is the argument: pyramiding is not about
getting a better average price — it is about **arranging for your largest exposure to exist only at the moment
the thesis has the most evidence behind it**. Early, when you are least certain, you are smallest; late, when the
trend has proved itself repeatedly, you are largest. That inverts the usual retail pattern of full size at entry
and averaging down afterwards, and it is why the same post pairs the pilot buy with close stops rather than
treating them as separate rules.

## Key claims

- **"I have the greatest position (and risk) only after the movement is consistent with my initial purchases"** — the actual argument for pyramiding, stated once and clearly.
- Position size tracks accumulated evidence, so exposure is smallest when uncertainty is highest.
- **The pilot buy and the close stop are one rule, not two** — small size early only helps if the small position is also protected.
- Attributed to Livermore via *Reminiscences*, consistent with the 2005 statement of the same idea.
