# WW 2013-03-17 — More green line break-out stocks; the pilot-buy entry; a Dow Theory buy signal

**Source:** [Some more green line break-out stocks: DFS, WAT, CAB, NEE…..; Dow Theory Buy Signal](../../raw/posts/2013-03-17-some-more-green-line-break-out-stocks-dfs-wat-cab-nee-dow-theory-buy-signal.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2013/03/some-more-green-line-break-out-stocks-dfs-wat-cab-nee-dow-theory-buy-signal/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

"One of my most important discoveries is the green line chart." The definition, the overhead-supply rationale ("a green line break-out stock has no overhead supply of stock from people who bought at higher levels and are holding losses"), the workflow (TC2000 GLB scan → draw the line on the monthly → check recency on the weekly), and the entry: "buy a little and wait to see if the green line holds. If it does, and the stock gathers strength I add to my position. A lot of stocks will fall back to the green line before they advance… I make a small pilot buy… I then get out of those that weaken and add to my stronger positions." GMCR's 2009 breakout, drawn in after the fact, ran ~900%: "stocks breaking out of a green line top after a large market decline can become huge winners." GLBs "work best in up-trending markets, with the GMI on a buy signal." DJ-20 and DIA both had GLBs — "a classic Dow Theory buy signal." Holdings named: TRIP, Z, IBM, AXP, CVX.

## Key claims

- GLB defined with the overhead-supply rationale.
- Entry = pilot buy, add on strength, cut the weak; "a lot of stocks will fall back to the green line before they advance."
- "Green line patterns are longer term in nature and meant to find stocks that will rise for months."
- Post-bear GLBs as the biggest winners (GMCR ~900%).
- GLB applied to indexes for a Dow Theory buy signal (DJ-20 + DIA).
