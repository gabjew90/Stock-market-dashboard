# WW 2012-05-07 — The 30-day close rule; new money vs old money

**Source:** [GMI sell signal; in cash; techs look very weak](../../raw/posts/2012-05-07-gmi-sell-signal-in-cash-techs-look-very-weak.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2012/05/gmi-sell-signal-in-cash-techs-look-very-weak/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

The 30-day-close sell rule stated at maximum strength, an overbought-reversal breadth scan, and
a distinction the wiki lacked entirely: **new pension contributions keep buying mutual funds on
the way down even while the transferred balance sits in cash** — because funds can't go
bankrupt and individual stocks can.

## Key claims

- **The 30-day close rule, endorsed flat-out:** "I typically sell a growth stock **as soon as it
  looks like it will *close* below its 30 day average. Check this signal out, it really works
  for me.** Why would one want to keep a stock that closes *below* its average closing price
  over the past six weeks?" On the day: 79% of Nasdaq 100 stocks were below their 30-day
  averages.
- **An overbought-reversal breadth scan:** a new PCF detecting "stocks that give a daily sell
  signal after being over-bought" — ~20% of Nasdaq100/Dow/S&P stocks fired it at once, read as
  a market-level warning rather than per-stock signals.
- **The AAPL tell again:** "AAPL's inability to rally after its blow-out earnings" plus a close
  below its 10-week — "I have learned only to be in AAPL when it is above its 10 week average."
  Same good-news-no-rise logic as January 2011.
- **New money vs old money — the missing distinction:** "in my university pension accounts,
  where money is being contributed each pay period, **I continue to have new money invested in
  mutual funds, even after I have transferred the full balance into money market funds**... I
  don't mind investing new money into mutual funds on the way down, assuming that the market
  will eventually rebound. **I never do so with individual stocks, because a company could go
  bankrupt, as was the case with GM and Enron and Lehman.**" Dollar-cost averaging is acceptable
  precisely where bankruptcy is impossible; never-average-down applies to single names.
- **A rare divergence recorded:** "We had a rare period when the GMI was above 4 **within a
  continuing short term down-trend in the QQQ**" — the two market-state instruments can
  disagree, and the day-count won this round.
- The anti-brainwashing line: "Most people feel they have to be in the market. This is the
  result of years of brainwashing by those who benefit from people holding positions."
