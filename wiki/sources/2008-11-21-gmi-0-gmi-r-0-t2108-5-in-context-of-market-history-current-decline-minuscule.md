# WW 2008-11-21 — The retest read via breadth; the Dow yearly chart

**Source:** [GMI: 0; GMI-R: 0; T2108: 5%; In context of market history, current decline minuscule](../../raw/posts/2008-11-21-gmi-0-gmi-r-0-t2108-5-in-context-of-market-history-current-decline-minuscule.md) · [original](https://wishingwealthblog.com/2008/11/gmi-0-gmi-r-0-t2108-5-in-context-of-market-history-current-decline-minuscule/) · tier: teaching

## What it covers

The November 2008 retest of the October low, read through breadth internals — a technique the
wiki had only from later posts — plus the maximum-zoom-out perspective (a *yearly* Dow chart
against 1929–32) and a historical note on T2108's earlier thresholds.

## Key claims

- **The retest read:** at the November price low, "there were only 23 new highs and **2,185 new
  lows**... On October 10, there were **2,832** new lows, so this decline has **not eclipsed**
  the number of new lows set at the October low." Fewer stocks making new lows on a retest =
  less internal damage — the same positive-divergence logic he later uses at the November 2021
  top in reverse.
- **T2108 corroboration and its record book:** 5% now, **1% on October 10**; "Since 1986, the
  level was lower only once, in October, 1987, when it hit **0.5%**."
- **T2108's earlier bands, an evolution datapoint:** "It **used to be** a reliable indicator of
  when the market reaches tops (**above 70%**) and bottoms (**below 20%**)." Different numbers
  from both the modern table band (80/25) and the <10% extreme — with "used to be" implying he
  already considered those calibrations dated by 2008.
- **The yearly Dow chart against 1929–32:** "the current decline in the Dow appears minuscule
  when compared to the one in 1929-1932... the current decline is well above [the 30-year trend
  line]. So, **we may not be near a bottom today or this year**." Zooming out to the maximum
  timeframe as a hysteria check — and a bearish one, not a comforting one.
- **The temptation named and refused:** "The great temptation that we all face is to buy the
  bargains and look for a bottom. If I have learned anything from studying the great traders of
  the past (Livermore, Baruch, Darvas, O'Neil) it is that **one cannot predict the market, only
  ride the current trend until it ends.**" Day 58; still in cash with a few shorts.
- Note **Baruch** in the lineage list — a name absent from the wiki's influences elsewhere.
